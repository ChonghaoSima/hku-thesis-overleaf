\section{Summary of Contributions}\label{sec:concl:summary}

This dissertation has investigated how physical agents can be made robust to real-world conditions absent from their training distributions, a challenge we termed the curse of reality, across the perceive--reason--act loop. The research was organised around three progressive stages and four core technical contributions, with every claim evaluated at an explicit level of the evaluation-fidelity ladder (L0 open-loop log evaluation, L1 non-reactive short-horizon rollout simulation, L2 reactive closed-loop simulation, and L3 physical robot deployment).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 1: Perceiving 3D Scenes as Volumetric Occupancy.}

The first stage confronted the representational bottleneck of 3D bounding boxes and flat bird's-eye-view grids, which presuppose rigid cuboids or planar surfaces and struggle to represent articulated vehicles, overhanging loads, or arbitrary 3D obstacles.

\textbf{OccNet, OpenOcc, and OpenScene (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}).} Stage~1 replaced cuboid and planar scene abstractions with camera-predicted 3D semantic occupancy. OccNet's cascade decoder reaches 26.98 mIoU and 41.08 $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$ on OpenOcc (and 60.46 mIoU on camera-based nuScenes LiDAR segmentation), while the OpenOcc and OpenScene releases provide dense nuScenes supervision and more than 120 hours of compact nuPlan logs, respectively. The representation better fits the labelled geometry of irregular instances than voxelised boxes (widening from 2.21 to 3.51 mIoU as voxel size tightens from 0.50\,m to 0.25\,m) and supports transfer to detection and segmentation. Its planning evidence is much weaker: the same-model open-loop comparison changes collision rates by only 0.06--0.24 percentage points in single runs, and two NAVSIM occupancy mechanisms (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}) do not improve PDMS. RQ1 is therefore answered at the level of geometric representation, not universal planning benefit or open-world semantics; the vocabulary remains closed at 16 classes and no distribution-shift experiment was run.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 2: Structured Reasoning with Vision-Language Models.}

Because 3D geometry specifies what occupies space but not why a traffic configuration matters or how the ego vehicle should respond, the second stage investigated how pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) can be grounded in driving scenes to bridge perception, prediction, and planning.

\textbf{DriveLM (Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}).} Stage~2 introduced Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA), two DriveLM datasets (4,871 nuScenes keyframes and $\sim$1.6 million CARLA QA pairs, hosting 152 teams in the CVPR 2024 Challenge), and the DriveLM-Agent baseline to make perception-to-planning dependencies explicit. In zero-shot nuScenes-to-Waymo transfer, full graph context reaches 2.63\,m ADE and raises speed accuracy from 43.90\% to 54.29\%. The result does not isolate graph reasoning: a waypoint-only BLIP-2 model already reaches 2.78\,m (and context-free DriveLM-Agent 2.76\,m) against 4.16\,m for UniAD-Single, and the comparison is confounded by a large parameter gap (3.955 billion versus 131.9 million) and asymmetric camera inputs. Graph context also worsens in-domain behaviour accuracy (61.45\% to 57.49\%), ADE (1.39\,m to 1.74\,m), and collision rate (1.67\% to 1.89\%). On withheld CARLA pedestrians, recovery requires a class-specific prompt (lifting accuracy from 4.59\% to 27.04\% vs.\ 59.63\% in regular scenes) and remains far from reliable. RQ2 therefore supports structured language as an inspectable interface with selective transfer benefits, while leaving visual grounding, real-time inference (0.16\,FPS), and causal reasoning unresolved.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 3: Reliable Action via Test-Time Adaptation and Train--Deploy Alignment.}

The third stage addressed RQ3 at the action level, investigating how learned policies can maintain reliability when deployment conditions diverge from offline training in both autonomous driving and physical robot manipulation.

\textbf{Centaur (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur})} addressed RQ3(a) by adapting trajectory-scoring end-to-end driving planners online via a single gradient step of test-time training (TTT) minimising Cluster Entropy, a label-free uncertainty measure over five lateral driving-direction clusters, alongside the 229-frame, ten-category \texttt{navsafe} diagnostic subset of \texttt{navtest}. Evaluated in non-reactive L1 NAVSIM v1 simulation on logged frames (where per-frame resets prevent compounding errors), Centaur raised single-seed Hydra-MDP from 90.3 to 92.6 PDMS on v1.0 and achieved $92.10 \pm 0.33$ PDMS across three seeds on the official NAVSIM v1.1 leaderboard (ranking first among three-seed entries in March 2025). All four TTT objectives on Hydra-MDP gain 1.2--2.3 PDMS (0.8--2.3 PDMS across base planners, or 0.4 with SVD gradient projection) whereas uncertainty-triggered fallback to prior trajectories degraded all planners to 59.9--67.4 PDMS (63.1--65.9 PDMS on Hydra-MDP). However, the three-seed margin over standard semantic entropy is small ($92.10 \pm 0.33$ vs.\ $91.87 \pm 0.19$, 0.23 PDMS) and lacks a matched three-seed unadapted baseline; standalone failure flagging accuracy (73.6\% at 62.8\% recall) trails a trivial never-flag baseline (84.4\%); and on \texttt{navsafe} (selected using base-planner scores rather than held out), Centaur improved frame-weighted mean PDMS from 49.4 to 68.9 but regressed on yielding (59.3 vs.\ 71.6) due to lateral-only clustering.

\textbf{$\chi_0$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0})} addressed RQ3(b) at L3 fidelity on physical bimanual robots by formalising long-horizon manipulation failures as distributional inconsistencies across human demonstrations $P_{\mathrm{train}}$, policy-induced trajectory distribution $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$, and hardware-executed trajectories $P_{\mathrm{test}}$. Combining Model Arithmetic (weight-space merging across subset checkpoints; author contribution), Stage Advantage (stage-progress advantage estimation; co-author component), and Train--Deploy Alignment (targeted DAgger recovery collection, spatiotemporal augmentation, heuristic chunk smoothing, and real-time chunking; author contribution) under a bounded post-training budget ($\sim$20 hours of base demonstrations per task, or 22.5 to 33.6 hours including targeted DAgger recovery rollouts, on eight A100 GPUs), \chiz lifted 30-trial real-robot T-shirt flattening and folding (Task~A) completion for $\pi_{0.5}$ from $\sim$27\% to $\sim$97\% (8/30 vs.\ 29/30 successes reconstructed from the plotted summaries, Fisher exact $p \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-8}$; Wilson 95\% confidence intervals 14--44\% vs.\ 83--99\%) and increased hourly throughput roughly elevenfold ($\approx 6$ to $\approx 66$ tasks/h) across long-horizon cyclic rollouts, with targeted DAgger recovery data providing the largest single gain.

\section{Unified Perspective: Towards Self-Correcting Physical Agents}\label{sec:disc:rq}

Looking across the perceive--reason--act loop, the individual chapters point toward a unified architectural perspective (RQ4). Each stage of this dissertation exposed a characteristic boundary of static offline learning: a geometry-open occupancy grid (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}) represents arbitrary 3D obstacle shapes but does not interpret traffic intent; a vision-language reasoning graph (Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}) transfers semantic priors across camera rigs and unseen categories when properly prompted, yet runs at 0.16\,FPS and can rely on language shortcuts~\cite{xie2025vlms}; per-frame test-time planner training (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}) refines trajectory scoring without labels at L1, but relies on frozen perception features and lateral-only uncertainty; and offline train--deploy alignment (Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0}) achieves high closed-loop reliability on real robots at L3, but still depends on human teleoperators to supply recovery trajectories.

Fig.~\ref{fig:disc:anatomy} synthesises these complementary strengths into a five-element architecture for \emph{self-correcting physical agents}, in which perception, reasoning, and action monitor and correct one another across deployment timescales. Because no integrated end-to-end self-correcting agent was constructed in a single experiment, Table~\ref{tab:disc:evidence} grades the empirical status and weakest link of each element across the dissertation:

\paragraph{1. An open, queryable scene representation.}
Downstream reasoning and control modules should query a 3D representation that does not presuppose which physical geometries matter (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}), trained end-to-end toward planning so upstream perception errors can be absorbed downstream as in the co-authored UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad}. Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet} establishes geometry-open 3D occupancy at L0 across multiple tasks, but its semantic vocabulary remains closed at 16 classes, its open-loop nuScenes planning gain over boxes is small (0.06--0.24 percentage points), and dynamic re-querying triggered by a runtime monitor has not yet been tested.

\paragraph{2. Fast and slow decision pathways.}
Because explicit VLM graph reasoning improved speed decisions under zero-shot sensor shift in Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm} but operates at 0.16\,FPS, a physical agent must decouple high-frequency reactive control from slow deliberative reasoning. As explored in the co-authored ETA~\cite{hamdan2025eta} (which runs a slow VLM asynchronously ahead of a fast planner in L2 CARLA simulation, lifting closed-loop driving score from 64.22 at \SI{931}{\milli\second} to 69.53 at \SI{50}{\milli\second}, as reported in~\cite{hamdan2025eta}) and FLARE~\cite{xie2026flare} (which aligns planner latents with future-aware VLM representations during training without test-time text generation), pairing fast visuomotor execution with selective or distilled VLM deliberation reconciles semantic generalisation with real-time control rates.

\paragraph{3. An online competence monitor.}
Self-correction requires an internal signal indicating when the fast policy is uncertain or stalls. Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur} introduced Cluster Entropy over directional driving modes as a label-free test-time uncertainty signal, while Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0} introduced supervised Stage Advantage (by co-author L.~Zhao) to estimate fine-grained sub-task progress during training. However, standalone online failure detection remains the weakest link in Table~\ref{tab:disc:evidence}: at a fixed threshold on \texttt{navtest}, Cluster Entropy flags 62.8\% of failures but achieves 73.6\% overall accuracy compared with 84.4\% for a trivial never-flag rule due to the 15.6\% base failure rate, and Stage Advantage requires stage annotations. Developing a well-calibrated, threshold-free competence monitor that arbitrates between fast execution, slow VLM reasoning, and test-time adaptation remains open.

\paragraph{4. Online adaptation at deployment.}
When the competence monitor detects out-of-distribution ambiguity, the agent must adjust its behaviour without waiting for offline retraining. Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur} demonstrated at L1 fidelity that single-step gradient updates on Cluster Entropy improve trajectory scoring across multiple planner backbones, outperforming both an uncertainty-triggered fallback to prior trajectories and UniAD's occupancy cost post-optimisation. Extending online parameter adaptation from per-frame resets to multi-step closed-loop episodes without weight drift or category-specific regressions (such as the yielding drop on \texttt{navsafe}) is the next step toward deployment-time self-correction.

\paragraph{5. A continual learning loop that outlives deployment.}
Finally, residual failures that cannot be resolved by online adaptation should flow back into targeted recovery data, weight-space model merging, execution alignment, and updated diagnostic benchmarks. Across research projects, this loop took the form of our benchmark-driven cycle (where OpenOcc led to OpenScene and NAVSIM, and DriveLM led to DriveBench~\cite{xie2025vlms} and FLARE~\cite{xie2026flare}); within a single robot deployment, Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0} closed the loop through human-collected DAgger recovery trajectories, Model Arithmetic, and Train--Deploy Alignment. Replacing human teleoperators in that loop with autonomous failure recovery and skill practice is the central thesis of our co-authored self-directed learning perspective~\cite{chen2025robot}.

\section{Limitations and Future Directions}\label{sec:concl:future}

The conclusions of this dissertation are bounded by several methodological and empirical limitations that directly motivate future research:

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Four limits bound the thesis-wide conclusions. First, \emph{driving evidence stops at logged-frame evaluation or non-reactive rollout}: unlike the L3 physical robot experiments in Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0}, the L0 (Chapters~\ref{ch:occnet} and~\ref{ch:drivelm}) and L1 (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}) driving protocols cannot reveal compounding control errors, reactive traffic responses, or multi-step parameter drift. Second, \emph{the shift studies contain important confounds}: Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet} tests unusual geometry within known classes without distribution shift, Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}'s Waymo comparison mixes model scale with camera asymmetry, its CARLA pedestrian result uses an oracle-guided class-specific query, and \texttt{navsafe} in Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur} is selected with the base planner's scores rather than held out. Third, \emph{statistical and resource precision is uneven}: most driving results are single-run, several multi-seed margins (such as 0.23 PDMS between Cluster Entropy and semantic entropy over three seeds) are small, and the 30-trial robot results are reconstructed from plotted summaries without a matched-budget data-scaling study or complete operator and compute accounting. Finally, \emph{the proposed safeguards remain incomplete}: OpenOcc is closed-vocabulary (16 classes), language models can exploit textual priors rather than causal visual evidence~\cite{xie2025vlms,liu2026perturbot}, and unsupervised test-time weight updates have neither formal safety bounds nor uniform gains across scenario categories such as yielding.

\medskip
\noindent Overcoming these limitations points to four primary directions for future work:

\paragraph{Open, predictive 4D world models.}
Future scene representations should extend 3D occupancy from a closed 16-class taxonomy to open-vocabulary 3D semantics via vision-language distillation~\cite{vobecky2023pop3d,jiang2025gausstr} and self-supervised temporal rendering~\cite{huang2024selfocc}, while scaling 4D occupancy forecasting on OpenScene~\cite{sima2023openscene,zheng2024occworld}. Crucially, predictive occupancy world models should be evaluated by whether they improve closed-loop planning under geometric and environmental shift in reactive simulation (L2) rather than open-loop L0 displacement metrics alone.

\paragraph{Grounded, adaptive fast--slow reasoning.}
To eliminate linguistic shortcuts in VLM and VLA policies~\cite{xie2025vlms,liu2026perturbot}, future reasoning benchmarks and training pipelines should enforce class-balanced action distributions, blind and ego-status-only input ablations, and causal perturbation checks verifying that corrupting intermediate perception or prediction answers alters downstream actions. Architecturally, slow VLM reasoning should be invoked selectively by a calibrated competence monitor or distilled into fast latent policy representations~\cite{hamdan2025eta,xie2026flare} to satisfy onboard latency budgets.

\paragraph{Lifelong closed-loop test-time adaptation with safety bounds.}
Test-time planner training (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}) should be extended from episodic single-frame updates in non-reactive NAVSIM v1 to continuous multi-step adaptation in reactive closed-loop simulators and 3D Gaussian-splatting pseudo-simulation (NAVSIM v2~\cite{cao2025pseudo}). Incorporating longitudinal speed modes alongside lateral steering clusters in Cluster Entropy, pairing online weight updates with scene-aware safety filters, and establishing formal bounds on multi-step parameter drift will be essential to prevent category-specific regressions such as yielding failures.

\paragraph{Self-directed robot learning and matched-budget scaling laws.}
In embodied manipulation, future work should systematically compare distributional alignment (\chiz) against raw demonstration scaling~\cite{bu2025agibot_iros} at equal total robot, operator, and compute budgets. More fundamentally, moving beyond human teleoperation and human-triggered DAgger interventions requires robots that autonomously detect execution failures, propose sub-goals, practice recovery skills in the physical world, and evaluate their own progress via learned value functions~\cite{chen2025robot}.

\medskip\label{sec:concl:closing}
\noindent No finite training dataset will ever record every obstacle geometry, traffic interaction, sensor configuration, or deformable object state that the physical world presents. Rather than treating out-of-distribution failures as edge cases to be solved by offline dataset scale alone, this dissertation has argued for designing and evaluating physical agents around the inevitability of deployment shift: representing 3D geometry without rigid cuboids, structuring multimodal reasoning so intermediate steps can be inspected and grounded, adapting planner predictions to test-time uncertainty, and aligning demonstration data, policy weights, and hardware execution across the training--deployment loop. Having taken concrete steps across open-loop logs, non-reactive rollouts, and physical robots, closing the full perceive--reason--act self-correction loop in lifelong real-world deployment remains the central frontier ahead.
