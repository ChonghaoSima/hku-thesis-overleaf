\section{Introduction}\label{kai0:sec:intro}

Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur} showed in non-reactive driving simulation that an end-to-end planner can adjust frame-level predictions at test time by lowering predictive uncertainty without ground-truth supervision~\cite{sima2024centaur}. Extending the Act pillar to physical robotics, RQ3(b) asks how a learned controller can stay dependable once test-time conditions diverge from offline training in \emph{real-world robot manipulation}. Deformable cloth manipulation entails contact-rich dynamics across a high-dimensional configuration space from arbitrary crumpled initial states, whereas human teleoperation samples only a narrow fraction of feasible trajectories. Practical resource limits add further constraints: recording demonstrations consumes substantial operator and robot time, post-training a large vision-language-action (VLA) model requires considerable GPU compute, and closed-loop control delays plus mechanical limits cause physically executed motions to drift from offline predictions.

Resource limits make data and model scale only part of the manipulation problem. We analyse reliability through three linked objects across the learning loop: the recorded human demonstrations $P_{\mathrm{train}}$, the policy-induced trajectory distribution $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$, and the trajectories produced on hardware $P_{\mathrm{test}}$. In the terminology of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} (Section~\ref{sec:method:framework}), these correspond to training data $p_{\mathrm{train}}$, policy-induced behaviour, and the physically executed component of deployment $p_{\mathrm{deploy}}$. They are conceptual distributions rather than fitted density models (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:distributions}), so the experiments test interventions associated with each mismatch rather than measured reductions in distributional divergence. The \chiz\footnote{Project page: \url{https://mmlab.hk/research/kai0}. Code: \url{https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/KAI0}.} framework links this analysis to three modules:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{Model Arithmetic} (MA) fits separate checkpoints on disjoint subsets of $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ and interpolates their weights using convex coefficients tuned on out-of-distribution (OOD) DAgger recovery rollouts, reconciling $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ with $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ without enlarging the primary training corpus (though gathering the validation rollouts still requires DAgger interventions).
  \item \emph{Stage Advantage} (SA), designed by co-author Lirui Zhao, regresses relative task progress directly from observation pairs conditioned on discrete semantic stages and converts the prediction into binary weights for advantage-weighted imitation, guiding $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ toward productive actions with lower temporal variance than subtracting independent state-value estimates.
  \item \emph{Train--Deploy Alignment} (TDA) shifts $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ toward $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ via heuristic DAgger recovery collection and spatiotemporal augmentation while mitigating inference-to-actuation latency at run time through temporal chunk-wise smoothing.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate \chiz on two bimanual platforms across garment flattening, folding, conditional sorting, and rack hanging. The full system is compared with the base policy on Task~A (T-shirt flattening and folding), while Tasks~B and~C provide module-level tests. In 30 Task~A rollouts (with values read from the plotted evaluation summaries; Section~\ref{kai0:sec:protocol}), completion rises from approximately 8/30 (27\%; 95\% Wilson interval 14--44\%) for the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline to 29/30 (97\%; 83--99\%) for \chiz (Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA}). Post-training uses a pre-trained $\pi_{0.5}$ backbone, about 20 hours of base demonstrations per task, and eight A100 GPUs; total compute, robot time, and operator time across all sub-experiments were not separately recorded. Because targeted DAgger recovery data supply the largest individual gain (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_tda}) and no matched-budget data-scaling baseline was run, the experiment supports alignment as a useful complement to scale, not as evidence that alignment is the dominant bottleneck (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:limitations}).

The rest of this chapter covers prior literature (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:related}), problem formulation (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:problem}), the three alignment modules (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:method}), hardware and benchmark protocols (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:setup}), system-level and component-level experiments (Sections~\ref{kai0:sec:exp} and~\ref{kai0:sec:ablation}), limitations (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:limitations}), implementation details and supplementary ablations (Section~\ref{app:kai0}), and conclusions (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:summary}).

Learned driving systems now operate on public roads in both rider-only automated services and supervised driver-assistance deployments~\cite{Kusano2025waymocrash,tesla2025fsd,waymo2025blog}, whereas physical manipulation in open-ended environments remains fragile because controllers must act over high-dimensional joint spaces under wide variations in cloth geometry and lighting. Recent robotic foundation efforts have scaled VLA backbones on large compute clusters~\cite{black2024pi0,sunday2025act1,black2025pi05,zheng2025xvla,amin2026pistar06,beingbeyond2026beingh05,generalist2025gen0} and assembled large teleoperation datasets such as AgiBot World~\cite{bu2025agibot_iros} (co-authored by the author, comprising over one million trajectories across more than 100 robots). While network capacity and dataset scale are necessary, they do not by themselves ensure reliable closed-loop execution: a key barrier is \emph{the distributional mismatch across data collection ($P_{\mathrm{train}}$), policy-induced trajectory distribution ($Q_{\mathrm{model}}$; Eq.~\eqref{kai0:eq:qmodel}), and physical deployment ($P_{\mathrm{test}}$)}, which affects task completion, motion continuity, hourly throughput, and corrective retry counts~\cite{chen2025benchmarking,shi2025diversity,bu2025agibot_iros,jiang2025behavior,amin2026pistar06}.

In physical rollouts, observed failures fall into three systematic mismatches across these distributions, as shown in Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:teaser}. First, because deformable cloth spans a high-dimensional state space, $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ covers only a sparse set of valid trajectories, biasing $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ toward narrow demonstration habits. Second, computation and communication delays between policy inference ($Q_{\mathrm{model}}$) and physical actuation ($P_{\mathrm{test}}$) introduce temporal offsets that disrupt action sequences at chunk boundaries~\cite{black2025rtc,tang2025vlash}. Third, because expert demonstrations rarely visit error states, minor disturbances during execution in $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ trigger compounding deviations from which the policy cannot recover~\cite{hu2025rac,pan2026sop,amin2026pistar06}. Standard remedies such as broader data collection~\cite{oneill2024openx,khazatsky2024droid,bu2025agibot_iros}, synthetic augmentation~\cite{cubuk2019autoaugment,cubuk2020randaugment,laskin2020reinforcement}, or deployment-time adaptation~\cite{sun2020ttt,wang2022cotta,hansen2021pad} are difficult to apply directly when teleoperation is expensive, control loops are latency-critical, and VLA post-training is compute-intensive.

To counter these three discrepancies under practical robotics budgets, \chiz pairs each distributional mismatch with a targeted module (bottom panel of Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:teaser}): (a)~\textbf{Model Arithmetic (MA)} interpolates parameters across checkpoints trained on disjoint splits of $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ to combine behavioural modes that a single training run can miss; (b)~\textbf{Stage Advantage (SA)}, contributed by co-author Lirui Zhao, partitions a multi-step task into semantic stages and supplies stage-conditioned advantage weights for behaviour cloning~\cite{peng2019awr}, yielding a less noisy signal than a $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$-style value-differencing baseline~\cite{amin2026pistar06} (see Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_sa}); and (c)~\textbf{Train--Deploy Alignment (TDA)} expands $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ toward $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ via heuristic DAgger recovery trajectories and spatiotemporal augmentation while applying temporal chunk-wise smoothing at run time to counter inference delay, outperforming RTC-only control~\cite{black2025rtc} in throughput under two of three augmentation regimes on Task~A (see Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_tda}). Across 30 evaluation rollouts per setting taken from the plotted summaries (where differences of up to four successes fall within binomial sampling variation; Section~\ref{kai0:sec:protocol}), our primary findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Integrated system.} On Task~A, combining all three modules lifts $\pi_{0.5}$ completion from roughly 27\% to roughly 97\%; without an equal-volume unaugmented baseline, this gain does not separate distributional alignment from total data volume.
  \item \textbf{Train--Deploy Alignment.} DAgger recovery data provide the largest standalone improvement, raising $\pi_{0.5}$ completion from roughly 23\% to 87--93\% on Task~A and from roughly 10\% to 43--53\% on Task~C. Heuristic DAgger raises retry counts across all tested settings, whereas standard DAgger leaves retries nearly unchanged except on $\pi_{0}$ in Task~A. Spatiotemporal augmentation alone shows mixed effects on Task~A; temporal chunk-wise smoothing beats RTC in completion rate under two of three augmentation conditions, and combining both yields the strongest unaugmented controller.
  \item \textbf{Model Arithmetic.} On Task~C, every weight-merged policy exceeds both the single-best subset checkpoint and the full-data checkpoint in mean completion rate and throughput, though only the OOD-validated greedy merge clears sampling noise over the full-data baseline. Across Tasks~A and~B, merging surpasses unmerged baselines on most metrics. On Task~A, MA alone leaves $\pi_{0.5}$ completion nearly unchanged (roughly 30\% vs.\ 27\%) and lowers the rubric score (35 vs.\ 41), whereas pairing MA with TDA or SA adds roughly 30 percentage points in completion rate.
  \item \textbf{Stage Advantage.} Across all three tasks, Stage Advantage produces smoother temporal advantage traces than value differencing (without ground-truth verification). In closed-loop rollouts, completion rises on Task~A (roughly 80\% vs.\ 67\%, within sampling noise); on Task~B, Stage Advantage reduces retries and raises the rubric score but trails in completion rate (roughly 17\% vs.\ 23\%) and throughput; on Task~C, completion rate, throughput, and score all decrease.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}\label{kai0:sec:related}

\subsection{Imitation Learning and Real-World Policy Deployment}\label{kai0:sec:related_il}
Imitation learning for robot manipulation spans compact transformer policies~\cite{zhao2023aloha,chi2023diffusion,chi2024umi,zeng2024learning} and pre-trained VLA backbones~\cite{brohan2023rt1,kim2024openvla,zitkovich2023rt2,bu2025univla,team2024octo,black2025pi05,amin2026pistar06}, with the $\pi$ series~\cite{black2024pi0,black2025pi05,amin2026pistar06} demonstrating strong multi-robot transfer. Because collecting large demonstration corpora~\cite{khazatsky2024droid,bu2025agibot_iros,oneill2024openx,walke2023bridgedata,jiang2025galaxea} requires major hardware and operator investment, prior work explores interactive DAgger collection~\cite{ross2011dagger,hu2025rac,kelly2019hgdagger,amin2026pistar06}, visual and kinematic augmentation~\cite{laskin2020reinforcement,yu2023scaling,kostrikov2020image,li2025gr}, and run-time trajectory blending or inpainting to counter inference delay~\cite{zhao2023aloha,black2025rtc,tang2025vlash}. Whereas existing studies typically address data collection, policy training, or run-time control separately~\cite{chen2025retaining,tajwar2024preference}, \chiz jointly targets all three pairwise gaps among $(P_{\mathrm{train}}, Q_{\mathrm{model}}, P_{\mathrm{test}})$. In complementary co-authored work, PerturBot~\cite{liu2026perturbot} diagnoses how fine-tuned VLA policies rely on visual, language, and motor shortcut priors via an offline \texttt{GroundFscore} diagnostic. PerturBot then breaks these shortcuts through perturbative training, complementing \chiz's system-level distributional alignment across $(P_{\mathrm{train}}, Q_{\mathrm{model}}, P_{\mathrm{test}})$.

\subsection{Model Merging and Weight Interpolation}\label{kai0:sec:related_merge}
Interpolating parameters across checkpoints trained with varied hyper-parameters or fine-tuning splits improves out-of-distribution generalisation across vision, language, motion planning, and embodied control~\cite{wortsman2022soups,yadav2023ties,ilharco2023task,Yang2024ModelMI,Akiba2024EvolutionaryOO,Lee2025InteractionMergedMP,wang2024fleet}. While RETAIN~\cite{Yadav2025RobustFO} merges VLA checkpoints to preserve general pre-trained capabilities and standard recipes tune mixing weights on in-domain splits, our Model Arithmetic module targets narrow demonstration coverage by selecting convex combinations (across uniform averaging, inverse-loss weighting, gradient optimisation, and greedy soup construction) using an OOD validation set of DAgger recovery rollouts.

\subsection{Advantage Estimation for Long-Horizon Tasks}\label{kai0:sec:related_adv}
Conditioning imitation learning on returns, values, or advantages helps guide multi-step decision-making~\cite{schmidhuber2019reinforcement,zheng2022online,emmons2022rvs,kuba2023advantage,wu2023elastic}, with advantage-weighted regression (AWR) upweighting high-advantage transitions~\cite{peng2019awr} and $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$ computing VLA advantages from distributional value differences $V(s') - V(s)$~\cite{amin2026pistar06}. Because subtracting two independent frame-level value predictions compounds estimation noise, Stage Advantage (developed by co-author Lirui Zhao) regresses relative progress directly from paired frames conditioned on semantic task stages~\cite{sima2026kai0}, producing a smoother signal (see Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_sa}) that is thresholded into a binary optimality indicator~\cite{amin2026pistar06}.

\section{Distributional Inconsistencies in Robot Learning}\label{kai0:sec:problem}

\subsection{Preliminaries}\label{kai0:sec:prelim}
We model manipulation over horizon $H$ with state space $\mathcal{S}$, action space $\mathcal{A}$, initial state distribution $\mu(s_0)$, and transition kernel $s_{t+1} \sim T(\cdot \mid s_t, a_t, \xi)$ under environment parameters $\xi$. Under policy $\pi(a \mid s; \phi)$, a trajectory $\tau = (s_0, a_0, s_1, a_1, \dots, s_H)$ has density~\cite{schulman2015trust,levine2018reinforcement}:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\pi}(\tau) = \mu(s_0) \prod_{t=0}^{H-1} \pi(a_t \mid s_t; \phi)\, T(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, \xi),
  \label{kai0:eq:traj_distrib}
\end{equation}
written as $P$ whenever $\pi$ is clear from context.

\subsection{Three Distributions of the Robot Learning Cycle}\label{kai0:sec:distributions}
Denoting successful task trajectories by $\mathcal{G}$ and a base kinematic trajectory measure by $P_{0}(\tau)$, the target trajectory distribution is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{real}}(\tau) \propto P_{0}(\tau)\,\mathbb{I}(\tau \in \mathcal{G}).
  \label{kai0:eq:preal}
\end{equation}
Relative to $P_{\mathrm{real}}$, we distinguish three distributions in the learning loop:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Training distribution ($P_{\mathrm{train}}$)}: the empirical trajectory distribution of human teleoperation demonstrations, yielding dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{\tau^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}$.
  \item \textbf{Model distribution ($Q_{\mathrm{model}}$)}: the policy-induced trajectory distribution $Q_{\mathrm{model}}(\tau) = P_{\pi(\cdot\mid\cdot;\hat{\phi})}(\tau)$ induced via Eq.~\eqref{kai0:eq:traj_distrib} by rolling out the learned policy $\pi(a \mid s; \hat{\phi})$ fitted by maximum likelihood on $\mathcal{D}$~\cite{ross2011dagger,osa2018algorithmic}:
  \begin{equation}
    \hat{\phi} = \argmax_{\phi} \sum_{\tau \in \mathcal{D}} \sum_{t} \log \pi(a_t \mid s_t; \phi),
    \label{kai0:eq:qmodel}
  \end{equation}
  so that $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ lives on the same trajectory space as $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{test}}$, with extrapolation outside $\mathcal{D}$ reflecting architectural priors and pre-trained weights (for flow-matching policies such as $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2024pi0,black2025pi05}, maximum-likelihood training in Eq.~\eqref{kai0:eq:qmodel} is instantiated by minimising the conditional flow-matching velocity-field regression loss over action chunks).
  \item \textbf{Test distribution ($P_{\mathrm{test}}$)}: the closed-loop rollout distribution when $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ acts through a hardware execution operator $I(\tilde{a}_t \mid a_t, s_t)$ mapping predicted actions $a_t$ to applied commands $\tilde{a}_t$ under latency and actuator bounds:
  \begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{test}}(\tau) = \mu(s_0) \prod_{t=0}^{H-1} \pi(a_t \mid s_t; \hat{\phi})\, \tilde{T}(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, \xi),
    \label{kai0:eq:ptest}
  \end{equation}
  \begin{equation}
    \tilde{T}(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t, \xi) \triangleq \mathbb{E}_{\tilde{a}_t \sim I(\cdot \mid a_t, s_t)} \big[ T(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, \tilde{a}_t, \xi) \big].
    \label{kai0:eq:ttilde}
  \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Because we do not fit explicit density estimators or compute divergences among $P_{\mathrm{train}}$, $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$, and $P_{\mathrm{test}}$, these definitions serve as a conceptual lens for each module's objective rather than measured divergence reductions, and the t-SNE projections (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:consistency}) are qualitative.

\subsection{Three Systematic Inconsistencies}\label{kai0:sec:inconsistencies}
Observed rollout failures correspond to three recurring mismatches among these distributions: (i)~\textbf{coverage deficiency}, where sparse sampling of $P_{\mathrm{real}}$ by $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ causes $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ to overfit narrow demonstration modes; (ii)~\textbf{temporal mismatch}, where visually similar cloth states at different task stages confuse $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ while inference latency offsets applied commands in $P_{\mathrm{test}}$; and (iii)~\textbf{failure cascade}, where the lack of corrective manoeuvres in $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ leaves the policy unable to recover once disturbances push trajectories off course in $P_{\mathrm{test}}$.

\section{Method}\label{kai0:sec:method}

\subsection{Overview of the Framework}\label{kai0:sec:pipeline}
To counter the three mismatches of Section~\ref{kai0:sec:inconsistencies} within practical data and compute budgets, \chiz coordinates three modules across the learning pipeline (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:pipeline}). Model Arithmetic (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:ma}) expands policy coverage in weight space by interpolating checkpoints trained on disjoint data partitions. Stage Advantage (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:sa}) resolves temporal ambiguity during imitation learning via stage-conditioned advantage estimation, providing lower-variance supervision across multi-stage tasks. Train--Deploy Alignment (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:tda}) connects physical execution back to training by combining run-time chunk smoothing with targeted recovery data and spatiotemporal augmentation.

\subsection{Model Arithmetic}\label{kai0:sec:ma}
Under modest teleoperation budgets, $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ samples the state space sparsely, biasing a single fine-tuned checkpoint toward narrow manipulation routines. How can we reduce this coverage bias in $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ without collecting additional raw demonstrations? Unlike mixture-of-experts architectures that require learned routers~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously,fedus2022switch} or output ensembles that multiply forward-pass latency~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, Model Arithmetic (MA) combines multiple checkpoints into a single weight vector at zero run-time overhead. Specifically, we partition $\mathcal{D} \sim P_{\mathrm{train}}$ randomly into $n$ disjoint subsets $\{\mathcal{D}_1, \mathcal{D}_2, \dots, \mathcal{D}_n\}$, fine-tune an independent checkpoint $\theta_i$ on each subset, and form the deployed parameters via convex combination:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_{\mathrm{merged}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i \theta_i, \qquad \text{s.t.}\quad \alpha_i \ge 0, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i = 1,
  \label{kai0:eq:arith}
\end{equation}
where the coefficients $\{\alpha_i\}$ are selected to reduce validation loss on a held-out set, consolidating complementary behavioural modes across partitions.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{ch-kai0/images/arithmetic_v4.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Weight-merging schemes in Model Arithmetic.} Checkpoints trained on disjoint subsets are merged via convex weight interpolation using inverse-loss weighting (\textbf{top}) or uniform averaging, gradient optimisation, and greedy selection (\textbf{bottom}).}
  \label{kai0:fig:arithmetic}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Out-of-distribution validation.} Rather than tuning $\{\alpha_i\}$ only on in-domain demonstrations, we construct an OOD validation split from DAgger recovery rollouts~\cite{ross2011dagger,kelly2019hgdagger} gathered by running the subset policies on the physical robot with human corrective interventions. Although MA does not enlarge the primary training splits, collecting this OOD validation set still entails physical rollouts and operator time (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{Merging strategies.} Using this validation set, we evaluate four weight-souping rules~\cite{wortsman2022soups} (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:arithmetic}; Sections~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_ma} and~\ref{kai0:app:ma}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Average weighting}~\cite{wortsman2022soups} assigns uniform coefficients $\alpha_i = 1/n$.
  \item \emph{Inverse-loss weighting}~\cite{maiti2025souper} weights each checkpoint $i$ inversely to its validation loss $L_i$ with scalar hyper-parameters $\delta$ and $p$:
  \begin{equation}
    \alpha_i \propto \frac{1}{(L_i + \delta)^{p}}.
    \label{kai0:eq:invloss}
  \end{equation}
  \item \emph{Gradient descent}~\cite{sima2026kai0} (plus an adaptive-step variant) parameterises the simplex weights via a softmax map and directly minimises the validation loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}$ of $\theta_{\mathrm{merged}}$:
  \begin{equation}
    \bm{\alpha} = \operatorname{softmax}(\mathbf{w}), \qquad \min_{\mathbf{w}}\ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{val}}\Big(\sum_{i} \alpha_i \theta_i\Big).
    \label{kai0:eq:gd}
  \end{equation}
  \item \emph{Greedy search}~\cite{wortsman2022soups} incrementally adds the candidate checkpoint that achieves the largest validation-loss drop when uniformly averaged with the active set.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage Advantage}\label{kai0:sec:sa}
Developed by co-author Lirui Zhao, Stage Advantage (SA) addresses temporal ambiguity in $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ when visually similar cloth states recur at different phases of a multi-stage sequence, providing a low-variance, stage-aware progress signal for policy training~\cite{sima2026kai0}.

\paragraph{Value differencing vs.\ direct stage-conditioned advantage.} Prior VLA methods such as $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$~\cite{amin2026pistar06} pair AWR~\cite{wu2023elastic,kuba2023advantage} with state-value differences,
\begin{equation}
  A(s, a) = V(s') - V(s),
  \label{kai0:eq:adv_vdiff}
\end{equation}
which compounds per-frame errors $e, e'$ with variance $\operatorname{Var}(e) + \operatorname{Var}(e') - 2\operatorname{Cov}(e, e')$ ($\operatorname{Cov}(e, e') \approx 0$) and becomes ill-posed when separate stages look visually similar. SA instead regresses relative timestamp progress directly from frame pairs $(s, s')$ ($s' = s_{t+\Delta}$ across varied step offsets $\Delta$) via a VLM estimator $f_{\theta}$ (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:pipeline}):
\begin{equation}
  A(s, a) = f_{\theta}(s, s'),
  \label{kai0:eq:adv_direct}
\end{equation}
inducing positive error correlation across the pair to lower temporal variance (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_sa}). To disambiguate similar cloth states across $S$ manual semantic stages, $f_{\theta}(s, s' \mid g)$ acts as a stage-conditioned multi-step progress predictor conditioned on the normalised stage index $g$ (written $A_{\mathrm{stage}}$ following the paper's terminology):
\begin{equation}
  A_{\mathrm{stage}}(s, a, g) = f_{\theta}(s, s' \mid g), \qquad g \in \Big\{0, \frac{1}{S}, \dots, \frac{S-1}{S}\Big\}.
  \label{kai0:eq:adv_stage}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{kai0:fig:advantage} plots the resulting cumulative progress curves across Tasks~A--C (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:tasks}). Following the binary optimality construction in $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$~\cite{amin2026pistar06}, $A_{\mathrm{stage}}$ is converted into a binary optimality indicator for advantage-weighted behaviour cloning:
\begin{equation}
  I = \mathbf{1}[A_{\mathrm{stage}} > \epsilon],
  \label{kai0:eq:indicator}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon$ is set at the 70th percentile so the top fraction $q = 0.3$ of transitions receives $I = 1$, upweighting productive demonstration segments in $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ to reduce stalling in $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ (Sections~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_sa} and~\ref{kai0:app:sa}).

\subsection{Train--Deploy Alignment}\label{kai0:sec:tda}
During physical deployment, forward-pass and communication delays between $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ create velocity and position jumps at action-chunk boundaries. Whereas prior controllers smooth or inpaint chunks at run time~\cite{zhao2023aloha,black2025rtc,tang2025vlash}, \chiz applies \emph{temporal chunk-wise smoothing} to maintain continuous actuation across chunk updates (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:consistency}, bottom left; Algorithm~\ref{kai0:alg:chunk_smoothing}). Given the unconsumed suffix $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}$ of the previous action chunk starting at index $k$ (so $a^{\mathrm{old}}_j$ is the $j$-th remaining action in the buffer) and a new chunk $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}$, we discard the stale prefix of length $d = \min(k, d_{\max})$ from $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}$ (when the early-return branch $d \ge |\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}|$ fires, $k$ continues incrementing along the unconsumed buffer), pad $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}$ to a minimum overlap $m_{\min}$, and linearly cross-fade the remaining suffix $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mathrm{rem}}$ with $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}$ over $L$ overlapping steps via decaying weights $w_i$. Unlike ACT temporal ensembling~\cite{zhao2023aloha} (which queries the model at every control step to average all historical chunks) or RTC~\cite{black2025rtc} (which inpaints chunks during sampling conditioned on committed actions), chunk-wise smoothing post-processes consecutive buffers without extra model evaluations and combines orthogonally with RTC (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_tda}).

  \label{kai0:fig:consistency}
\end{figure}

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
  \caption{Temporal chunk-wise smoothing}\label{kai0:alg:chunk_smoothing}\label{kai0:alg:smoothing}
  \KwIn{unconsumed suffix $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}$ of previous action chunk starting at index $k$ ($a^{\mathrm{old}}_j$ is the $j$-th remaining action), consumption index $k$, new chunk $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}$, drop threshold $d_{\max}$, minimum overlap $m_{\min}$}
  \KwOut{updated buffer $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{buf}}$ and updated index $k$}
  $d \leftarrow \min(k, d_{\max})$\tcp*{stale prefix length}
  \If{$d \ge |\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}|$}{
    \Return{$\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}, k$}\tcp*{skip outdated chunk; $k$ continues incrementing along $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}$}
  }
  $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mathrm{rem}} \leftarrow (a^{\mathrm{new}}_{d}, \dots)$\tcp*{unconsumed new actions}
  \If{$|\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}| < m_{\min}$}{
    pad $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}$ by repeating its final command\;
  }
  $L \leftarrow \min(|\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{old}}|, |\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mathrm{rem}}|)$\tcp*{blending window}
  \For{$i \leftarrow 0$ \KwTo $L-1$}{
    $w_i \leftarrow 1 - i/\max(L-1, 1)$\;
    $\tilde{a}_i \leftarrow w_i\, a^{\mathrm{old}}_i + (1 - w_i)\, a^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mathrm{rem},i}$\;
  }
  $\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{buf}} \leftarrow (\tilde{a}_0, \dots, \tilde{a}_{L-1}) \,\Vert\, (\text{suffix of } \mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{new}}_{\mathrm{rem}})$\;
  $k \leftarrow 0$\tcp*{reset buffer index}
  \Return{$\mathbf{a}^{\mathrm{buf}}, k$}\;
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Closing the loop from deployment to training.} To fold error states from $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ back into $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ with minimal collection overhead, TDA uses two data-side mechanisms (Section~\ref{kai0:app:tda}): (1)~whereas interactive DAgger collects expert labels on states visited by the learned policy~\cite{ross2011dagger,kelly2019hgdagger}, \emph{heuristic DAgger} places the garment directly into hand-crafted fault states (such as slipped grasps or half-dropped cloth) and records human recovery trajectories without running the learned policy (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:consistency}, top left), concentrating targeted data expansion at failure states; and (2)~\emph{spatiotemporal augmentation} horizontally mirrors camera frames and joint actions with left-right arm swap~\cite{li2025gr} while subsampling frames to synthesise varied execution speeds.

\subsection{Robot Platform}\label{kai0:sec:platform}
Our dual-station bimanual setup (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:robot_setup}; Section~\ref{kai0:app:hardware}) comprises one station with AgileX Piper arms and one with ARX X5 arms. Each station mounts two 6-DoF manipulators with 1-DoF parallel-jaw grippers and three $640 \times 480$ Intel RealSense D435i RGB cameras (one overhead static camera plus two wrist cameras), with policy inference running on a local NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU workstation.

We evaluate \chiz across three garment manipulation benchmarks ordered by difficulty (Table~\ref{kai0:tab:tasks}):

\subsection{Metrics}\label{kai0:sec:metrics}
Each policy configuration runs for 30 rollouts (10 rollouts across each of three test garments) and is scored on four summary metrics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Success rate (SR, \% $\uparrow$)}: percentage of rollouts that satisfy the full task completion criteria.
  \item \textbf{Throughput (TP $\uparrow$)}: projected count of completed tasks per hour of operation.
  \item \textbf{Retry cost ($\downarrow$)}: mean number of corrective re-attempts per rollout during closed-loop execution.
  \item \textbf{Average score ($\uparrow$)}: partial-credit score normalised to a 100-point scale under a milestone rubric (sub-goal weights in Table~\ref{kai0:tab:score}, Section~\ref{kai0:app:exp}).
\end{itemize}
Stage Advantage ablations additionally report the smooth frame ratio (\textbf{SFR} $\uparrow$) and mean squared temporal difference (\textbf{MSTD} $\downarrow$), which quantify frame-to-frame trajectory smoothness rather than ground-truth progress accuracy (since a constant predictor has zero temporal variation).

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Uncertainty}\label{kai0:sec:protocol}
Closed-loop rollouts run on the two physical stations of Section~\ref{kai0:sec:platform} in the demonstration workspace with garments tossed into arbitrary starting poses under randomised lighting (Section~\ref{kai0:app:data}). Because archived logs store only aggregate rollout summaries rather than the per-episode initial-state matching, operator blinding, scheduling, and garment-overlap metadata recommended by Kress-Gazit~\etal~\cite{kressgazit2024robot}, our experiments measure closed-loop reliability within the training workspace rather than zero-shot transfer to unseen rooms, hardware, or garments.

Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA} and Figs.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_triple}--\ref{kai0:fig:exp_aug_control} report metrics read from the plotted evaluation summaries (accurate to $\approx$1 axis unit), paired with reconstructed integer success counts (in 3.3-point steps for $n = 30$, where 27\% is 8/30), two-sided Fisher exact $p$-values, and 95\% Wilson intervals. Plotted whiskers labelled ``accumulative standard error'' are omitted from comparisons because that quantity is undocumented and sometimes smaller than binomial variance permits (e.g., the +SA bar at 47\%, or 14/30, shows a $\pm 1$-point whisker whereas the standard error across the three 10-trial garment strata is at least $\approx$3 points and the pooled binomial standard error is near 9 points). At $n = 30$, a 95\% Wilson interval around 50\% spans $\approx$34 points, so differences of four or fewer successes never clear sampling noise ($p > 0.1$); nominal 5\% differences in multi-factor grids (Figs.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_souping} and~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_aug_control}) also face multiple-testing inflation, and throughput, retries, and average score are point means.

\subsection{Data Collection and Training}\label{kai0:sec:data}
Roughly 20 hours of human teleoperated demonstrations are collected per benchmark across varied garment colours, fabrics, poses, and lighting; combining the episode counts and mean durations in Table~\ref{kai0:tab:tasks} yields roughly 22.5, 33.6, and 32.4 hours of trajectories (base demonstrations plus DAgger recovery clips) for Tasks~A--C. We fine-tune all parameters of $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2025pi05} on 8$\times$A100 GPUs under flow matching~\cite{black2024pi0} (Table~\ref{kai0:tab:hyperparams}, Section~\ref{kai0:app:exp}; resource caveats in Section~\ref{kai0:sec:limitations}).

\section{Experiments}\label{kai0:sec:exp}

Physical rollouts on the dual-station platform of Section~\ref{kai0:sec:setup} address four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{System efficacy}: how the three modules interact in the eight-way factorial evaluation on Task~A (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_overall}).
  \item \textbf{Model Arithmetic}: whether weight merging of subset checkpoints outperforms both the strongest subset checkpoint and pooled training, and whether OOD DAgger validation beats in-domain validation (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_ma}).
  \item \textbf{Stage Advantage}: whether direct stage-conditioned progress estimation yields smoother supervision traces and stronger rollouts than a $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$-style RECAP value-differencing baseline~\cite{amin2026pistar06} (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_sa}).
  \item \textbf{Train--Deploy Alignment}: how heuristic DAgger compares with standard on-policy DAgger, and how run-time chunk smoothing interacts with spatiotemporal augmentation (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_tda}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baselines and Ablation Design}\label{kai0:sec:baselines}

\paragraph{Base policy.} We adopt $\pi_{0.5}$ as our primary backbone and $\pi_{0}$ in secondary comparisons, as only these two open-weight policies achieved non-trivial completion on our cloth tasks; GO-1 from AgiBot World~\cite{bu2025agibot_iros} (co-authored by the author), X-VLA~\cite{zheng2025xvla}, DexVLA~\cite{wen2025dexvla}, UniVLA~\cite{bu2025univla}, and OpenVLA~\cite{kim2024openvla} failed to complete the manipulation sequences reliably (Section~\ref{kai0:app:questions}).

\paragraph{Module ablation baselines.} For \textbf{MA}, we benchmark the five coefficient-selection rules of Section~\ref{kai0:sec:ma} (with in-domain or OOD DAgger validation) against the \emph{single-best} subset policy and the pooled \emph{full-data} policy. For \textbf{SA}, we compare stage-agnostic direct regression (Direct, Eq.~\eqref{kai0:eq:adv_direct}) and stage-conditioned regression (Direct+Stage, Eq.~\eqref{kai0:eq:adv_stage}) against a $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$-style RECAP value-differencing baseline~\cite{amin2026pistar06} sharing the PaliGemma backbone of $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2025pi05} that regresses point state values on offline frames over a 50-step lookahead (rather than full RECAP's distributional value head over demonstrations, on-policy rollouts, and human interventions; Section~\ref{sec:bg:manip}). For \textbf{TDA}, we compare temporal chunk-wise smoothing against synchronous and asynchronous chunk execution~\cite{tang2025vlash}, ACT-style temporal ensembling~\cite{zhao2023aloha}, and RTC~\cite{black2025rtc} across spatial mirroring and temporal frame skipping, and compare standard on-policy DAgger~\cite{ross2011dagger} with heuristic DAgger on $\pi_{0}$~\cite{black2024pi0} and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2025pi05}.

\subsection{System Efficacy Breakdown}\label{kai0:sec:exp_overall}
Figure~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_triple} and Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA} report the eight-way factorial ablation on Task~A using the best module settings from Section~\ref{kai0:sec:ablation}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{TDA} boosts completion rate by roughly 17 to 53 percentage points across every pairing; its effect on retry count depends on the partner module, rising when paired with +MA (from $\approx$1.8 to 2.6) or +SA (from $\approx$1.1 to 2.3) yet dropping when added to bare $\pi_{0.5}$ (from $\approx$3.0 to 1.7) or +MA+SA, consistent with recovery data encouraging active re-grasping after slips.
  \item \textbf{SA} raises both completion rate and hourly throughput across all pairings, with configurations containing SA achieving the fastest task completions overall.
  \item \textbf{MA} in isolation leaves completion rate nearly unchanged relative to $\pi_{0.5}$ (roughly 30\% vs.\ 27\%, or 9 vs.\ 8 of 30 trials) and lowers the rubric score (35 vs.\ 41), but adds roughly 30 percentage points in completion rate when paired with +TDA (16 to 25 of 30; uncorrected Fisher $p \approx 0.025$) or +SA (14 to 24 of 30; $p \approx 0.015$).
  \item \textbf{Full \chiz} achieves the highest completion rate (roughly 97\%, 29/30) and rubric score ($\approx$97) while matching +SA+TDA in throughput (66 vs.\ 68 completions/h). At $n = 30$ trials, 29/30 cannot be statistically separated from the 24 to 26 successes of two-module pairings (Fisher $p \ge 0.10$), nor does any single module clear the sampling threshold over bare $\pi_{0.5}$ ($p \ge 0.06$); statistically decisive gains require combining at least two modules.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ablation Studies}\label{kai0:sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Cross-figure consistency.} The module studies (Figs.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_souping}--\ref{kai0:fig:exp_aug_control} and~\ref{kai0:fig:app_soup}--\ref{kai0:fig:app_tda_c}, including the in-chunk smoothing baselines in Figs.~\ref{kai0:fig:app_tda_a}--\ref{kai0:fig:app_tda_c}) were conducted in separate sessions with different checkpoints and dataset snapshots, so nominal baselines are not interchangeable across figures. On Task~A, for example, $\pi_{0.5}$ completion ranges from about 23\% (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_dagger_a}) to 27\% (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_triple}) and 37\% (synchronous in Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_aug_control}). Likewise, heuristic DAgger reaches 87\% completion with 8.6 retries in Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_dagger_a}, whereas the factorial `+TDA' condition reaches 53\% with 1.7 retries in Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_triple}; unaugmented chunk-wise smoothing incurs $\approx$12 retries and 77\% completion in Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_aug_control} (70\% in Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:app_tda_a_abs}) versus 1.1 retries for the full system in Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA}; and the Stage Advantage (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_advantage}) and Model Arithmetic (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:app_soup_a}) figures use still stronger references (67--83\%). Task~C baselines similarly range from about 10\% (Figs.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_dagger_c} and~\ref{kai0:fig:app_sa_c}) to 20\% (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_souping}) and 37\% (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:app_tda_c_abs}). These shifts preclude cross-figure effect estimates. All ablation claims below are therefore restricted to comparisons made within the same figure and evaluation session.

\subsection{Model Arithmetic}\label{kai0:sec:exp_ma}
On Task~C (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_souping}), every weight-merged policy exceeds both the single-best subset checkpoint ($\approx$7/30) and the full-data policy ($\approx$6/30, 20\%) in mean completion rate and throughput, though some merge rules raise retries and only OOD-validated greedy search ($\approx$15/30, 50\%) clears the nominal 5\% threshold over full-data (uncorrected Fisher $p \approx 0.03$; $p \approx 0.06$ vs.\ single-best). On the partial-credit rubric, only greedy search and OOD inverse-loss weighting beat the single-best candidate (score $\approx$70); why linear interpolation matches or beats pooled training remains an open question, potentially linked to VLA weight redundancy~\cite{yu2024language}.

Replacing in-domain validation with OOD DAgger validation lifts completion for three of four loss-guided rules (inverse-loss from $\approx$30\% to 47\%, gradient descent from 27\% to 33\%, greedy search from 40\% to 50\%, or 12 vs.\ 15 of 30, Fisher $p \approx 0.60$, with adaptive gradient descent near 40\%) and reduces retries under three rules despite minor throughput dips under adaptive gradient descent and greedy search. Across both validation splits on Task~C, greedy search posts the highest (or tied-highest) completion rate, top score, and lowest retries. Parallel ablations on Tasks~A and~B appear in Section~\ref{kai0:app:ablation_ma}, and Section~\ref{kai0:app:ma} notes the in-domain inverse-loss rule used in Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA}.

\subsection{Stage Advantage}\label{kai0:sec:exp_sa}
Figure~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_advantage} compares Stage Advantage (Direct+Stage, co-author module) against the $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$-style value-differencing baseline and stage-agnostic Direct regression on Tasks~A and~B. \subsection{Train--Deploy Alignment}\label{kai0:sec:exp_tda}

\paragraph{Heuristic vs.\ standard DAgger.} Across $\pi_{0.5}$ and $\pi_0$ on Tasks~A and~C (Figs.~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_dagger_a} and~\ref{kai0:fig:exp_dagger_c}), DAgger recovery data yield the largest standalone gain in this chapter. \section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{kai0:sec:limitations}

By combining Model Arithmetic, Stage Advantage, and Train--Deploy Alignment, \chiz lifts Task~A completion of $\pi_{0.5}$ from roughly 27\% to 97\%, subject to three main caveats:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Alignment vs.\ data volume and resource accounting.} Lacking an iso-budget scaling baseline trained on an equal number of operator and robot hours of standard demonstrations, the Task~A study does not separate distributional alignment from targeted data volume, especially since DAgger recovery clips drive the largest single gain (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:exp_tda}) and only Task~A received the full eight-way factorial evaluation. Moreover, while $\pi_{0.5}$ post-training uses roughly 20 demonstration hours per task and 8 A100 GPUs over 80{,}000 steps (Tables~\ref{kai0:tab:tasks} and~\ref{kai0:tab:hyperparams}), cumulative GPU, robot, and operator hours across the four MA checkpoints, the SA estimator, and DAgger rollouts were not itemised separately.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation scope, statistics, and generalisation.} All 30-trial rollouts took place in the training workspace without logged blinding, scheduling, or unseen-garment splits (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:protocol}), measuring in-workspace closed-loop reliability rather than zero-shot transfer to unseen rooms, embodiments, or cloth categories; cross-session baseline shifts and 30-trial binomial variance (Section~\ref{kai0:sec:ablation}) further restrict statistical resolution to large effects, and retention of general pre-trained VLA skills, rigid-body transfer, and cross-task merging were not tested.
  \item \textbf{Data valuation, advantage supervision, and failure modes.} Demonstration cleanliness informally shifted completion rates between roughly 20\% and 60\% under fixed training settings (Section~\ref{kai0:app:questions}), motivating offline data-utility proxies. Furthermore, Stage Advantage assumes monotonic timestamp progress without human advantage labels, and neither MA nor SA was separately benchmarked against the two main rollout failure modes of imprecise 3D grasping and mid-episode stagnation (Sections~\ref{kai0:app:questions} and~\ref{kai0:app:failure}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Module Implementation, Hardware, and Evaluation Rubric}\label{kai0:app:name}\label{kai0:app:related_rl}\label{kai0:app:related_control}\label{kai0:app:data}\label{kai0:app:hardware}\label{kai0:app:loss}

\paragraph{Nomenclature, baseline screening, and data quality.}
\textbf{K}inetics-\textbf{A}ligned-\textbf{I}ntelligence (KAI\textsubscript{0}, written \chiz after the Greek letter $\chi$ in parallel with the $\pi$ family) aligns demonstration collection ($P_{\mathrm{train}}$) and physical deployment ($P_{\mathrm{test}}$) with the policy-induced trajectory distribution ($Q_{\mathrm{model}}$). In pilot screening on our 20-hour corpus, X-VLA~\cite{zheng2025xvla}, DexVLA~\cite{wen2025dexvla}, OpenVLA~\cite{kim2024openvla}, UniVLA~\cite{bu2025univla}, and GO-1 (from AgiBot World~\cite{bu2025agibot_iros}, co-authored by the thesis author) all scored near zero over 30 rollouts despite matched camera views and converged losses, whereas $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$ showed stronger post-training plasticity. Because demonstration quality shifted completion between 20\% and 60\% under identical settings, we use open-loop \emph{replay-ability} from a comparable initial state to screen corrupted logs and hardware drift between $P_{\mathrm{train}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{test}}$. At run time, whereas SmolVLA~\cite{shukor2025smolvla} switches directly between asynchronous chunks and A2C2~\cite{sendai2025leave} adds auxiliary heads, \chiz blends overlapping chunks linearly (Algorithm~\ref{kai0:alg:chunk_smoothing}) without architectural changes.

\paragraph{Module specifications and DAgger protocol.}
Model Arithmetic partitions demonstrations into four disjoint splits; Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA} uses in-domain inverse-loss weighting, whereas ablations compare all five rules against the lowest-validation-loss \emph{single-best} checkpoint and the pooled \emph{full-data} model. For Stage Advantage (by co-author Lirui Zhao; cf.\ VLA/robot RL post-training~\cite{chen2025tgrpo,li2025simplevla,lu2025vla,luo2024serl,xiao2025PLD,luo2025hilserl,lei2025rl,kumar2019reward,frans2025diffusion,ghasemipour2025self,zhai2025vlac,zhang2025rewind,ma2024gvl,sutton1998reinforcement,schulman2017PPO,wagenmaker2025dsrl} and benchmark suites used to evaluate long-horizon transfer~\cite{mees2022calvin,mu2025robotwin,liu2023libero}), given frame pairs $(s_t, s_{t+\Delta})$ from an episode of length $T_e$, stage-agnostic direct regression predicts the normalised step offset:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{A}_{\mathrm{direct}}(s_t, s_{t+\Delta}) = f_{\theta}(s_t, s_{t+\Delta}) \approx \frac{\Delta}{T_e},
  \label{kai0:eq:app_direct_adv}
\end{equation}
stage-conditioned regression conditions on $g \in \{0, 1/S, \dots, (S-1)/S\}$ across $S \in \{2, 4, 3\}$ stages on Tasks~A--C:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{A}_{\mathrm{stage}}(s_t, s_{t+\Delta}, g) = f_{\theta}(s_t, s_{t+\Delta} \mid g),
  \label{kai0:eq:app_stage_adv}
\end{equation}
and 70th-percentile thresholding $\epsilon_{1-q}$ ($q = 0.3$) yields the binary indicator:
\begin{equation}
  I_t = \mathbf{1}\big[\hat{A}_{\mathrm{stage}}(s_t, s_{t+\Delta}, g) > \epsilon_{1-q}\big].
  \label{kai0:eq:app_indicator}
\end{equation}
Stage Advantage converges to a lower terminal loss than the $\pi^{*}_{0.6}$-style baseline (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:loss_curve_sa}). Standard DAgger (1)~launches an autonomous rollout, (2)~halts after $>10$ consecutive missed grasps or stagnant loop steps while freezing observations and holding joints for human takeover, and (3)~logs both segments; heuristic DAgger directly places the cloth and arms into typical halt states to record expert recoveries. On the dual-station rig (Fig.~\ref{kai0:fig:robot_setup}; AgileX Piper with pitch joint~5, ARX X5 with yaw joint~5), cameras stream at 30\,Hz under a standard operating protocol and joint controllers run at 100--200\,Hz on an Ubuntu 20.04 workstation. Tables~\ref{kai0:tab:hyperparams} and~\ref{kai0:tab:score} list the $\pi_{0.5}$ fine-tuning hyper-parameters and the 100-point milestone rubric.

\section{Chapter Summary}\label{kai0:sec:summary}

Addressing RQ3(b), this chapter investigated how bimanual manipulation policies can maintain closed-loop reliability under practical data and compute limits when deployment conditions diverge from offline training. Organising the learning loop around the demonstration distribution $P_{\mathrm{train}}$, the policy-induced trajectory distribution $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$, and the physical rollout distribution $P_{\mathrm{test}}$, \chiz pairs each pairwise mismatch with a dedicated module: Model Arithmetic interpolates subset-trained checkpoints using merge weights tuned on OOD DAgger validation rollouts; Stage Advantage (contributed by a co-author) supplies stage-conditioned progress labels for advantage-weighted behaviour cloning; and Train--Deploy Alignment combines heuristic DAgger recovery collection, spatiotemporal augmentation, and run-time temporal chunk-wise smoothing.

In the eight-way factorial study on Task~A using the dual-station bimanual platform, \chiz lifted the 30-trial completion rate of $\pi_{0.5}$ from roughly 27\% to 97\% (8 vs.\ 29 successes, an about 260\% relative gain and $+70$ percentage points alongside a roughly elevenfold increase in hourly throughput; Table~\ref{kai0:tab:taskA}), driven primarily by targeted DAgger recovery data together with complementary multi-module interactions across MA, SA, and run-time smoothing+RTC control. Taken together with the autonomous driving study in Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}, where Centaur adapts online at test time via uncertainty-guided updates in non-reactive log replay, \chiz shows how routing physical rollout feedback back into data collection, checkpoint selection, and run-time control closes the loop between deployment behaviour and policy learning. Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} synthesises these findings into broader design principles for self-correcting embodied systems.
