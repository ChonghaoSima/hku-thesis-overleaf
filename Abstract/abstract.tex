\begin{abstract}

Learning-based autonomous vehicles and robotic manipulators have advanced rapidly by imitating offline datasets and adapting multimodal foundation models. Deployed in the physical world, however, data-driven policies confront a persistent mismatch between finite training distributions and open-ended operating conditions across obstacle geometry, scene semantics, multi-agent behaviour, and hardware execution, termed here the \emph{curse of reality}. This dissertation pursues dependable physical agents under such distributional shifts across a three-stage research arc spanning perception, reasoning, and action.

In the first research stage, we address the representational limits of rigid 3D bounding boxes and planar bird's-eye-view maps, which miss arbitrary obstacle shapes and vertical clearance. We introduce OccNet, reconstructing dense 3D semantic occupancy from surround-view cameras through a cascade of voxel decoders, alongside OpenOcc for dense voxel and motion-flow supervision on nuScenes. OccNet reaches 26.98 mIoU on semantic scene completion, captures irregular vehicle geometries more accurately than voxelised detections, transfers to 3D detection and BEV segmentation, and lowers open-loop planning collision rates by 0.06--0.24 percentage points within a 16-class vocabulary. Building on this, OpenScene scales volumetric supervision to over 120 hours of nuPlan logs for occupancy, end-to-end driving, and world-modelling benchmarks.

In the second stage, we observe that 3D geometry specifies what occupies space, yet safe planning also requires reasoning about road-user intent and interactions. We propose Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA), organising perception, prediction, planning, behaviour, and motion questions into a directed acyclic graph, together with the DriveLM datasets and DriveLM-Agent baseline. Under zero-shot transfer from nuScenes to an unseen Waymo camera layout, DriveLM-Agent achieves 2.63\,m average displacement error and lifts speed accuracy from 43.90\% to 54.29\%, combining pre-trained vision-language priors with graph context while revealing trade-offs on in-domain open-loop planning and prompt-guided recovery on withheld CARLA pedestrians.

In the third stage, we study how policies remain reliable when deployment departs from training across driving and manipulation. For end-to-end driving, we present Centaur, adapting planner decoder weights online via single-step test-time training on Cluster Entropy without test-time rules, raising Hydra-MDP from 90.3 to 92.6 PDMS on NAVSIM v1.0 and reaching $92.10 \pm 0.33$ PDMS across three v1.1 seeds alongside a safety-critical split (\texttt{navsafe}). Extending to closed-loop physical manipulation, we develop $\chi_0$, a co-led framework aligning demonstrations, policy learning, and hardware execution via Model Arithmetic, Stage Advantage, and Train--Deploy Alignment, lifting 30-trial real-robot T-shirt folding completion of $\pi_{0.5}$ from about 27\% (8/30) to 97\% (29/30) with roughly 20 hours of base demonstrations per task (22.5--33.6 hours with targeted recovery rollouts).

Collectively, these contributions establish an evidence-graded foundation for self-correcting physical agents, integrating queryable 3D representations, fast and slow decision pathways, competence monitoring, test-time adaptation, and continual learning loops toward dependable embodied intelligence.
