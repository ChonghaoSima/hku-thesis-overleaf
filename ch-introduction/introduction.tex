\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:intro}\label{ch:background}\label{ch:methodology}

A vehicle drives through an unfamiliar city and encounters a flatbed truck carrying an overhanging crane boom. A dual-arm robot trained to fold garments from human teleoperation is handed a T-shirt crumpled in an unseen configuration. Neither situation is exotic, yet each lies outside the agent's training distribution. This dissertation studies such conditions and how physical agents can be built and evaluated to cope with them across the perceive--reason--act loop.

\section{Motivation}\label{sec:intro:context}\label{sec:intro:problem}\label{sec:intro:rq}\label{sec:method:framework}\label{sec:method:evaluation}\label{sec:method:practice}\label{sec:method:validity}\label{sec:bg:manip}

A \emph{physical agent} perceives its surroundings through onboard sensors, reasons about scene semantics and multi-agent interactions, and acts through physical actuators in a closed control loop. This dissertation focuses on two representative embodiments: autonomous vehicles and robotic manipulators. Over the past decade, both fields have shifted from hand-engineered pipelines toward learning from data. Perception modules are trained on annotated multi-sensor logs, motion planners and visuomotor policies imitate human demonstrations, and large foundation models pretrained on web-scale vision and language corpora are adapted to driving and manipulation.

Between 2021 and 2026, when the research in this dissertation was conducted, three developments shaped the architecture of data-driven physical agents. Multi-camera BEV models such as BEVFormer replaced isolated per-camera detections with shared spatiotemporal features for detection, mapping, and tracking~\cite{li2022bevformer,li2023bevdevils}. UniAD then trained perception, forecasting, and planning jointly, reducing open-loop planning error by optimising upstream features for the driving objective~\cite{hu2023uniad}. Most recently, multimodal foundation models brought web-scale visual and linguistic priors to driving and paired those priors with large manipulation datasets and generalist robot policies~\cite{sima2024drivelm,bu2025agibot_iros,black2025pi05}.

Learning from finite datasets, however, confronts a fundamental obstacle when deployed in the physical world. Formally, let $s$ denote the underlying world state, $o$ the sensor observation, and $a$ the agent's action. Offline imitation learning draws training pairs from
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{train}}(o,a) \;=\; \sum_{s} p_{\mathrm{train}}(s)\; p_{\mathrm{train}}(o \mid s)\; \pi^{*}(a \mid o),
  \label{eq:method:train}
\end{equation}
where $\pi^{*}$ denotes the human demonstrator or expert controller; for continuous states, the sum in Eq.~\eqref{eq:method:train} becomes an integral. The equation is a one-step observation--action model and should not be confused with the closed-loop visitation distribution. At deployment, a learned policy $\pi$ encounters a different state distribution $p_{\mathrm{deploy}}(s)$ and sensor model $p_{\mathrm{deploy}}(o \mid s)$, induces its own visitation distribution $d_{\pi}$ rather than $d_{\pi^{*}}$, and acts through imperfect hardware dynamics $a_{\mathrm{exec}} \sim p(a_{\mathrm{exec}} \mid a)$. These differences allow stepwise errors to compound~\cite{ross2010efficient,ross2011dagger}. They also interact with the \emph{curse of rarity}: individually uncommon hazards occur frequently in aggregate~\cite{liu2024curse}. This dissertation uses \emph{curse of reality} for this combined mismatch across $p(s)$, $p(o \mid s)$, $d_{\pi}$, and $p(a_{\mathrm{exec}} \mid a)$: whatever finite dataset an agent is trained on, physical deployment keeps presenting conditions absent from training.

Used here as an organising analytical lens, the curse of reality can be decomposed into four overlapping distribution shifts alongside two structural failure sources:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Perceptual shift and representational limits.} Changes in the sensing distribution $p(o \mid s)$ arise from day-to-night illumination changes~\cite{li2023bevdevils}, camera--LiDAR feature-fusion limitations~\cite{gao2023sdf}, or cross-dataset camera-rig changes (such as transferring from nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} to Waymo~\cite{sun2020waymo}). Distinct from sensing shift is \emph{representational limitation}: fixed 3D bounding boxes presuppose rigid cuboids and cannot faithfully represent articulated trailers, construction vehicles with protruding booms, or arbitrary roadside debris even for known categories~\cite{sima2023occnet}.
  \item \textbf{Semantic shift.} Changes in the world state distribution $p(s)$ expose the agent to unseen object categories or unusual multi-agent interactions; separately, 3D lane and topology benchmarks represent variation in road structure~\cite{chen2022persformer,wang2023openlanev2}. While pre-trained VLMs offer broad semantic priors, naive language conditioning can rely on ungrounded linguistic shortcuts without attending to visual evidence~\cite{xie2025vlms}.
  \item \textbf{Behavioural shift and rare scenarios.} In sequential control, closed-loop execution causes $d_{\pi}$ to drift away from expert demonstrations $d_{\pi^{*}}$, while rare tail scenarios expose miscalibrated trajectory scoring in end-to-end planners~\cite{sima2024centaur}.
  \item \textbf{Deployment shift and demonstrator inconsistency.} In physical robot manipulation, discrepancies arise across recorded human demonstrations $P_{\mathrm{train}}$, the learned policy distribution $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$, and hardware-executed trajectories $P_{\mathrm{test}}$ due to state-space coverage gaps, action-chunk boundary discontinuities, and multimodal or suboptimal human demonstrators $\pi^{*}$~\cite{sima2026kai0}.
\end{enumerate}

The curse of reality is especially acute for physical agents for four reasons: control mistakes carry physical safety consequences; collecting and annotating dense 3D driving logs or long-horizon robot trajectories is expensive; closed-loop execution compounds small prediction errors; and onboard latency, memory, and compute budgets preclude achieving reliability solely by scaling model parameters at inference time.

To address these challenges systematically, this dissertation asks: \emph{how can physical agents be made robust to conditions that the physical world presents but their training data do not contain?} Following the perceive--reason--act loop illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:intro:overview}, we investigate four research questions across a progressive three-stage research programme:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2cm, labelwidth=1cm, itemsep=3pt]
  \item[RQ1] \textbf{Perceive (Stage~1).} How should a physical agent represent its 3D surroundings so that the representation remains geometrically valid for arbitrary obstacle shapes while remaining learnable from multi-view cameras and queryable by downstream driving tasks (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet})?
  \item[RQ2] \textbf{Reason (Stage~2).} How can the world knowledge of pretrained vision-language models be grounded in driving scenes through structured, verifiable perception-to-planning reasoning, and when does such reasoning improve generalisation under sensor and semantic shifts (Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm})?
  \item[RQ3] \textbf{Act (Stage~3).} How can a learned policy remain reliable when deployment departs from training: (a) for end-to-end autonomous driving, can a planner adapt its per-frame decisions at test time without ground-truth labels or hand-crafted fallback rules (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}); and (b) for real-world robot manipulation, how can distributional inconsistencies across demonstrations, policy learning, and hardware execution be tamed under bounded data and compute budgets (Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0})?
  \item[RQ4] \textbf{Architecture (Synthesis).} What design principles and architectural requirements for self-correcting physical agents follow from Stages~1--3, and what is the strength of the empirical evidence supporting each element (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion})?
\end{description}

Methodologically, the works in this dissertation follow a design-science paradigm~\cite{simon1996sciences,hevner2004design} built around a benchmark-driven research cycle: observing a deployment failure mode, formalising a task representation and metric, curating a public benchmark, establishing a scalable baseline, organising open community challenges (including the CVPR 2023 3D Occupancy Challenge and the 152-team CVPR 2024 ``Driving with Language'' Challenge), and analysing residual failures to motivate subsequent iterations. To prevent claims from being overstated across simulation regimes, every experimental result in this dissertation is reported at its explicit evaluation fidelity along a four-level ladder: \textbf{L0} open-loop evaluation on logged sensor frames (Chapters~\ref{ch:occnet} and~\ref{ch:drivelm}, where ego-status shortcuts must be accounted for on nuScenes~\cite{zhai2023rethinking,li2024ego}); \textbf{L1} non-reactive short-horizon rollout simulation from logged states in NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim} (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}, which evaluates 4-second trajectories with kinematic simulation but resets to logged states at each frame so closed-loop errors cannot compound); \textbf{L2} reactive closed-loop simulation in CARLA~\cite{dosovitskiy2017carla} (used by the rule-based PDM-Lite expert to generate DriveLM-CARLA in Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}); and \textbf{L3} closed-loop deployment on physical robots (Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0}). Furthermore, following statistical best practices for empirical AI and robotics~\cite{agarwal2021precipice,brown2001interval,kressgazit2024robot}, single-seed training runs, benchmark construction biases (such as non-held-out stress-split selection in \texttt{navsafe} and answer-distribution skew in DriveLM-Data~\cite{xie2025vlms}), co-author-led experiments, and Wilson 95\% confidence intervals for finite-trial robot evaluations are stated explicitly throughout the dissertation.

\section{Contributions}\label{sec:intro:contributions}

The main contributions of this dissertation are organised along the three stages of the perceive--reason--act loop, followed by related co-authored contributions.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 1: Perceiving Open-World 3D Geometry.}

\noindent\textbf{OccNet, OpenOcc, and OpenScene (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}).}
Stage~1 contributes OccNet, OpenOcc, and OpenScene. OccNet extends a BEVFormer encoder with a coarse-to-fine 3D voxel decoder (contemporary with VoxFormer~\cite{li2023voxformer}); OpenOcc supplies 34,149 nuScenes keyframes with more than 1.4 billion labelled voxels across 16 classes and flow for the eight movable foreground classes; and OpenScene scales related supervision to more than 120 hours of nuPlan logs~\cite{sima2023occnet,sima2023openscene}. On OpenOcc, OccNet reaches 26.98 mIoU and 41.08 $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$, compared with 23.67 and 37.47 for our retrained TPVFormer~\cite{huang2023tpvformer}, and reaches 60.46 mIoU when queried at nuScenes LiDAR points. Occupancy also fits irregular vehicle geometry more closely than voxelised boxes (by 2.21 to 3.51 mIoU as voxel size tightens from 0.50\,m to 0.25\,m) and supports low-data detection pre-training. These findings are bounded: the vocabulary is closed at 16 classes, distribution shift was not tested, the 0.06--0.24 percentage-point planning advantage comes from single runs, and occupancy-based mechanisms did not improve the two NAVSIM planners examined in Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 2: Structured Reasoning with Vision-Language Models.}

\noindent\textbf{DriveLM (Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}).}
Stage~2 introduces Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA), DriveLM-nuScenes, DriveLM-CARLA, and the DriveLM-Agent baseline~\cite{sima2024drivelm}. GVQA connects perception ($P_1$), prediction ($P_2$), planning ($P_3$), behaviour ($B$), and motion ($M$) questions in a directed acyclic graph. The two datasets contain 4,871 nuScenes keyframes with 91.4 QA pairs per frame and about 1.6 million rule-generated CARLA QA pairs collected with PDM-Lite~\cite{beisswenger2024pdmlite}, respectively, and supported the 152-team CVPR 2024 ``Driving with Language'' Challenge. Under zero-shot transfer to 1,000 Waymo frames, full graph context yields 2.63\,m ADE (versus 4.16\,m for UniAD-Single) and raises speed accuracy from 43.90\% to 54.29\%. Most of the trajectory advantage over UniAD-Single is already present without graph context, however, and the comparison is confounded by a roughly thirtyfold parameter difference and asymmetric camera inputs. In-domain graph context worsens behaviour accuracy, ADE, and collision rate; on withheld CARLA pedestrians, recovery requires a class-specific prompt.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 3: Self-Correction and Alignment in Action.}

\noindent\textbf{Centaur (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}).}
To adapt end-to-end driving planners at deployment without ground-truth labels or hand-crafted fallback rules, we propose Centaur~\cite{sima2024centaur}, which updates planner decoder parameters via a single gradient step of test-time training (TTT) minimising Cluster Entropy, a label-free uncertainty measure computed over five lateral driving-direction clusters inspired by DriveLM. We also curate \texttt{navsafe}, a 229-frame, ten-category safety-critical diagnostic subset of \texttt{navtest}. In non-reactive L1 NAVSIM v1 simulation on logged frames (where states reset every frame so multi-step errors cannot compound), Centaur improves Hydra-MDP from 90.3 to 92.6 PDMS in a single-seed NAVSIM v1.0 run and achieves $92.10 \pm 0.33$ PDMS across three seeds on the official NAVSIM v1.1 leaderboard (ranking first among three-seed entries in March 2025, vs.\ 94.8 for human trajectories). Across ablation studies, all four TTT objectives on Hydra-MDP gain 1.2--2.3 PDMS (0.8--2.3 PDMS across base planners, or 0.4 with SVD gradient projection) whereas uncertainty-triggered fallback to prior trajectories degrades all tested planners to 59.9--67.4 PDMS (63.1--65.9 PDMS on Hydra-MDP). Key qualifications are stated explicitly: the three-seed v1.1 margin over standard semantic entropy is small ($92.10 \pm 0.33$ vs.\ $91.87 \pm 0.19$, 0.23 PDMS) and lacks a matched three-seed unadapted Hydra-MDP entry; Cluster Entropy's standalone failure classification accuracy at a fixed cutoff (73.6\%, TPR 62.8\%) trails a trivial never-flag baseline (84.4\% at a 15.6\% failure rate); and on \texttt{navsafe} (which was selected using base-planner scores rather than held out), Centaur improves frame-weighted mean PDMS from 49.4 to 68.9 but regresses on yielding (59.3 vs.\ 71.6) because lateral clustering omits longitudinal stop-or-go ambiguity.

\noindent\textbf{$\chi_0$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0}).}
Extending reliable action to closed-loop physical robot manipulation (L3 fidelity), we formulate long-horizon deformable manipulation failures as distributional inconsistencies across human demonstrations $P_{\mathrm{train}}$, policy-induced trajectory distribution $Q_{\mathrm{model}}$, and hardware execution $P_{\mathrm{test}}$, and introduce \chiz~\cite{sima2026kai0} (co-led with L.~Chen). \chiz combines three modules under a bounded post-training budget ($\sim$20 hours of base demonstrations per task, or 22.5 to 33.6 hours including targeted DAgger recovery rollouts, on eight A100 GPUs): Model Arithmetic (merging checkpoints trained on diverse data subsets in weight space; author contribution), Stage Advantage (stage-aware progress estimation for advantage-weighted policy training; co-author component by L.~Zhao), and Train--Deploy Alignment (combining targeted DAgger failure-recovery trajectories, spatiotemporal observation augmentation, heuristic temporal chunk smoothing, and real-time chunking; author contribution). When post-training $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{black2025pi05} on dual-arm bimanual garment manipulation, \chiz raises 30-trial Task~A (T-shirt flattening and folding) completion rate from $\sim$27\% to $\sim$97\% (8/30 vs.\ 29/30 successes, chart-read from the factorial progression; Wilson 95\% confidence intervals 14--44\% vs.\ 83--99\%) while increasing hourly throughput roughly elevenfold ($\approx 6$ to $\approx 66$ tasks/h) across long-horizon cyclic rollouts. We note that targeted DAgger recovery data accounts for the largest single-step improvement and that total dataset size was not varied against alignment at a matched resource budget.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Additional Contributions.}
Beyond the four core chapters, fifteen additional publications (including first-authored and co-first-authored works) and three US patents trace the broader perceive--reason--act programme (Fig.~\ref{fig:method:lineage}). In 3D perception and architecture, PersFormer and the OpenLane benchmark~\cite{chen2022persformer} (co-first author) established single-camera 3D lane detection; BEVFormer~\cite{li2022bevformer} and our T-PAMI BEV survey~\cite{li2023bevdevils} (co-first author, winning 1st place in the 2022 Waymo 3D Camera-Only Detection Challenge) established multi-camera BEV perception recipes; SDF~\cite{gao2023sdf} and VCD~\cite{huang2023vcd} addressed camera--LiDAR feature fusion and LiDAR-guided distillation, respectively; OpenLane-V2~\cite{wang2023openlanev2} introduced road-topology reasoning; and UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad} (CVPR 2023 Best Paper Award) unified perception, prediction, and planning in a single end-to-end network. In language-grounded reasoning, Hint-AD~\cite{ding2024hint} aligned natural-language explanations with end-to-end driving features; DriveBench~\cite{xie2025vlms} audited VLM driving reliability, exposing visual-grounding shortcuts and dataset answer skew in DriveLM-Data; ETA~\cite{hamdan2025eta} mitigated VLM inference latency via an asynchronous dual-speed fast--slow architecture; and FLARE~\cite{xie2026flare} distilled future-aware VLM latent priors without test-time text generation. In embodied manipulation and simulation, LSH-SMILE~\cite{sima2021lshsmile} (first author) accelerated physics simulation and learning via locality-sensitive hashing; AgiBot World Colosseo~\cite{bu2025agibot_iros} (IROS 2025 Best Paper Finalist) established a 1-million-trajectory, 100-robot manipulation platform and the GO-1 policy; PerturBot~\cite{liu2026perturbot} diagnosed and mitigated visual-language-motor shortcuts in VLA policies; and our CVPRW 2026 perspective paper~\cite{chen2025robot} formulated self-directed robot learning beyond human imitation.

\section{Thesis Outline}\label{sec:intro:outline}\label{tab:intro:abbrev}

The remainder of this dissertation is organised into five chapters following the perceive--reason--act loop, supplemented by Appendix~\ref{app:supp}.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}} (\emph{Perceive: Scene as Occupancy}) introduces 3D semantic occupancy prediction, the OccNet cascade voxel-decoder architecture, the OpenOcc benchmark on nuScenes, and the large-scale OpenScene dataset on nuPlan, evaluating multi-task 3D perception and open-loop motion planning at L0 fidelity.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}} (\emph{Reason: Driving with Graph Visual Question Answering}) formulates Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA), presents the DriveLM-nuScenes and DriveLM-CARLA datasets and evaluation metrics, and evaluates the DriveLM-Agent baseline on in-domain VQA as well as zero-shot cross-sensor (Waymo) and unseen-object (CARLA) transfer at L0 fidelity.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}} (\emph{Act I: Robust End-to-End Driving via Test-Time Training}) presents Centaur, which adapts end-to-end trajectory-scoring planners online via single-step test-time training on Cluster Entropy, and evaluates per-frame adaptation and failure detection on NAVSIM \texttt{navtest} and the 229-frame \texttt{navsafe} diagnostic split at L1 fidelity.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0}} (\emph{Act II: Resource-Aware Robust Manipulation}) analyses distributional inconsistencies across demonstration collection, policy training, and hardware execution in real-world robot manipulation, and evaluates \chiz (Model Arithmetic, Stage Advantage, and Train--Deploy Alignment) on physical dual-arm garment manipulation tasks at L3 fidelity.

\textbf{Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}} (\emph{Conclusion}) summarises the stage-by-stage contributions, synthesises a unified perspective and evidence-graded architecture for self-correcting physical agents, and discusses thesis-wide limitations and future research directions.
