\section{Introduction}\label{drivelm:sec:motivation}

Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet} established a geometry-first 3D occupancy representation, yet occupancy alone does not dictate ego control. A planner must still identify salient road users, anticipate their trajectories and choose a safe manoeuvre. When sensor layouts or obstacle classes depart from the training distribution, memorised input--output mappings falter and structured multi-step reasoning becomes essential, forming the second pillar of this thesis.

Web-pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) bring broad internet priors, human-readable text rationales and sequential prompting, but lack physical grounding in vehicle control. Moreover, early driving QA datasets reduced reasoning to isolated scene summaries or single-hazard chains rather than modelling multi-agent interactions from perception through prediction to planning. This gap motivates our second research question:
\begin{quote}
    \textbf{RQ2 (Reason).} \emph{How can the general knowledge of vision-language models be grounded in driving in a structured and verifiable way that mirrors how humans reason from perception through prediction to planning, and does such reasoning help a driving agent generalise to unseen situations?}
\end{quote}

To investigate RQ2, DriveLM comprises the Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA) task, two datasets (DriveLM-Data), an evaluation suite, and the DriveLM-Agent baseline. GVQA represents dependencies among perception, prediction, planning, behaviour, and motion questions as a directed acyclic graph. DriveLM-nuScenes contains 4,871 keyframes with an average of 91.4 mostly human-authored QA pairs per frame, while DriveLM-CARLA contains about 1.6 million rule-generated QA pairs collected with the PDM-Lite expert~\cite{beisswenger2024pdmlite} on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0. DriveLM-Agent combines BLIP-2 parent-answer context conditioning with RT-2-style waypoint tokens. Graph context harms in-domain nuScenes planning (ADE 1.39 $\rightarrow$ 1.74, collision rate 1.67 $\rightarrow$ 1.89, behaviour accuracy 61.45 $\rightarrow$ 57.49) but improves speed classification (43.90\% to 54.29\%) after zero-shot transfer to Waymo (a perceptual sensing shift in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}); most of the Waymo trajectory advantage over single-frame UniAD (2.63\,m versus 4.16\,m ADE) is already present in the pre-trained backbone (2.78\,m), and the comparison is confounded by model size (3.955 billion versus 131.9 million parameters) and camera input. On withheld CARLA pedestrians, a class-specific prompt improves stopping accuracy (4.59\% to 27.04\%) while lowering regular-scene accuracy (59.63\% to 52.17\%). All reported model comparisons come from single-seed runs.

Sections~\ref{drivelm:sec:related}--\ref{drivelm:sec:agent} review related work and present the GVQA formalism, DriveLM-Data, and DriveLM-Agent; Sections~\ref{drivelm:sec:experiments}--\ref{drivelm:sec:summary} cover experiments, limitations, community adoption, dataset and implementation details (Section~\ref{app:drivelm}), and conclusions.

Autonomous driving stacks face persistent challenges in generalising to altered sensor rigs or rare hazards and in explaining decisions to human occupants or regulators~\cite{chen2024e2esurvey,chib2023recent,atakishiyev2023explaining}. Whereas most end-to-end planners map dense bird's-eye-view (BEV) grids directly to trajectories~\cite{hu2023uniad,chitta2023transfuser,li2023bevdevils}, we model driving through an object-centric progression $P_{1-3}$: spotting key road users, forecasting their future motions and selecting a safe ego manoeuvre. Inspired conceptually by object-centred vision~\cite{Marr82} and core object cognition~\cite{spelke2007core}, $P_{1-3}$ is treated here strictly as a structured task-design hypothesis; Chapters~\ref{ch:occnet}, \ref{ch:drivelm}, and~\ref{ch:centaur} investigate perception, reasoning, and test-time adaptation on their respective standard task stacks rather than passing intermediate tensors inside one monolithic software binary, and this chapter adopts a 2D front-camera VLM (BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2}) to evaluate language-model priors without confounding the representation with Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}'s 3D voxel decoder.

Pre-trained VLMs~\cite{li2023blip2,zhang2023llamaadapter,liu2023llava,wang2023visionllm} support this formulation through three properties: web-scale priors that aid zero-shot robotic transfer~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2,driess2023palme}; interpretable language outputs in place of opaque boxes or waypoints~\cite{li2022bevformer,hu2022stp3,Renz2022CORL,dauner2023parting}; and multi-hop autoregressive reasoning~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2,wei2022cot,wang2023selfconsistency,yao2023tree,besta2023got,ding2023everything}. Passing discrete text between stages, however, blocks end-to-end gradient flow~\cite{chen2024e2esurvey}, and DriveLM-Agent trains under teacher forcing with separate motion adapter weights (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:agent}).

Earlier driving VQA benchmarks used either scene-level rationales, \eg, ``The car is moving into the right lane because it is safe to do so''~\cite{kim2018bddx,kim2019had}, or single-object chains, \eg, ``The ego vehicle stops because there is a pedestrian in a white shirt crossing the intersection in front of the ego vehicle and it does not want to crash into the pedestrian''~\cite{sachdeva2023rank2tell,malla2023drama,qian2024nuscenesqa}. Neither captures multi-object dependencies across $P_{1-3}$. DriveLM instead organises frame QAs into a directed acyclic graph terminating in ego behaviour and motion waypoints (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:teaser}), and pairs open datasets, metrics and evaluation servers with zero-shot tests across camera rigs (nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} to Waymo~\cite{sun2020waymo}) and withheld classes (pedestrians in DriveLM-CARLA), following Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}.

\section{Related Work}\label{drivelm:sec:related}

We summarise prior and concurrent 2024 work here; Chapter~\ref{ch:background} gives broader background and Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:subsequent} reviews later advances.

\paragraph{Generalisation in autonomous driving.}
Long-tail traffic events remain a core safety bottleneck~\cite{chen2024e2esurvey,teng2023motion,tampuu2020survey}. Beyond data augmentation and critical-scenario simulation such as TrafficSim~\cite{suo2021trafficsim,chen2022learning,akhauri2020enhanced,wang2021advsim,hanselmann2022king}, recent methods leverage semantic embeddings to detect out-of-distribution obstacles~\cite{elhafsi2023semantic,wang2023driveanywhere,chen2023categorical,pan2024vlp}; we test whether structured GVQA reasoning aids zero-shot transfer.

\paragraph{Language-grounded driving.}
Concurrent works couple LLMs with driving~\cite{seff2023motionlm,mao2023gptdriver,chen2024drivingwithllms,jin2023adapt,xu2024drivegpt4,wayve2023lingo1,wang2023driveanywhere,marcu2024lingoqa,chen2023categorical,wang2023drivemlm,mao2023language,tian2024drivevlm,shao2024lmdrive,yuan2024ragdriver,pan2024vlp,han2024dmedriver}: GPT-Driver~\cite{mao2023gptdriver} and Driving-with-LLMs~\cite{chen2024drivingwithllms} convert scene detections into text prompts for trajectory generation, while DriveGPT4~\cite{xu2024drivegpt4} predicts control signals and rationales from visual tokens. Unlike those studies, DriveLM evaluates zero-shot transfer across camera rigs and withheld object categories (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Vision-language benchmarks for driving.}
Prior driving-language datasets~\cite{zhou2023vision,Dewangan2023talk2bev,wu2023referkitti,wu2025nuprompt,qian2024nuscenesqa,kim2019had,kim2018bddx,sachdeva2023rank2tell,malla2023drama,marcu2024lingoqa,ding2024holistic} target isolated subtasks: perception queries in NuScenes-QA~\cite{qian2024nuscenesqa} and NuPrompt~\cite{wu2025nuprompt}, post-hoc rationales in BDD-X~\cite{kim2018bddx}, or single-hazard chains in DRAMA~\cite{malla2023drama} and Rank2Tell~\cite{sachdeva2023rank2tell}. DriveLM instead provides dense multi-object graphs spanning perception, prediction and planning (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:dataset}).

\paragraph{Embodied planning with LLMs.}
Robotics pipelines use LLMs~\cite{touvron2023llama,floridi2020gpt,kojima2022large} for embodied planning~\cite{brohan2023can,huang2022inner,zitkovich2023rt2,driess2023palme,liang2023code,karamcheti2023voltron,rana2023sayplan} via multimodal token fusion in PaLM-E~\cite{driess2023palme}, policy code synthesis in Code as Policies~\cite{liang2023code}, and discrete action tokenisation in RT-2~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2}, which we adapt for vehicle waypoints.

\paragraph{Reasoning over graph structures.}
Explicit graph topologies aid multi-step inference~\cite{chen2020review,shi2019scenegraph,besta2023got,chen2019graph}, including scene-graph reasoning in Explainable Neural Modules (XNMs)~\cite{shi2019scenegraph} and directed prompt graphs in Graph of Thoughts~\cite{besta2023got}. DriveLM casts driving QA as a DAG across $P_{1-3}$, $B$ and $M$.

\section{Graph Visual Question Answering}\label{drivelm:sec:gvqa}

Human driving progresses from perceiving salient road users to predicting their interactions and planning an ego response~\cite{macadam2003understanding,groeger2013understanding}. We formalise this progression as GVQA (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:task}) and define stage-wise metrics (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:metrics}).

\subsection{Task Formulation}\label{drivelm:sec:task}

For each frame, GVQA organises question-answer pairs into a directed acyclic graph $G=(V,E)$, where each vertex $v=(q,a)\in V$ queries one or more traffic participants and each directed edge $e=(v_p,v_c)\in E\subseteq V\times V$ passes the parent answer text $a_p$ as context to child question $q_c$ following the topological stage order $P_1 \to P_2 \to P_3 \to B \to M$. Edges encode both cross-object interactions (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:teaser}, centre) and five sequential stages:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Perception} ($P_1$): identifying, describing and localising salient road users in the current frame.
  \item \textbf{Prediction} ($P_2$): forecasting future motions and interactions conditioned on $P_1$.
  \item \textbf{Planning} ($P_3$): enumerating safe candidate ego manoeuvres.
  \item \textbf{Behaviour} ($B$): classifying the high-level ego manoeuvre in natural language.
  \item \textbf{Motion} ($M$): generating continuous BEV future trajectory waypoints.
\end{itemize}

Stages $P_{1-3}$ mirror modular driving stacks~\cite{chen2024e2esurvey}, whereas $B$ and $M$ are derived from the ground-truth future trajectory $M=\{(x_0,y_0),(x_1,y_1),\ldots,(x_N,y_N)\}$ sampled at uniform intervals, with step displacements
\begin{equation}
  \{x,y\}_{\text{dist}} = \{(\delta_{x,1},\delta_{y,1}),\ldots,(\delta_{x,N},\delta_{y,N})\},
  \label{drivelm:eq:xy_dist}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{x,i}=x_i-x_{i-1}$ and $\delta_{y,i}=y_i-y_{i-1}$ ($i=1,2,\ldots,N$). Discrete behaviour $B=(B_{sp},B_{st})$ bridges $P_{1-3}$ and $M$ by binning mean longitudinal and lateral step displacements into five speed and five steering classes:
\begin{align*}
  B_{sp} &\in \{\texttt{fast}_2, \texttt{fast}_1, \texttt{moderate}, \texttt{slow}_1, \texttt{slow}_2\},\\
  B_{st} &\in \{\texttt{left}_2, \texttt{left}_1, \texttt{straight}, \texttt{right}_1, \texttt{right}_2\},
\end{align*}
where subscripts indicate magnitude; richer semantic manoeuvres can also be represented.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}\label{drivelm:sec:metrics}

DriveLM-Metrics score $M$, $B$ and $P_{1-3}$ nodes independently. Motion $M$ uses the open-loop UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad} criteria: average displacement error (\textbf{ADE}), final displacement error (\textbf{FDE}) and \textbf{collision rate}. Behaviour $B$ reports joint, speed and steering \textbf{classification accuracy}. Stage $P_{1-3}$ answers use two main metrics: \textbf{SPICE}~\cite{anderson2016spice}, which computes a scene-graph tuple F-score over objects, attributes and relations, and \textbf{GPT Score}, which queries an unversioned \texttt{gpt-3.5-turbo} endpoint using the prompt archived by DriveLM~\cite{sima2024drivelm} (Section~\ref{drivelm:app:metrics_vqa}) with reference and predicted answers (omitting the question) to output a score from 0 to 100. We selected GPT Score as a headline $P_{1-3}$ indicator post hoc after surface n-gram metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, CIDEr) and Completeness diverged under context ablations (Section~\ref{drivelm:app:metrics}); the API snapshot and judge variance were not recorded, and node-wise scoring does not verify parent--child logical consistency across edges (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:discussion}).

\subsection{DriveLM-nuScenes}\label{drivelm:sec:data_nus}

DriveLM-nuScenes is constructed by sampling interaction-rich nuScenes keyframes, identifying salient road users, and annotating object-grounded $P_{1-3}$ question graphs. Some $P_1$ facts are extracted from nuScenes and OpenLane-V2 labels, while annotators write the remaining $P_1$ content and all 299,000 prediction and planning QA pairs (among 443k total in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:dataset}) using templates designed by five driving specialists~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes,wang2023openlanev2}. Inspectors audit 10\% of each batch; a failed sample returns the full batch for relabelling, after which the English text receives manual cleanup and GPT-3.5 polishing (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:data_collection}, Section~\ref{drivelm:app:nus_collection}). Rejection thresholds, relabelled proportions, and inter-annotator agreement were not archived. The final split contains 4,072 training and 799 validation keyframes, averaging 91.4 QA pairs per frame versus at most 15.3 previously (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:dataset}; Section~\ref{drivelm:app:nuscenes}).

\subsection{PDM-Lite: An Expert Driver for CARLA Leaderboard 2.0}\label{drivelm:sec:data_expert}

We collect DriveLM-CARLA in CARLA 0.9.14 under Leaderboard 2.0~\cite{dosovitskiy2017carla}, whose long-horizon scenarios reduce Leaderboard 1.0 experts such as TransFuser++~\cite{jaeger2023hidden} to a 2\% Driving Score (DS) on the 8+ km validation routes. Co-author Jens Bei{\ss}wenger therefore built PDM-Lite~\cite{beisswenger2024pdmlite}, adapting nuPlan's PDM-Closed~\cite{dauner2023parting,caesar2021nuplan} by pairing Intelligent Driver Model (IDM)~\cite{treiber2000idm} longitudinal control (for lead vehicles, pedestrians, stop signs and traffic lights) with two lateral path candidates instead of 16-trajectory rollouts, scored via TransFuser++ costs~\cite{jaeger2023hidden}. Across 20 official validation routes over three seeds (60 runs by co-authors; Section~\ref{drivelm:app:pdm}), PDM-Lite reaches a pooled mean DS of 36.3\,$\pm$\,28.1 (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:pdm_results};~\cite{sima2024drivelm} cited $\sim$44\% from an early subset), with a route-mean SD of 23.9 and within-route three-seed SDs of 0.0 to 37.4.

\subsection{DriveLM-CARLA}\label{drivelm:sec:data_carla}

PDM-Lite drives urban, residential and rural routes at 4 FPS while logging sensors, simulator states and rule activations (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:data_collection}), sampling keyframes when longitudinal or lateral decisions change. Privileged actor states and expert rules across 35 of the 38 Leaderboard 2.0 scenarios (excluding \mbox{\emph{InterurbanAdvancedActorFlow}}, \mbox{\emph{MergerIntoSlowTraffic}} and \mbox{\emph{VehicleTurningRoute}}) populate templates that wire $P_{1-3}$, $B$ and $M$ QAs into a 1.6M-QA DAG (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:dataset}, Section~\ref{drivelm:app:carla}).

\section{DriveLM-Agent: A GVQA Baseline}\label{drivelm:sec:agent}

DriveLM-Agent adapts BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2} via Q-Former prompting, parent-to-child text conditioning and RT-2 waypoint tokenisation~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2} across three tiers (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:model_pipeline}): (1) $P_{1-3}$ object reasoning, (2) discrete behaviour classification $B$, and (3) continuous trajectory generation $M$.

\subsection{Prompting with Context}\label{drivelm:sec:context}

Unlike direct pixel-to-waypoint regression~\cite{Prakash2021transfuser,chitta2021neat}, DriveLM-Agent prepends every parent QA $v_p$ (`$(v_p,v_c)\in E$', concatenated when $|\{v_p:(v_p,v_c)\in E\}|>1$) under a ``\emph{Context:}'' prefix to child prompt $v_c$. Training uses teacher forcing on all frame QAs, whereas inference rolls out $P_1 \rightarrow P_2 \rightarrow P_3 \rightarrow B \rightarrow M$ autoregressively on sampled subgraphs (Sections~\ref{drivelm:app:agent} and~\ref{drivelm:app:impl}).

\paragraph{Context aggregation through behaviour.}
Distinct hazards (red lights, stop signs, braking lead cars) often require the same ego manoeuvre. Behaviour node $B$ summarises upstream $P_{1-3}$ QAs into a discrete command bottleneck: predicting $B$ before $M$ without $P_{1-3}$ context cuts nuScenes ADE from 2.63 (BLIP-RT-2) to 1.39 (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:arch}), whereas $P_{1-3}$ context aids $B$ only under transfer to Waymo (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:experiments}).

\subsection{Trajectory Tokenisation for Motion}\label{drivelm:sec:traj_token}

Following RT-2~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2}, longitudinal and lateral waypoint offsets are quantised into 256 uniform bins mapped to 256 BLIP-2 vocabulary tokens (Section~\ref{drivelm:app:agent}). Stage $M$ reuses the BLIP-2 backbone with separate LoRA weights trained solely on motion QAs conditioned on the image and $B$, though any lightweight LLM~\cite{radford2019language} or command-conditioned head~\cite{hu2022stp3,wu2022tcp} could substitute.

\section{Experiments}\label{drivelm:sec:experiments}

We evaluate five questions: (1) open-loop VLM trajectory planning; (2) zero-shot cross-sensor transfer; (3) transfer to a withheld object category; (4) question-level ablations across $P_{1-3}$; and (5) context effects on $P_{1-3}$ VQA (further ablations in Section~\ref{drivelm:app:exp}).

\paragraph{Setup.}
Models use LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} with single seed 1234: 10 epochs at batch size 2 per GPU on DriveLM-nuScenes \texttt{train} ($\sim$7 hours on 8 V100 GPUs), and 6 epochs on DriveLM-CARLA keyframe \texttt{train} ($\sim$6 hours on 4 A100 GPUs; Section~\ref{drivelm:app:impl}).

\paragraph{Evaluation sets and uncertainty.}
We test on 799 DriveLM-nuScenes \texttt{val} keyframes, 1,000 sampled Waymo \texttt{val} frames, and DriveLM-CARLA \texttt{val} (161 regular frames to within 0.01 rounding in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:carla}) plus DriveLM-CARLA-ped (unarchived QA denominators/histograms). Single-seed runs preclude cross-seed variance; binomial 95\% Wilson intervals are $\pm$3.4 points at 60\% over 799 frames and $\pm$3.1 points at 50\% over 1,000 frames.

\subsection{VLMs for End-to-End Driving}\label{drivelm:sec:exp_arch}

To reduce open-loop ego-history shortcuts on nuScenes~\cite{li2024ego}, DriveLM-Agent omits CAN-bus ego status and evaluates on the 799 interaction-rich keyframes (Section~\ref{drivelm:app:nus_collection}), though no ego-status-only baseline was run on this split.

\paragraph{Baselines and variants.}
\textbf{Command Mean} averages training trajectories sharing the frame's command (`turn left', `turn right', `go straight'); \textbf{UniAD-Single} adapts multi-frame surround-view UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad} (shown in italics) to single-frame input; and \textbf{BLIP-RT-2} fine-tunes BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2} directly on waypoint tokens without $B$ or $P_{1-3}$. DriveLM-Agent compares `\textbf{None}' ($B$ without $P_{1-3}$ context), `\textbf{Chain}' (only $P_3$ passed to $B$) and `\textbf{Graph}' (all sampled $P_{1-3}$ QAs passed to $B$).

\subsection{Generalisation to Unseen Objects}\label{drivelm:sec:exp_carla}

Co-authors evaluated zero-shot transfer to a withheld object category by removing all pedestrian frames from DriveLM-CARLA \texttt{train}/\texttt{val} and assembling DriveLM-CARLA-ped solely from frames where a pedestrian crosses ahead of the ego vehicle, requiring a stop with straight steering. Because every frame in DriveLM-CARLA-ped shares this identical target action, a constant stopping rule (not separately evaluated) would score 100; accuracy on DriveLM-CARLA-ped therefore measures stopping recall and must be read alongside pedestrian-free DriveLM-CARLA accuracy.

\paragraph{Baselines and variants.}
We compare against TransFuser++~\cite{jaeger2023hidden}, which takes higher-resolution images plus LiDAR and requires dense depth, segmentation, 3D box and HD map supervision, preventing joint training on general vision datasets. By contrast, DriveLM-Agent's text interface permits co-training on DriveLM-CARLA alongside COCO~\cite{lin2014microsoft} and GQA~\cite{hudson2019gqa}. At test time, we evaluate two additions (wording in Section~\ref{drivelm:app:impl}): (1) `\textbf{+ Pedestrian QA}', which inserts a pedestrian-check $P_1$ prompt whose output selects a follow-up ego-action prompt appended to behaviour context; and (2) `\textbf{GT}', an oracle setting that substitutes ground-truth $P_{1-3}$ answers at test time. Under `+ Pedestrian QA', `GT' supplies the affirmative ground-truth answer to ``Is there a person in the scene?'' on every DriveLM-CARLA-ped frame and triggers its ground-truth follow-up, leaking the target condition rather than bounding achievable performance.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{drivelm:sec:discussion}

Our experiments show that graph-structured prompting hurts in-domain open-loop planning on DriveLM-nuScenes relative to context-free behaviour prediction (ADE 1.39 $\rightarrow$ 1.74, collision rate 1.67 $\rightarrow$ 1.89, behaviour accuracy 61.45 $\rightarrow$ 57.49 in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:arch}) and yields gains only under distribution shift: improving speed accuracy on Waymo (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:exp_waymo}) and stopping recall on unseen CARLA pedestrians once a class-specific prompt is added (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:exp_carla}). Several methodological and architectural limitations bound these findings.

\paragraph{Backbone versus graph.}
The transfer experiments do not isolate graph reasoning from foundation-model pre-training. On Waymo, BLIP-RT-2 and context-free DriveLM-Agent already achieve 2.78 and 2.76 ADE, respectively, compared with 4.16 for UniAD-Single; full graph context changes ADE to 2.63 ($P_2$-only 2.62 in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:waymo_supp}) and primarily improves speed accuracy, from 43.90\% to 54.29\%. The remaining comparison is confounded because UniAD-Single loses its rear camera and has 131.9 million parameters, whereas DriveLM-Agent keeps its front-view layout and has 3.955 billion (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:latency}). The CARLA result is narrower: pedestrian stopping improves to 27.04\% only after adding a hand-crafted class-specific prompt alongside unablated COCO/GQA co-training, regular-scene accuracy falls from 59.63\% to 52.17\%, and the 92.35\% `GT' result directly reveals pedestrian presence. These are single-run findings.

\paragraph{Omitted baselines and consistency checks.}
Five diagnostic checks were omitted: (1) \emph{majority-class baselines}, where DriveBench~\cite{xie2025vlms} later found $\sim$78.6\% ``Going Straight'' labels in DriveLM-nuScenes (exceeding `Chain' at 75.34 in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:arch} and $P_1$-only rows at 74.34--75.72 in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:question_wise}), while a constant stop scores 100 on DriveLM-CARLA-ped; (2) \emph{image-blind baselines} on our evaluated checkpoints; (3) \emph{ego-status-only baselines} on the 799 keyframes; (4) \emph{cross-edge logical consistency} between parent ($P_{1-3}$) and child ($B, M$) predictions, which node-wise DriveLM-Metrics do not test; and (5) \emph{multi-run LLM judge variance} under a version-locked model to test whether 1--2 point GPT Score gaps (Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:vqa}) exceed judge noise.

\paragraph{Data priors, latency, single-view input and open-loop scope.}
DriveLM-nuScenes reports process-level quality control without inter-annotator agreement or post-audit error rates, and subjective keyframe selection leaves ``Going Straight'' dominant (Section~\ref{drivelm:app:nus_collection}). Sequential LLM decoding runs at 0.16 FPS (8.5 tokens/s, $\sim$11$\times$ slower than UniAD's 1.8 FPS, Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:latency}; quantisation, token pruning~\cite{xiao2023smoothquant,shi2023crossget} or multi-rate scheduling could lower cost), which also made 20\,Hz synchronous closed-loop CARLA evaluation ($\sim$125\,s compute per simulated second) impractical here. Furthermore, DriveLM-Agent consumes a single front-camera image without LiDAR or temporal history, yet trains and evaluates on unfiltered six-camera DriveLM-nuScenes QAs (Section~\ref{drivelm:app:nus_composition}), forcing non-front queries to rely on coordinate text or label priors. Finally, prompt templates remain heuristic, and $P_{1-3}$ VQA gains translate weakly into planning improvements.

\section{From Benchmark to Community}\label{drivelm:sec:community}

We released the DriveLM datasets, checkpoints and evaluation server\footnote{\url{https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/DriveLM}}, which anchored the \emph{Driving with Language} track of the CVPR 2024 Autonomous Grand Challenge\footnote{\url{https://opendrivelab.com/challenge2024/}} (led by the thesis author). Evaluated on DriveLM-nuScenes v1.1 \texttt{val} (withheld test answers) using a weighted composite of ChatGPT score (0.4) and BLEU/ROUGE-L/CIDEr, coordinate matching and accuracy (0.2 each) from a LLaMA-Adapter V2~\cite{gao2023llamaadapterv2} starter kit, the track drew 978 submissions from 152 teams across 14 countries; team ADLM won the Innovation Award for aligning BEV features with an LLM. Separately, PDM-Lite~\cite{beisswenger2024pdmlite} became a standard data-collection expert on CARLA Leaderboard 2.0~\cite{hamdan2025eta}.

In follow-up work co-authored by the thesis author, DriveBench~\cite{xie2025vlms} audited 12 VLMs across 200 DriveLM-nuScenes keyframes under clean, 15 corrupted and black-image inputs, finding that models retain most of their score without images (\eg, GPT-4o kept $\sim$95\% of its clean GPT Score on text alone, whereas human perception accuracy fell from 93.33\% to 33.33\% in fog). DriveBench traced this shortcut to the 78.6\% ``Going Straight'' prior and 2D camera/coordinate prompt leakage, and showed that n-gram metrics and reference-only LLM judges reward memorised templates (the fine-tuned DriveLM baseline beat GPT-4o on planning ROUGE-L, 53.12 \vs 6.54, while trailing Qwen2-VL-72B and GPT-4o on prediction GPT Score, 44.33 \vs 49.35 and 51.30). This audit shows that Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:vqa} and behaviour accuracies are vulnerable to text priors, and completes the benchmark--challenge--audit loop of Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}.

\subsection{Subsequent Developments (2023--2026)}\label{drivelm:sec:subsequent}

Follow-up work has advanced three areas: (1) \textbf{Reasoning verification}, moving from fixed QA graphs and isolated node metrics to executable visual programs~\cite{gupta2023visprog,suris2023vipergpt}, crop-grounded Visual CoT~\cite{shao2024visualcot}, and driving benchmarks that score reasoning--action consistency directly, including Reason2Drive~\cite{nie2024reason2drive}, counterfactual 3D OmniDrive~\cite{wang2025omnidrive} and step-annotated DriveLMM-o1~\cite{ishaq2025drivelmmo1}; (2) \textbf{Unified and closed-loop policies}, including CoT-supervised EMMA~\cite{hwang2025emma}, RL-aligned token planners AutoVLA~\cite{zhou2025autovla} and Alpamayo-R1~\cite{nvidia2025alpamayo}, closed-loop evaluations on Bench2Drive~\cite{jia2024bench2drive,fu2025orion} and CARLA (where SimLingo~\cite{renz2025simlingo} won the 2024 CARLA Challenge using balanced DriveLM-style VQA), dual-system fast/slow planners~\cite{tian2024drivevlm,jiang2026senna,hamdan2025eta}, and text-free latent feature alignment in FLARE (co-authored by the thesis author)~\cite{xie2026flare}; and (3) \textbf{Evaluation}, replacing generic LLM judges and open-loop $L_2$ error with trained verifiers such as Lingo-Judge~\cite{marcu2024lingoqa} and rater-preference long-tail benchmarks such as WOD-E2E~\cite{xu2026wode2e}.

\subsection{DriveLM-nuScenes Schema, Workflow, and Statistics}

DriveLM-nuScenes comprises 4,072 training and 799 validation keyframes, pairing clip-level ego-manoeuvre descriptions with six-camera 2D bounding boxes and three-stage frame QAs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Perception} ($P_1$): 144k QAs combining automated template extraction from nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} and OpenLane-V2~\cite{wang2023openlanev2} with manual questions on visible scene attributes and road-user states.
  \item \textbf{Prediction} ($P_2$): 153k QAs on future ego and road-user interactions, with all answers \emph{written manually}.
  \item \textbf{Planning} ($P_3$): 146k QAs on candidate ego actions, collision risks and rationales, collected \emph{through manual annotation}.
\end{itemize}
In total, human annotators authored all 299k prediction and planning answers (over two thirds of the 443k QAs in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:dataset}). Every referenced road user or traffic control device is indexed by a \textbf{c tag} $\langle c, \mathit{CAM}, x, y\rangle$ storing instance identifier $c$, source camera $\mathit{CAM}$ and 2D bounding-box centre $(x, y)$, linked to a per-frame metadata dictionary recording 2D box dimensions, semantic category, motion state and visual appearance (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:composition}).

Licensed drivers annotate six-camera panoramas in three steps (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:nus_anno_pipeline}, left): (1) \emph{selecting keyframes} where interactions or state transitions are judged imminent and writing clip-level ego summaries (without kinematic filtering, leaving straight driving frequent as noted in Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:community}); (2) \emph{marking key objects} by selecting projected 2D nuScenes boxes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} or drawing new 2D boxes for unlabelled hazards that influence the ego trajectory; and (3) \emph{authoring QAs} via structured multiple-choice options, a custom ``Other (blank entry)'' slot and free-form text, alongside rule-extracted factual QAs. Quality control operates on batches of 8 video clips (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:nus_anno_pipeline}, right): automated scripts and human inspectors audit each batch, returning the entire 8-clip packet for full rework whenever manual labels fail inspection, whereas template errors are corrected directly by inspectors. Chinese annotations are translated into English using a domain terminology glossary combined with human-proofread GPT-3.5 translation; a second release adds GPT-3.5 stylistic polishing~\cite{sima2024drivelm}. Stage-level QA proportions, fine-grained subcategory breakdowns within each stage, and key-object class and question distributions for traffic controls versus dynamic road users are shown in Figs.~\ref{drivelm:fig:data_distribution}, \ref{drivelm:fig:nus_statis} and~\ref{drivelm:fig:nus_obj_stats}.

\subsection{DriveLM-CARLA Topology, PDM-Lite, and Collection}\label{drivelm:app:carla}\label{drivelm:app:carla_composition}\label{drivelm:app:carla_collection}

DriveLM-CARLA pairs multi-sensor simulator logs with a deterministic rule-generated QA graph (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:carla_graph}) covering road layout, dynamic vehicles, traffic signals and stop signs, and is extensible to parked vehicles, weather and speed signs. Synthetic collection eliminates manual labelling cost while providing synchronised multi-view RGB, LiDAR, depth, and perspective/BEV semantic segmentation streams, and each object QA stores a simulator actor ID together with explicit parent--child graph edges.

Because Leaderboard 1.0 agents such as TransFuser++~\cite{jaeger2023hidden} fail on the 38 long-horizon scenarios of CARLA 0.9.14 Leaderboard 2.0~\cite{dosovitskiy2017carla}, co-authors developed PDM-Lite~\cite{beisswenger2024pdmlite}, a privileged six-stage rule-based expert combining A* route following, kinematic actor forecasting, IDM longitudinal speed control, two-path lateral selection and PID actuation. Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:pdm_results} reports co-author evaluations of PDM-Lite across the 20 official Leaderboard 2.0 validation routes over three seeds: routes 10--19 repeat the geometry of routes 0--9 under altered weather and traffic, and because PDM-Lite reads weather-invariant simulator states, inter-run differences reflect traffic randomisation. Pooling all 60 route--seed runs gives a mean Driving Score (DS) of 36.3\,$\pm$\,28.1, with a 20-route mean SD of 23.9 (inter-seed SD of the 20-route average is not recoverable from per-route summary cells).

For dataset generation, we split the 90 Town 12 training routes (780.6\,km) and 20 Town 13 evaluation routes (247.6\,km) into $\sim$150\,m segments across two $10\times10\,\text{km}^2$ maps, discarding degenerate loops, randomising daytime weather presets, and spawning cars, trucks, vans, bicycles and motorcycles. Alongside the five sensor streams (DriveLM-Agent uses front RGB only, whereas auxiliary modalities train TransFuser++), we log privileged simulator dictionaries for:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Ego vehicle}: actor ID, 3D bounding box, speed and brake state.
  \item \emph{Other vehicles}: actor ID, 3D box, LiDAR point count, ego distance, speed, control commands, colour, vehicle class, wheel count, light status, road/lane IDs, junction flag/distance and active route command.
  \item \emph{Pedestrians}: actor ID, 3D box, LiDAR hits, age/gender attributes, ego distance, speed and lane location.
  \item \emph{Traffic lights, stop signs, parked cars, landmarks and weather}: 3D boxes, ego distance, signal phase or speed limit, lane relevance and simulator weather parameters.
\end{itemize}
These dictionaries populate deterministic QA templates directly without LLM paraphrasing.

\subsection{DriveLM-Metrics, Tokenisation, and Implementation}\label{drivelm:app:metrics_behavior}\label{drivelm:app:agent_context}\label{drivelm:app:agent_tokenisation}\label{drivelm:app:waymo_ablation}\label{drivelm:app:more_vlms}\label{drivelm:app:complexity}

DriveLM-Metrics scores each node independently of parent accuracy across three stage suites. For stage $P_{1-3}$ VQA, we compute five lexical/graph metrics (\textbf{BLEU-4}~\cite{papineni2002bleu} for n-gram precision, \textbf{ROUGE-L}~\cite{lin2004rouge} for longest common subsequence recall, \textbf{METEOR}~\cite{lavie-agarwal-2007-meteor} for exact, stem and synonym unigram alignment with word-order penalty, \textbf{CIDEr}~\cite{vedantam2015cider} for TF-IDF cosine similarity, and \textbf{SPICE}~\cite{anderson2016spice} for scene-graph tuple F-score), together with \textbf{Completeness} (the fraction of frame QAs achieving $\text{SPICE}>0.5$) and \textbf{GPT Score}, which queries \texttt{gpt-3.5-turbo}~\cite{ouyang2022training,chatgpt,sima2024drivelm} using reference \texttt{\{GT\}} and prediction \texttt{\{Pred\}}:
\begin{quote}\small\raggedright
\textit{System:} \texttt{An evaluator who rates my answer based on the correct answer.}\\[2pt]
\textit{User:} \texttt{Rate my answer based on the correct answer out of 100, with higher scores indicating that the answer is closer to the correct answer, and you should be accurate to single digits like 62, 78, 41, etc. This is the correct answer: \{GT\}. This is my answer: \{Pred\}.}
\end{quote}
Because the prompt omits the question text, camera views and grading rubrics, scores cluster tightly (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:community}) with untracked run variance. For stage $B$, we report classification accuracy on joint $(B_{sp},B_{st})$ and marginal speed ($B_{sp}$) and steering ($B_{st}$) bins derived from future displacements (Eq.~\eqref{drivelm:eq:xy_dist}). For stage $M$, we report open-loop \textbf{ADE} (mean Euclidean error over 1\,s, 2\,s and 3\,s), \textbf{FDE} (error at 3\,s) and \textbf{collision rate} (percentage of frames with ego--obstacle box overlap across 1/2/3\,s in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:arch}), following instantaneous UniAD evaluation~\cite{hu2023uniad} rather than cumulative ST-P3 averaging~\cite{hu2022stp3}\footnote{\raggedright\url{https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/UniAD/commit/ffac1a69a6d01c9dca5cd2ed751007896df351f8}}; results on our 799 keyframes are not directly comparable to the full 6,019-frame nuScenes \texttt{val} split.

In DriveLM-Agent, training applies teacher forcing~\cite{lamb2016professor,toomarian1992learning} across all frame QAs using LoRA~\cite{hu2021lora} (constant learning rate $10^{-4}$, seed 1234, default rank/scaling, 400-token cap), prepending ground-truth parent QAs to child queries along edges $e\in E$ under cross-entropy loss. Following RT-2~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2}, each trajectory coordinate $(x,y)$ is quantised into 256 uniform bins mapped to multi-digit numeric tokens in \texttt{blip-2-flan-t5-xl} (sparing single-digit tokens) between SOT and EOT markers and decoded back to metric waypoints (Fig.~\ref{drivelm:fig:simple_pipeline}). Because $P_{1-3}$ QA counts vary widely across DriveLM-nuScenes frames (variance $>260$), validation samples a balanced template subset ($\ge 1$ prompt per stage per frame) and rolls out $P_1 \rightarrow P_2 \rightarrow P_3 \rightarrow B \rightarrow M$ autoregressively from predicted parent answers. For zero-shot Waymo transfer (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:exp_waymo}), generic $P_1$ prompts run on front-camera frames and detections of shared categories (``cars'', ``trucks'', ``pedestrians'') trigger rule-based $P_2$/$P_3$ prompts. For DriveLM-CARLA-ped (Section~\ref{drivelm:sec:exp_carla}), we insert \emph{Is there a person in the scene?} and, when affirmative, \emph{What should the vehicle do based on the pedestrian that is crossing the road?} (otherwise \emph{What should the ego vehicle do?}), appending the resulting answer to the behaviour context.

Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:vqa_more_metrics} reports the full VQA metric suite on DriveLM-nuScenes \texttt{val}, showing that graph conditioning lowers ROUGE-L for DriveLM-Agent (66.79 $\rightarrow$ 65.58) and CIDEr/Completeness for zero-shot BLIP-2, which motivated our post-hoc selection of GPT Score. Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:waymo_supp} expands the Waymo transfer study across individual stage contexts: behaviour accuracy rankings closely mirror ADE (top two within 0.01\,m), with $P_2$-only context achieving the best steering accuracy (78.67) and ADE (2.62) and full $P_{1-3}$ context achieving the best overall behaviour (39.73) and speed accuracy (54.29). Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:llama} substitutes a 9-epoch LLaMA-Adapter V2 (7B)~\cite{gao2023llamaadapterv2} backbone on DriveLM-nuScenes, where single-frame performance matches BLIP-2 and multi-frame input modestly improves behaviour accuracy (57.83) and collision rate (1.69). Caching visual tokens and batching same-stage queries can reduce the latency reported in Table~\ref{drivelm:tab:latency}. More broadly, coupling driving stacks with foundation VLMs provides human-readable rationales for passengers and regulators, though hallucinations and compute cost must be addressed before safety-critical deployment.

\section{Chapter Summary}\label{drivelm:sec:summary}

Addressing RQ2, this chapter introduced DriveLM and Graph VQA (GVQA) to link pre-trained VLMs to driving via a directed QA graph across perception, prediction, planning, behaviour and motion. We built DriveLM-nuScenes (4,871 keyframes, 91.4 QAs/frame) and DriveLM-CARLA (1.6M QAs from PDM-Lite~\cite{beisswenger2024pdmlite}, which scores 36.3\,$\pm$\,28.1 DS over 20 Leaderboard 2.0 validation routes versus 2\% for TransFuser++), alongside DriveLM-Metrics and the BLIP-2 baseline DriveLM-Agent.

In single-run experiments, graph context hurt in-domain nuScenes planning relative to context-free behaviour prediction (ADE 1.39 $\rightarrow$ 1.74, collision rate 1.67 $\rightarrow$ 1.89, behaviour accuracy 61.45 $\rightarrow$ 57.49; UniAD-Single 1.80 ADE) and helped only under distribution shift. On Waymo, most of the ADE gain over UniAD-Single (2.63 \vs 4.16) came from the backbone before graph conditioning (BLIP-RT-2 2.78, context-free 2.76) amidst rear-camera and 3.955B \vs 131.9M parameter asymmetries, while full-graph context raised speed accuracy (43.90 $\rightarrow$ 54.29) and $P_2$-only context matched ADE (2.62). On withheld CARLA pedestrians (by co-authors), TransFuser++ fell from 70.19 to 8.72 and DriveLM-Agent rose from 4.59 to 27.04 only with a hand-crafted pedestrian prompt (reducing regular-scene accuracy from 59.63 to 52.17; 92.35 `GT' leaks pedestrian presence). Question-level and $P_{1-3}$ VQA ablations showed modest gains amidst 0.16 FPS latency (8.5 tokens/s), front-camera-only input, omitted diagnostic/consistency baselines, and text-shortcut vulnerabilities later exposed by DriveBench~\cite{xie2025vlms}. Because static weights still cannot self-correct when deployment shifts miscalibrate confidence, Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur} introduces online test-time training over the five steering behaviour clusters defined here.
