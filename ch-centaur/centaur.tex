\section{Introduction}\label{centaur:sec:intro}

While Chapters~\ref{ch:occnet} and~\ref{ch:drivelm} examined perception and reasoning through 3D occupancy and vision-language grounding, action remains the ultimate test of an embodied agent. Because an end-to-end planner directly governs vehicle motion on public roads, any planning error risks a collision when encountering rare, safety-critical situations absent from training logs, such as unfamiliar roundabouts, ambiguous yellow signals, or sudden pedestrian crossings, a long-tail exposure known as the curse of rarity~\cite{liu2024curse}.

Existing end-to-end planners are often paired with fallback rules that override plans upon rule breaches or post-hoc cost optimisation over auxiliary drivable-area maps and obstacle boxes. Those safeguards add engineering and annotation requirements and may trade progress for caution. Centaur instead asks a bounded version of RQ3a: \emph{whether planner weights can adapt at deployment under distributional shift without ground-truth labels or a hand-crafted test-time objective.} We compare that adaptation with a simple uncertainty-triggered prior-trajectory fallback implemented for this study and with UniAD's reported post-hoc optimisation~\cite{hu2023uniad}. The scope is deliberately limited: NAVSIM scores independent plans per frame from logged states, so closed-loop errors cannot accumulate (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:setup}), and while no rules run at test time, the base Hydra-MDP heads were trained offline to distil rule-based simulator metrics (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:prelim}).

We propose \emph{Centaur} (\textbf{C}luster \textbf{En}tropy for \textbf{T}est-time tr\textbf{A}ining using \textbf{U}nce\textbf{r}tainty), which updates a pre-trained planner via test-time training (TTT) rather than overriding its outputs, taking a single gradient step averaged over a short ring buffer of recent frames to reduce trajectory-score dispersion (evaluated sequentially here, though compatible with asynchronous execution). Three contributions anchor this chapter: (i) introducing TTT for an end-to-end planner on NAVSIM (March 2025), contrasting with forecasting TTT~\cite{park2024t4p} and fixed-weight search~\cite{yang2024diffusiones}; (ii) formulating \emph{Cluster Entropy} across five lateral steering directions; and (iii) curating \texttt{navsafe}, a 229-frame diagnostic subset of \texttt{navtest} across ten safety-critical categories guided by the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) pre-crash typology~\cite{najm2007pre} and ranked by baseline Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp} scores on NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim}.

In NAVSIM v1.0 non-reactive 4-second replay~\cite{dauner2024navsim} (where each frame resets to the human state, leaving closed-loop shift in Chapter~\ref{ch:intro} and long-horizon drift untested), Centaur raises Hydra-MDP from 90.3 to 92.6 PDMS in one run (human reference: 94.8). On v1.1 it reaches $92.10 \pm 0.33$ over three seeds (ranking first among three-seed leaderboard entries in March 2025), although the leaderboard has no matched three-seed unadapted Hydra-MDP entry. The 229-frame \texttt{navsafe} analysis (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navsafe_overall}, Section~\ref{centaur:sec:navsafe_results}) is less uniform: Centaur improves the frame-weighted mean from 49.36 to 68.86 (68.9 \vs 49.4), the simple category mean from 48.33 to 71.96 (72.0 \vs 48.3), and the preprint Overall from 56.47 to 74.14, but the subset was selected using Hydra-MDP scores and performance on yielding falls from 71.6 to 59.3 while trailing human logs (94.65). NAVSIM derives from OpenScene~\cite{sima2023openscene} (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}) and nuPlan~\cite{caesar2021nuplan}, and Cluster Entropy reuses DriveLM's five lateral steering bins~\cite{sima2024drivelm} (Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}) without longitudinal speed bins. Sections~\ref{centaur:sec:related}--\ref{centaur:sec:summary} cover related work, method, \texttt{navsafe}, experiments, analysis, discussion, supplementary evaluations (Section~\ref{app:centaur}), and summary.

Operating safely alongside human traffic requires handling rare interactions and unfamiliar road geometries~\cite{shalevshwartz2017rss,liu2024curse}. While end-to-end neural planners scale directly with driving logs~\cite{chitta2023transfuser,hu2023uniad,weng2024paradrive,li2024hydramdp,chen2024e2esurvey,li2023open}, deployed policies are routinely paired with rule-based fallback modules that override the network under hazard conditions~\cite{chitta2023transfuser,vitelli2022safetynet,waywe2024ad2} or post-hoc cost optimisers~\cite{hu2023uniad,Hu2023hoplan} that require expensive intermediate perceptual annotations such as drivable surfaces and motion forecasts, undercutting end-to-end learning from logged ego poses~\cite{Prakash2021transfuser,wu2022tcp}. Our goal is to improve deployment reliability in a purely data-driven manner at inference time without hand-tuned fallback rules or test-time perceptual supervision, while noting that our base planner distils rule-based simulation scores offline (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:prelim}).

Centaur achieves this online adaptation via test-time training~\cite{sun2020ttt,liang2025ttasurvey,wang2022cotta,Gao2024fstta}, updating score decoder weights by gradient descent on an unsupervised uncertainty loss over recent observations (evaluated sequentially in Section~\ref{centaur:sec:latency}, though compatible with asynchronous execution). Whereas classic trajectory regression policies output a single continuous waypoint sequence~\cite{hu2023uniad,chitta2023transfuser,weng2024paradrive,li2024ego}, discrete trajectory-scoring planners~\cite{li2024hydramdp,Sadat2020P3,Casas2021mp3} evaluate a fixed candidate vocabulary analogous to classification~\cite{Gao2024fstta,wang2021tent}, enabling Cluster Entropy over five directional trajectory buckets.

In Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:teaser}, a single TTT step on Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp} shifts selection from an isolated `slight left' outlier to the `slight right' cluster, preventing a turn into oncoming traffic. Cluster Entropy can also flag high-risk frames at a fixed threshold, attaining the highest true-positive rate among tested uncertainty estimators, though its overall accuracy remains below a never-flag baseline and threshold-free curves were not logged (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:failure}). Lastly, because aggregate test averages obscure rare failures, we curate \texttt{navsafe}, a 229-frame NHTSA-guided subset of \texttt{navtest} that exposes shared vulnerabilities across planners (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:navsafe}).

\section{Related Work}\label{centaur:sec:related}

\paragraph{End-to-end autonomous driving.} Modern end-to-end architectures map raw multi-view camera and LiDAR streams directly to future waypoints or control commands with a single trainable network~\cite{chen2024e2esurvey,Prakash2021transfuser,chitta2021neat,jaeger2023hidden,zimmerlin2024tf,chen2024vadv2,jiang2023vad,hu2023uniad,hu2022model,jia2023think,wang2023drivedreamer,jia2023driveadapter,li2024hydramdp,liao2025diffusiondrive}, including the 3D occupancy pipeline in Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}~\cite{sima2023occnet}.

\paragraph{End-to-end planner deployment.} Deployed neural policies typically attach rule-based verifiers or cost maps: UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad} refines trajectories against an obstacle cost map, TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser} triggers hard braking on LiDAR obstacles, and SafetyNet~\cite{vitelli2022safetynet} validates kinematic feasibility before falling back to a rule controller. Centaur invokes no hand-crafted rules at test time, though its base policy distils rule-based simulation scores offline (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:prelim}).

\paragraph{Test-time compute and training.} Allocating test-time computation strengthens reasoning~\cite{openaio1}, while test-time adaptation and training (TTT)~\cite{liang2025ttasurvey} improve robustness in image recognition~\cite{sun2020ttt,wang2021tent,hakim2023clust3,marsden2024universal}, multimodal alignment~\cite{shu2022test,yoon2024c}, navigation~\cite{Gao2024fstta}, motion forecasting~\cite{park2024t4p}, manipulation~\cite{hansen2021pad}, and language modelling~\cite{sun2024learning}. Unlike forecasting TTT (T4P~\cite{park2024t4p}) or fixed-weight trajectory search (Diffusion-ES~\cite{yang2024diffusiones}, TOAD~\cite{xu2026toad}), Centaur adapts end-to-end planner decoder weights directly on NAVSIM (see Chapter~\ref{ch:intro} and Section~\ref{centaur:app:related}).

\subsection{Preliminaries}\label{centaur:sec:prelim}

An end-to-end driving policy $\pi$ maps sensor observations $\mathbf{x}_0$ and discrete route command $\mathbf{c}_0$ at step $0$ to $N$ Bird's Eye View (BEV) waypoints $\tilde{\mathbf{t}} = (\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_N)$. While TTT can also adapt continuous trajectory regression policies by attaching evidential heads (see Sections~\ref{centaur:app:regression} and~\ref{centaur:app:ablations} for UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad} and TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser}), we focus primarily on trajectory scoring planners such as Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp}, VADv2~\cite{chen2024vadv2}, and RAD~\cite{gao2025rad}.

\paragraph{Trajectory scoring.} Rather than regressing continuous coordinates, a scoring planner evaluates a fixed vocabulary of $k$ candidate trajectories $\{\mathbf{t}_j\}_{j=1}^k$, predicting score feature vectors $\{s_j\}_{j=1}^k$ that a combining function $\phi$ maps to a scalar utility to pick the highest-scoring plan:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:multi-score-output}
  \{s_j\}_{j=1}^k = \{ \pi_\theta(\mathbf{t}_j, \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{c}_0) \}_{j=1}^k,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:cost-select}
  \hat{\mathbf{t}}_0 = \argmax_{\mathbf{t}_j} \{\phi(s_j)\}_{j=1}^k.
\end{equation}
Offline knowledge distillation trains the weights $\theta$ over a dataset $D_{kd} = \{(\mathbf{t}, \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{c}), \dots\}$ rolled out in a driving simulator~\cite{caesar2021nuplan,dosovitskiy2017carla} or world model~\cite{yang2024genad,gao2024vista}:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:objective-score}
  \argmin_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\sim D_{kd}} \left[\mathcal{L}_{kd}\big(e(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c}), \pi_\theta(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big)\right],
\end{equation}
using binary cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{kd}$ on $(0,1)$-scaled sub-scores. In Hydra-MDP, the teacher $e$ evaluates the rule-based NAVSIM sub-scores (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:setup}) alongside a human imitation loss (Section~\ref{centaur:app:scoring}), so the adapted decoder heads approximate rule-based metrics distilled offline.

\subsection{Cluster Entropy}\label{centaur:sec:ce}

While Eqs.~\eqref{centaur:eq:multi-score-output}--\eqref{centaur:eq:cost-select} resemble $k$-way classification, minimising raw $k$-way Shannon entropy over-penalises dense vocabulary neighbourhoods around common manoeuvres such as cruising straight. Cluster Entropy avoids this density artefact in three stages without requiring teacher $e$ at deployment.

\paragraph{Trajectory candidates.} Offline, we draw a fixed subset of $M \ll k$ candidates $\{\mathbf{t}_m\}_{m=1}^M$ weighted by their mean expert score $e$ across \texttt{navtrain} (Section~\ref{centaur:app:entropy}), which under-represents rare manoeuvres. Computing Shannon entropy directly over these $M$ scores defines our Full Entropy baseline.

\paragraph{Direction-based sampling.} From the $M$ sampled candidates, Centaur defines five directional anchors by endpoint lateral offset inspired by DriveLM's five steering categories~\cite{sima2024drivelm} (Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm}): \texttt{sharp left} and \texttt{sharp right} (most extreme left and right lateral endpoint displacements), \texttt{slight left} and \texttt{slight right} (closest to half of those extremes), and \texttt{forward} (minimal lateral displacement). Because longitudinal speed bins are omitted, braking and cruising along the same lane share a directional bucket, leaving stop-or-go ambiguity unrepresented (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Score aggregation.} Assigning each of the $M$ candidates to its closest anchor in Euclidean ($L_2$) waypoint distance partitions the subset into five directional clusters $\mathcal{C}_1, \dots, \mathcal{C}_5$ (Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:pipeline}, top). Summing post-sigmoid positive composite scores $\phi(s_m) > 0$ within each cluster $\mathcal{C}_a$ yields unnormalised cluster mass $\mathit{score}_a = \sum_{m:\,\mathbf{t}_m \in \mathcal{C}_a} \phi(s_m)$ and Shannon entropy $H(\mathbf{x}) \in [0, \ln 5]$ (with $0\log 0 = 0$):
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:ce}
  p_a = \frac{\mathit{score}_a}{\sum_{a'=1}^{5} \mathit{score}_{a'}}, \qquad
  H(\mathbf{x}) = -\sum_{a=1}^{5} p_a \log p_a .
\end{equation}
Because offline cluster cardinalities are unequal, summing within clusters retains a mild density bias whose exact size distribution was not separately audited.

\subsection{Test-Time Training}\label{centaur:sec:ttt}

Following single-step TTT~\cite{Gao2024fstta}, Centaur evaluates $H(\mathbf{x}_r;\theta)$ at the base decoder weights $\theta$ on each frame, backpropagates buffered gradients $g_r = \nabla_\theta H(\mathbf{x}_r;\theta)$, and averages the last $F=4$ gradients in a ring buffer (Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:pipeline}, bottom) to take a single step of size $\eta$ from the base decoder weights $\theta$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:ttt}
  \hat{\theta}_i = \theta - \eta \left\{\frac{\partial H}{\partial \theta}\right\}_{avg},
\end{equation}
dampening isolated high-scoring outliers in weakly supported directions (Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:teaser}), though entropy minimisation can reinforce an erroneous dominant cluster when the base policy is confidently wrong (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:discussion}). Algorithm~\ref{centaur:alg:ttt} details the update loop.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \DontPrintSemicolon
  \caption{Test-time training in Centaur}\label{centaur:alg:ttt}
  \KwIn{pre-trained scoring policy $\pi_\theta$ with adaptable decoder weights $\theta$ (perception backbones frozen); vocabulary $\{\mathbf{t}_j\}_{j=1}^{k}$; $M$ sampled candidates partitioned around five directional anchors (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:ce}); gradient buffer size $F$; step size $\eta$}
  \KwOut{selected plan $\hat{\mathbf{t}}_0$ at each evaluation step}
  $\mathcal{B} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \tcp*{FIFO ring buffer of past gradients}
  \ForEach{evaluation step $i$ with sensor observation $\mathbf{x}_0$ and route command $\mathbf{c}_0$}{
    \eIf{$\mathcal{B} \neq \emptyset$}{
      $\hat{\theta}_i \leftarrow \theta - \eta \left\{\frac{\partial H}{\partial \theta}\right\}_{avg}$ using the mean gradient across $\mathcal{B}$ \tcp*{Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:ttt}}
    }{
      $\hat{\theta}_i \leftarrow \theta$\;
    }
    Evaluate vocabulary scores under $\pi_{\hat{\theta}_i}$ and pick $\hat{\mathbf{t}}_0$ \tcp*{Eqs.~\eqref{centaur:eq:multi-score-output}--\eqref{centaur:eq:cost-select}}
    \tcp{can execute asynchronously on parallel compute}
    Evaluate Cluster Entropy $H(\mathbf{x}_0;\theta)$ over the $M$ candidate scores at base weights $\theta$ \tcp*{Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:ce}}
    Backpropagate $g_i = \nabla_\theta H(\mathbf{x}_0;\theta)$ at $\theta$ and append to $\mathcal{B}$, retaining at most $F$ recent steps\;
  }
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Operational semantics and deployment.} Algorithm~\ref{centaur:alg:ttt} defines an episodic reset rule with four properties. First, every step $i$ resets to the base decoder weights $\theta$ before applying Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:ttt}, preventing weight drift across long sequences and limiting memory to the $F=4$ buffered gradients. Second, step $i$ depends only on the four preceding evaluation steps ($\hat{\theta}_1 = \theta$), which in NAVSIM come from logged human states rather than states visited by the planner's own past actions. Third, the ring buffer $\mathcal{B}$ does not reset across clips. Fourth, adaptation modifies only the score decoder parameters $\theta$ while visual and LiDAR backbones remain frozen. Because $\mathcal{B}$ consumes only past steps, backpropagation can execute asynchronously alongside inference, though our current benchmarks run it sequentially (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:latency}).

\section{\texorpdfstring{\texttt{navsafe}}{navsafe}: A Safety-Critical Scenario Set}\label{centaur:sec:navsafe}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{ch-centaur/images/navsafe_v12.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Curation workflow of \texttt{navsafe}.} \texttt{navtest} clips are ranked by Hydra-MDP PDMS (Step~0), inspected by human annotators (Step~1), and grouped into ten scenario classes (Steps~2--4) guided by the NHTSA pre-crash typology~\cite{najm2007pre} (top-left inset), with a qualitative 2D OpenCLIP~\cite{ilharco2021openclip} projection of \texttt{navtest} (blue) and \texttt{navsafe} (red) frames at top right.}
  \label{centaur:fig:navsafe}
\end{figure}

To probe planner behaviour on rare hazards, we curate \texttt{navsafe}, an internal 229-frame diagnostic subset of \texttt{navtest} drawn from NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim}, OpenScene~\cite{sima2023openscene} (Chapter~\ref{ch:occnet}), and nuPlan~\cite{caesar2021nuplan}.

\paragraph{NAVSIM and construction.} NAVSIM filters nuPlan logs for dynamic manoeuvres, comprising 1,192 training logs (\texttt{navtrain}, 103,288 frames) and 136 test logs (\texttt{navtest}, 12,146 frames), where policies map 1.5 seconds (4 frames) of multi-camera, LiDAR, and ego history to a 4-second plan. Guided by the NHTSA pre-crash typology~\cite{najm2007pre,thorn2018framework} (whose five most prevalent categories are ``Lead Vehicle Stopped'', ``Control Loss Without Prior Vehicle Action'', ``Vehicle(s) Turning at Non-Signalized Junctions'', ``Lead Vehicle Decelerating'', and ``Road Edge Departure Without Prior Vehicle Maneuver''), we follow a five-step loop:
\begin{enumerate}[label={Step~\arabic*.}, start=0, leftmargin=*]
  \item Rank \texttt{navtest} clips by the mean PDMS of unadapted Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp}.
  \item Manually inspect low-scoring clips and assign frames to \texttt{navsafe} categories.
  \item Add verified frames to the corresponding scenario buckets.
  \item Consult the NHTSA typology to introduce uncovered scenario buckets.
  \item Re-rank the remaining \texttt{navtest} clips and repeat.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Categories and methodological caveats.} The 229 frames span 10 diagnostic categories: ``Roundabout'', ``Yellow light, rush or wait?'', ``Exit ramp'', ``Unprotected left'', ``Enter ramp'', ``Uncommon traffic sign'', ``Overtaking with lane change'', ``No lane area (\eg parking lot)'', ``Bad weather'', and ``Yielding (\eg to pedestrians)''. Three caveats govern interpretation: ranking clips by Hydra-MDP PDMS in Step~0 induces regression to the mean in favour of modified Hydra-MDP variants without an independent seed control; \texttt{navsafe} samples the tail of \texttt{navtest} rather than an unseen domain (with qualitative 2D OpenCLIP~\cite{ilharco2021openclip} periphery in Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:navsafe}); and human category assignments lack a formal inter-annotator agreement study.

\section{Experiments}\label{centaur:sec:exp}

We evaluate Centaur on NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim}: full \texttt{navtest}, the multi-seed leaderboard, and the 229-frame \texttt{navsafe} subset (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:navsafe}).

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{centaur:sec:setup}

\paragraph{Metrics and replay scope.} Under NAVSIM v1 non-reactive log replay, each 4-second plan is scored by the Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS):
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:pdms}
  \mathrm{PDMS} = 100 \cdot \mathrm{NC} \cdot \mathrm{DAC} \cdot \frac{5\,\mathrm{TTC} + 2\,\mathrm{C} + 5\,\mathrm{EP}}{12},
\end{equation}
combining No at-fault Collision (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), 1-second Time-to-Collision (TTC), Comfort (C), and Ego Progress (EP), where $\mathrm{NC}, \mathrm{DAC}, \mathrm{TTC}, \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{EP} \in [0,1]$ in the evaluator product and are displayed as percentages ($\times 100$) in tables~\cite{dauner2024navsim}. Resetting each frame to the human state precludes closed-loop shift or drift (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:discussion}), and logged human PDMS is a reference rather than a strict bound (EP is normalised by PDM-Closed~\cite{dauner2023parting}).

\paragraph{Base model and deployment.} Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp} fuses ResNet34~\cite{he2016deep} BEV LiDAR and \mbox{V2-99}~\cite{park2021pseudo} camera features via TransFuser blocks~\cite{chitta2023transfuser}, scoring $k=8192$ trajectories across five distilled PDMS sub-score heads (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:prelim}); we also test ResNet34 TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser} in Sections~\ref{centaur:sec:ablations} and~\ref{centaur:app:ablations}. Our prior-trajectory fallback caches the top 20 of $k=8192$ trajectories by \texttt{navtrain} PDMS and substitutes the nearest ($L_2$) prior whenever uncertainty exceeds a Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class} cutoff, without the feasibility checks or PDM-Closed~\cite{dauner2023parting} rules of SafetyNet~\cite{vitelli2022safetynet}.

\paragraph{Uncertainty measures and implementation.} Alongside Cluster Entropy, we test pairwise inter-head \textbf{KL Divergence} over $k=8192$ trajectories, \textbf{Full Entropy}~\cite{shannon1948entropy} over $M=100$ candidates, and \textbf{Semantic Entropy} (\textbf{Hydra-SE}) clustering $M=100$ candidates in score space~\cite{kuhn2023semantic} (Section~\ref{centaur:app:entropy}). Single-seed ablations (Tables~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation} and~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}) use NAVSIM v1.0; the three-seed leaderboard (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all}) uses NAVSIM v1.1. Hydra-MDP trains on \texttt{navtrain} for 20 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs (batch 256, AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} lr $10^{-4}$). TTT uses $M=100$, $F=4$, and $\eta = 10^{-4}$ on the decoder ($\eta, M, F, \tau$ tuned on \texttt{navtest}; Section~\ref{centaur:app:exp}).

\paragraph{Regression planners and explicit representations.} Adding an evidential head (Section~\ref{centaur:app:regression}) to TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser} with Semantic Entropy (TransFuser-SE) lifts single-seed NAVSIM v1.0 PDMS from 83.4 to 85.7 (+2.3; Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}), compared with 84.2 under direct evidential TTT and roughly 60 (EP $<15$) under the prior fallback (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}; audited in Section~\ref{centaur:app:consistency}). \paragraph{Gradient buffer and backbone.} In Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}, singular-value decomposition (SVD) gradient projection~\cite{Gao2024fstta} on TransFuser-SE scores 83.8 vs.\ 85.7 for uniform averaging; sweeping $F \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ on Hydra-SE yields 91.4, 91.3, 91.5, and 91.8 PDMS (a 0.5-point spread above Hydra-MDP's 90.3); and replacing \mbox{V2-99} with ViT-L in Hydra-MDP gives 89.9 vs.\ 90.3 unadapted, 90.9 vs.\ 91.5 under KL TTT, and 67.4 vs.\ 63.1 under KL fallback (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:results}).

\subsection{Computational Cost}\label{centaur:sec:latency}

On one A100 GPU (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}), unadapted Hydra-MDP runs at 4.1\,Hz (243.9\,ms per frame) versus 103.1\,ms for ResNet-34 TransFuser (roughly 7 PDMS lower). Synchronous TTT runs at 2.8--3.2\,Hz (312.5--357.1\,ms on one A100, with Cluster Entropy's exact overhead matching the 100-candidate entropy pass), above NAVSIM's 2\,Hz rate (4 frames in 1.5\,s). Asynchronous updates (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:ttt}) or uncertainty-gated $\text{Hydra-SE}^{\mathbf{4}}$ (3.6\,Hz / 279.6\,ms at 91.3 PDMS) reduce this overhead.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{centaur:sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Evidence and fallback comparison.} Under NAVSIM v1 non-reactive replay, TTT lifts single-seed Hydra-MDP from 90.3 to 92.6 PDMS (TTC 93.9 to 98.0, EP 86.5 to 85.9), reaches 92.10 $\pm$ 0.33 across three v1.1 seeds (without a matched three-seed Hydra-MDP baseline), and adds 0.8 to 2.3 PDMS across base planners (or 0.4 with SVD gradient projection; Tables~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation}, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}, and~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}) using offline rule-distilled heads. Whereas our 20-prior fallback cut EP below 15 (PDMS 63.1--65.9 on Hydra-MDP and 59.9--67.4 across all tested planners, with mismatched trigger rates in Section~\ref{centaur:sec:results}) and UniAD's cost optimiser reduced all sub-scores, full rule verifiers like SafetyNet~\cite{vitelli2022safetynet} remain untested here. On \texttt{navsafe}, Centaur's lead over Hydra-MDP (17.67 points in preprint Overall, 68.9 \vs 49.4 frame-weighted, and 72.0 \vs 48.3 simple mean in Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navsafe_overall}) is confounded by Hydra-MDP selection bias (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:navsafe}) and accompanied by a drop on YLD (71.6 to 59.3).

\paragraph{Cluster Entropy and failure detection.} Across three v1.1 seeds, Centaur leads Hydra-SE by 0.23 PDMS (92.10 $\pm$ 0.33 \vs 91.87 $\pm$ 0.19 in Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all}), within inter-seed variance, and all four objectives in Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation} gain 1.2 to 2.3 PDMS; thus the primary gain comes from generic test-time score sharpening, while Cluster Entropy provides directional interpretability (Figs.~\ref{centaur:fig:teaser} and~\ref{centaur:fig:vis}). As a failure detector (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}), Cluster Entropy's 62.8\% TPR comes with 73.6\% accuracy (below the 84.4\% Select None baseline at a 15.6\% non-zero failure rate) and no AUROC/AUPRC curves (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:failure}).

\paragraph{Threats to validity.} Four threats qualify our findings:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Evaluation protocol:} NAVSIM v1 resets each frame to logged human states with non-reactive traffic and Episodic weight resets to $\theta$ (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:ttt}), leaving closed-loop compounding errors, oscillation, and continual weight drift untested; hyper-parameters and \texttt{navsafe} were also selected on \texttt{navtest} without multi-seed confidence bounds on v1.0 ablations.
  \item \textbf{Confounding mechanisms:} Single-step TTT ($\eta=10^{-4}$) could reflect generic score re-calibration of heads distilled from NC, DAC, EP, C, and TTC on pre-filtered \texttt{navtrain} candidates (Section~\ref{centaur:app:entropy}); static pre-test sharpening, unrelated-clip buffers, and independent NAVSIM v2~\cite{cao2025pseudo} metrics were not tested.
  \item \textbf{Objective expressivity:} Lateral-only anchors cannot represent longitudinal stop-or-go uncertainty (YLD, YLLT, and NHTSA ``Lead Vehicle Stopped'', where Centaur drops from 71.6 to 59.3 on YLD): while Cluster Entropy groups candidates by full-trajectory Euclidean $L_2$ distance around five endpoint lateral anchors (conflating stopping and cruising along the same path), Semantic Entropy (Hydra-SE, which reaches 84.6 on YLD) clusters in the 5D predicted sub-score space where EP and TTC separate stopping from cruising, and entropy minimisation can reinforce confident errors~\cite{wang2022cotta} (Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:vis}).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{navsafe} curation:} Sorting \texttt{navtest} by Hydra-MDP PDMS induces regression to the mean without an independent seed control, five of ten categories have 5--12 frames without inter-annotator agreement, and Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navsafe_overall} audits the preprint Overall column of Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navsafe}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.} Future work must test closed-loop simulators, NAVSIM v2~\cite{cao2025pseudo}, and vehicle hardware under asynchronous compute (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:latency}), address regressions on YLD (59.3 \vs 71.6 Hydra-MDP, 84.6 Hydra-SE, 61.6 TransFuser), BWTH (60.2 \vs 64.4 Hydra-SE, 62.8 LTF), YLLT (65.0 \vs 70.2 LTF), and \texttt{navtest} EP (85.9 \vs 86.5), and extend adaptation beyond frozen perception backbones (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}).

\subsection{Related Literature and Methodological Formulations}\label{centaur:app:scoring}

\paragraph{Context and uncertainty formulations.} Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp} pairs TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser} with rule-based PDM~\cite{dauner2023parting} distillation; unlike model ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple,li2024perceptionuncertainties}, Centaur updates decoder parameters online via TTT. Semantic Entropy follows paraphrase clustering in language models~\cite{kuhn2023semantic} by grouping trajectories in feature space prior to computing entropy. For scenario evaluation, whereas open-loop ADE/FDE~\cite{hu2023uniad,jiang2023vad,li2024ego} omits rule compliance, NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim} scores non-reactive replay, Bench2Drive~\cite{jia2024bench2drive} evaluates 44 closed-loop scenario families, InterPlan~\cite{hallgarten2024interplan} synthesises rare hazards, and \texttt{navsafe} isolates 229 low-scoring \texttt{navtest} frames (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:navsafe}). For continuous regression policies, Deep Evidential Regression~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} predicts target coordinates alongside conjugate prior parameters in a single forward pass. Table~\ref{centaur:tab:notation} summarises the notation used across Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}.

\paragraph{Evidential regression planner.} Deep Evidential Regression~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} predicts Normal Inverse Gamma (NIG) parameters ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\upsilon$) for each scalar target $y_i$ of $\pi_\theta$:
\begin{align}
  (y_1, \dots, y_N) &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2), \nonumber \\
  \mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\gamma, \sigma^2 \upsilon^{-1}), \quad & \qquad \sigma^2 \sim \operatorname{InvGamma}(\alpha, \beta),
  \label{centaur:eq:evidential}
\end{align}
with Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\cdot)$, inverse-gamma distribution $\operatorname{InvGamma}(\alpha,\beta)$, predictive mean $(\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_N) = \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0) = \hat{\mathbf{t}}_0$, and imitation loss $\mathcal{L}_{im}$ defined as the Negative Log-Likelihood of the marginal Student-$t$ distribution plus the evidence regulariser of Amini \etal~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} over $(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{c}) \in D_{im}$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:objective}
  \argmin_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{c})\sim D_{im}} \left[\mathcal{L}_{im}\big(\mathbf{t}, \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big)\right].
\end{equation}
For an $N$-step vector trajectory $\vec{\mathbf{t}} = (\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_N)$ parameterised by $(\vec{\alpha}, \vec{\beta}, \vec{\gamma}, \vec{\upsilon})$:
\begin{align}
  (\vec{\mathbf{t}}_1, \dots, \vec{\mathbf{t}}_N) &\sim \mathcal{N}(\vec{\mu}, \vec{\sigma^2}), \nonumber \\
  \vec{\mu} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vec{\gamma}, \vec{\sigma^2 \upsilon^{-1}}), \quad & \qquad \vec{\sigma^2} \sim \operatorname{InvGamma}(\vec{\alpha}, \vec{\beta}),
  \label{centaur:eq:evidential-vector}
\end{align}
with mean plan $\vec{\gamma} = \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0) = \hat{\mathbf{t}}_0$ and vectorised imitation objective over $(\mathbf{x},\vec{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{c}) \in D_{im}$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:objective-imi-kd}
  \argmin_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},\vec{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{c})\sim D_{im}} \left[\mathcal{L}_{im}\big(\vec{\mathbf{t}}, \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big)\right].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Scoring objective and Semantic Entropy.} Following VADv2~\cite{chen2024vadv2}, Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp} combines the multi-target distillation loss $\mathcal{L}_{kd} = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \mathcal{L}_{kd}\big(e(\mathbf{t}_j,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c}), \allowbreak \pi_\theta(\mathbf{t}_j,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big)$ (Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:objective-score}) and human imitation regularisation $\mathcal{L}_{im}$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:multi-score}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{im} + \mathcal{L}_{kd},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{im}$ is the vocabulary-level cross-entropy~\cite{li2024hydramdp} over the $k$ candidates $\{\mathbf{t}_j\}_{j=1}^{k}$ with target distribution given by softmax-normalised negative $L_2$ distances to the logged trajectory $\tilde{\mathbf{t}}$. For entropy estimation, $M=100$ fixed candidates are sampled offline from the $8192$ vocabulary weighted by mean \texttt{navtrain} PDMS (Algorithm~\ref{centaur:alg:ttt}) around five lateral DriveLM~\cite{sima2024drivelm} anchors (Sections~\ref{centaur:sec:ce} and~\ref{centaur:sec:discussion}). Semantic Entropy assigns each of the $M$ candidates to the closest of the five anchors by feature-space $L_2$ distance $D(\cdot, \cdot)$ when $D \le \tau$ (falling back to trajectory-space $L_2$ distance if $D > \tau$) before computing Shannon entropy $H$: feature vectors are predicted sub-scores for scoring policies, or 2D pairs $\{L_{\gamma}, L_{\mu}\}$ ($L_2$ distances to $\gamma$ and the closest of $N$ samples $\mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\gamma, \sigma^2 \upsilon^{-1})$ with $\sigma^2 \sim \operatorname{InvGamma}(\alpha, \beta)$) for evidential regression policies.

\subsection{Hyper-Parameters, Baselines, and Extended Ablations}\label{centaur:app:exp}\label{centaur:app:baselines}

\paragraph{Implementation and baseline design.} Hydra-MDP evaluates $8192$ candidate trajectories~\cite{li2024hydramdp}, whereas TransFuser regresses $40$ waypoints at $10$\,Hz ($4$\,s horizon) with an evidential head~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} trained on one A100 GPU (batch size $64$, learning rate $10^{-4}$), using $\tau=0.06$ for Hydra-SE and $\tau=0.02$ for TransFuser-SE tuned on \texttt{navtest} (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:setup}). Three uncertainty and deployment baselines complement Cluster Entropy: (i) \textbf{KL Divergence}, motivated by inter-head sub-score divergence (particularly between NC and DAC) on failed versus successful scenes (Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:vis_group}); (ii) \textbf{Evidential uncertainty}, providing single-pass epistemic and aleatoric estimates for continuous regression in TransFuser; and (iii) \textbf{Prior fallback}, which flags frames exceeding the Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class} cutoff and selects the nearest ($L_2$) trajectory among $20$ \texttt{navtrain} priors without explicit perceptual maps (audited in Section~\ref{centaur:app:consistency}).

\subsection{Arithmetic Consistency Audits}

We audit internal arithmetic across Tables~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation}, \ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}, \ref{centaur:tab:navsafe}, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}, and~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class_supp}.

\paragraph{Failure base rate and fallback trigger proportions.} Because $\mathrm{PDMS} \in [0, 100]$, a $15.6\%$ fraction of strict $\mathrm{PDMS}=0$ frames (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}) would bound mean Hydra-MDP PDMS at $84.4 < 90.3$ (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation}), while Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:pdms} with $98.4$ NC and $97.8$ DAC bounds zero-PDMS frames at $1.6\% + 2.2\% = 3.8\%$ (for TransFuser, $24.1\%$ bounds PDMS at $75.9 < 83.4$, and $97.1$ NC with $91.9$ DAC allows at most $2.9\% + 8.1\% = 11.0\%$). Furthermore, given base rate $p$, $\mathrm{TPR}$, and accuracy $a$, the flagged fraction $1 - p - a + 2p\,\mathrm{TPR}$ and false-positive rate $(1 - p - a + p\,\mathrm{TPR})/(1 - p)$ imply $29.0\%$--$33.3\%$ triggered frames in Table~\ref{centaur:tab:implied_rates} (Sections~\ref{centaur:sec:setup} and~\ref{centaur:app:baselines}), which bounds Hydra-MDP EP at $\ge 53.2$ against the reported $8.2$--$13.2$ ($73.3\%$--$78.3\%$ trigger rates required; reported PDMS $63.1, 64.2, 65.3, 65.9$ also increases with flagging rate). Similarly, TransFuser-SE at threshold $0.8$ flags $29.8\%$ of frames, bounding EP at $49.5$ versus the reported $14.6$.

\section{Chapter Summary}\label{centaur:sec:summary}

Addressing RQ3a on per-frame logged states with offline rule-distilled heads, this chapter introduced Centaur, which resets decoder weights each step and applies one buffered ($F=4$) gradient step on Cluster Entropy across five lateral directions, alongside the 229-frame NHTSA-motivated \texttt{navsafe} subset of \texttt{navtest}.

Under NAVSIM v1 non-reactive replay, Centaur lifts single-seed Hydra-MDP from 90.3 to 92.6 PDMS (TTC 93.9 to 98.0, EP 86.5 to 85.9) while prior fallback drops PDMS to 63.1--65.9 on Hydra-MDP (59.9--67.4 across all tested planners, EP $<15$), and places first on the three-seed v1.1 leaderboard at 92.10 $\pm$ 0.33 PDMS (0.23 ahead of Hydra-SE, with +1.2 to +2.3 PDMS across the four Hydra-MDP objectives, +0.8 to +2.3 across base planners, or +0.4 with SVD gradient projection, and +2.3 on TransFuser-SE, whereas UniAD cost refinement drops PDMS from 83.4 to 75.2). At a fixed failure cutoff, Cluster Entropy reaches 62.8\% TPR and 73.6\% accuracy (vs.\ 84.4\% Select None at 15.6\% base rate). On \texttt{navsafe}, Centaur leads on frame-weighted (68.9 \vs 49.4), simple (72.0 \vs 48.3), and preprint Overall (74.14 \vs 56.47) means (Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navsafe_overall}), but drops from 71.6 to 59.3 on yielding and trails human logs (94.65). Open-loop resets, validation-free tuning, metric-distilled calibration, and lateral-only clustering remain key limitations. Chapter~\ref{ch:kai0} next examines robotic manipulation under noisy demonstrations and hardware shift.
