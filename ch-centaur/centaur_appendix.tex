\section{Additional Related Work}\label{centaur:app:related}

\paragraph{Hydra-MDP and test-time compute.} Our planner builds on Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp}, a trajectory-scoring method that pairs a multimodal TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser} perception backbone with multi-teacher knowledge distillation, drawing supervision from rule-based teachers such as the PDM planner~\cite{dauner2023parting}. Apart from model ensembles~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple,li2024perceptionuncertainties}, allocating extra computation at inference time has received limited attention in autonomous driving; Centaur instead updates planner weights online through test-time training.

\paragraph{Semantic entropy.} The Semantic Entropy baseline draws on semantic uncertainty estimation for large language models (LLMs)~\cite{kuhn2023semantic}. Standard token-level entropy conflates paraphrased outputs that share the same meaning; grouping equivalent responses into clusters before computing entropy isolates uncertainty across distinct semantic interpretations. Here, we apply an analogous clustering step to candidate vehicle trajectories and compute entropy over the resulting cluster distribution.

\paragraph{Safety-critical scenarios.} Evaluating end-to-end driving policies faithfully is non-trivial. Early studies relied heavily on open-loop spatial error metrics such as average displacement error (ADE) and final displacement error (FDE)~\cite{hu2023uniad,jiang2023vad,hu2023gaia}, but empirical audits~\cite{li2024ego} have demonstrated that open-loop coordinate errors correlate weakly with closed-loop driving safety. Consequently, recent benchmarks such as NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim} evaluate multi-second plans in non-reactive log replay by combining collision avoidance, drivable-area compliance, ego progress, and passenger comfort. Other recent suites target closed-loop or out-of-distribution settings: Bench2Drive~\cite{jia2024bench2drive} evaluates neural policies across 44 interactive scenario families in closed loop, while InterPlan~\cite{hallgarten2024interplan} synthesises rare road hazards (such as lane-blocking crash scenes) and invokes large language models to adjust rule-based planner hyper-parameters online. Complementing these efforts, \texttt{navsafe} curates a 229-frame diagnostic subset of \texttt{navtest} by ranking clips according to Hydra-MDP's baseline performance to isolate rare planning failures on recorded real-world sensor logs (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:navsafe}).

\paragraph{Evidential uncertainty.} Deep Evidential Regression~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} trains a deterministic neural network to output both continuous point predictions and higher-order distributional parameters in a single forward pass. By placing an evidential prior over the likelihood parameters, the network separates data noise (aleatoric uncertainty) from model ignorance (epistemic uncertainty) without requiring test-time sampling or out-of-distribution (OOD) training examples, while an evidence penalty discourages high confidence on large prediction errors. In Section~\ref{centaur:app:regression}, we incorporate this evidential loss into TransFuser~\cite{chitta2023transfuser} to test online adaptation on a regression planner.

\subsection{Trajectory Regression Planner}\label{centaur:app:regression}

To obtain a closed-form uncertainty estimate from a continuous regression architecture without Monte Carlo dropout or multi-model ensembling, we attach a Deep Evidential Regression layer~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} to the waypoint decoder of $\pi_\theta$. Instead of outputting a bare coordinate $y_i$ at each horizon step, the network predicts four conjugate prior hyper-parameters ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, and $\upsilon$) governing a Normal Inverse Gamma (NIG) density:
\begin{align}
  (y_1, \dots, y_N) &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2), \nonumber \\
  \mu \sim \mathcal{N}(\gamma, \sigma^2 \upsilon^{-1}), \quad & \qquad \sigma^2 \sim \Gamma^{-1}(\alpha, \beta),
  \label{centaur:eq:evidential}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{N}(\cdot)$ represents a univariate normal density and $\Gamma(\cdot)$ is the standard gamma function appearing in the Inverse-Gamma variance prior. When executing a plan at test time, the predictive mean $\gamma$ defines the chosen trajectory $(\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_N) = \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0) = \hat{\mathbf{t}}_0$. During offline training, the policy weights $\theta$ are optimised by minimising an imitation criterion over logged human demonstrations:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:objective}
  \argmin_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{c})\sim D_{im}} \left[\mathcal{L}_{im}\big(\mathbf{t}, \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big)\right],
\end{equation}
with expert driving sequences $D_{im}$ consisting of tuples $(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t},\mathbf{c})$ and $\mathcal{L}_{im}$ combining the marginal Student-$t$ negative log-likelihood of the NIG prior with the evidence penalty of Amini \etal~\cite{amini2020deepevidential}.

Because an ego plan across $N$ future steps forms a multi-dimensional waypoint sequence $\vec{\mathbf{t}}$ with waypoints $(\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_N)$, the regression head outputs vector-valued NIG hyper-parameters $(\vec{\alpha}, \vec{\beta}, \vec{\gamma}, \vec{\upsilon})$ across all waypoint coordinates simultaneously:
\begin{align}
  (\vec{\mathbf{t}}_1, \dots, \vec{\mathbf{t}}_N) &\sim \mathcal{N}(\vec{\mu}, \vec{\sigma^2}), \nonumber \\
  \vec{\mu} \sim \mathcal{N}(\vec{\gamma}, \vec{\sigma^2 \upsilon^{-1}}), \quad & \qquad \vec{\sigma^2} \sim \Gamma^{-1}(\vec{\alpha}, \vec{\beta}),
  \label{centaur:eq:evidential-vector}
\end{align}
retaining the distributional notation from Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:evidential}. Using the predictive location vector $\vec{\gamma} = \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x}_0) = \hat{\mathbf{t}}_0$ as the output plan gives the vectorised training objective
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:objective-imi-kd}
  \argmin_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},\vec{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{c})\sim D_{im}} \left[\mathcal{L}_{im}\big(\vec{\mathbf{t}}, \pi_\theta(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big)\right],
\end{equation}
minimised across the demonstration corpus $D_{im}$ of expert tuples $(\mathbf{x},\vec{\mathbf{t}},\mathbf{c})$ under the vectorised evidential loss $\mathcal{L}_{im}$~\cite{amini2020deepevidential}.

\subsection{Trajectory Scoring Planner}\label{centaur:app:scoring}

In line with VADv2~\cite{chen2024vadv2}, our training setup for Hydra-MDP~\cite{li2024hydramdp} combines the score-distillation objective $\mathcal{L}_{kd}$ from Eq.~\eqref{centaur:eq:objective-score} with a human-similarity term $\mathcal{L}_{im}$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{centaur:eq:multi-score}
  \mathcal{L} = \sum_j \mathcal{L}_{im}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{t}}, \pi_\theta(\mathbf{t}_j,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big) + \mathcal{L}_{kd}\big(e(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c}), \pi_\theta(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{c})\big),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{im}$ supervises each vocabulary query via cross-entropy~\cite{li2024hydramdp} against soft target weights formed by taking a softmax over the negative Euclidean distances to the logged human demonstration.

\subsection{Entropy Estimation}\label{centaur:app:entropy}

\paragraph{Candidate and anchor selection.} Prior to testing, we draw $M=100$ candidate trajectories from the 8192-element vocabulary using probability weights proportional to each trajectory's mean ground-truth PDMS on \texttt{navtrain} (matching the prior selection in our fallback baseline). This 100-trajectory subset is drawn once and shared across every evaluation step (Algorithm~\ref{centaur:alg:ttt}), although weighting by \texttt{navtrain} averages under-samples rare manoeuvres. Within those $M=100$ candidates, we designate five directional anchors following the steering taxonomy in Section~\ref{centaur:sec:ce} and DriveLM~\cite{sima2024drivelm}. Using fixed directional anchors avoids iterative k-means updates, preserves differentiability with negligible compute overhead, and guarantees coverage across lateral steering angles, though longitudinal speed variations are not separated (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Semantic Entropy.} When a planner assigns similar outcome profiles to most candidates, its decision uncertainty is lower than when candidate profiles diverge widely. Semantic Entropy captures this by grouping the $M$ candidates around the five anchors using Euclidean ($L_2$) distance in predicted feature space rather than spatial waypoint coordinates. For scoring architectures, each candidate's feature vector is simply its predicted sub-score tuple. For regression architectures, we form a two-component feature vector $\{L_{\gamma}, L_{\mu}\}$ from the NIG outputs $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \upsilon)$: after drawing $N$ variance samples $\sigma$ from $\Gamma^{-1}(\alpha, \beta)$ and location samples $\mu$ from $\mathcal{N}(\gamma, \sigma^2 \upsilon^{-1})$, we compute for each candidate the Euclidean distance $L_{\gamma}$ to $\gamma$ and the minimum Euclidean distance $L_{\mu}$ across the $N$ sampled $\mu$ paths. Mapping every candidate to its closest anchor in this feature space produces a five-cluster distribution whose Shannon entropy defines $H$.

\paragraph{Clustering threshold for Semantic Entropy.} Grouping candidates around anchors in Semantic Entropy uses the $L_2$ metric $D(\cdot, \cdot)$ in feature space together with a distance cap $\tau$. In a single assignment pass, each candidate joins the cluster of its nearest anchor in feature space provided their feature distance is at most $\tau$; otherwise, the candidate falls back to the anchor closest in spatial waypoint $L_2$ distance. In practice, we pick $\tau$ large enough that almost all candidates are assigned directly in feature space.

\section{Experimental Details}\label{centaur:app:exp}

\paragraph{Hyper-parameters.} Every Hydra-MDP run uses the 8192-candidate vocabulary of~\cite{li2024hydramdp}, while every TransFuser run attaches an evidential head~\cite{amini2020deepevidential} to predict 40 waypoints $(x, y, \text{heading})$ at 10\,Hz over the 4\,s horizon. Semantic Entropy uses $\tau=0.06$ on Hydra-SE and $\tau=0.02$ on TransFuser-SE, chosen on \texttt{navtest} like the other TTT settings (Section~\ref{centaur:sec:setup}). TransFuser training runs on one A100 GPU with batch size 64 and learning rate $10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Motivation of Baselines}\label{centaur:app:baselines}

Our baseline uncertainty measures and deployment rules were chosen for the following reasons:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{KL Divergence for trajectory scoring.} Comparing predicted sub-score distributions between successful and failed scenes shows mismatch across sub-metrics, most visibly between NC and DAC in failure scenes (Fig.~\ref{centaur:fig:vis_group}). We therefore test whether the KL divergence between sub-score distributions can flag impending failures.
  \item \textbf{Evidential uncertainty for trajectory regression.} Deep evidential regression is a standard single-pass uncertainty estimator for continuous regression tasks, making it a natural baseline for guiding TTT on TransFuser.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty-triggered fallback to prior trajectories.} Industrial driving stacks routinely pair learned planners with fallback mechanisms, and a trajectory-selection fallback (unlike post-hoc trajectory optimisation) requires no explicit occupancy or cost map. Our baseline is a deliberately simplified rule: whenever estimated uncertainty crosses the classification threshold of Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}, the planner output is replaced by the closest trajectory (in $L_2$ distance) from a fixed 20-trajectory prior bank, rather than invoking a feasibility check or a rule-based backup controller such as SafetyNet~\cite{vitelli2022safetynet}. It should therefore not be interpreted as an evaluation of SafetyNet or general rule-based safeguards. Concretely, deployment proceeds in three steps:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Evaluate the base end-to-end policy on every test frame.
    \item Apply the uncertainty detector and threshold from Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class} to flag suspected failure frames.
    \item Substitute the nearest prior trajectory on all flagged frames.
  \end{itemize}
  As shown in Section~\ref{centaur:app:consistency}, the flagging rates implied by Table~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class} cannot produce the low ego-progress values reported for this fallback.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Latency.} All per-frame timing measurements were collected on a single workstation equipped with one NVIDIA A100 GPU (driver 535.54.03), a 16-core AMD EPYC 9124 CPU, and 500\,GB of system memory. For unmodified baselines, latency reflects a single forward inference pass; for deployment-adapted variants, the measurement adds either the backward pass and decoder weight step (under TTT) or the Euclidean nearest-neighbour search across the 20-trajectory prior bank (under fallback). Among the unadapted models in the ablation table (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}), TransFuser completes a forward pass in 103.1\,ms thanks to its compact ResNet-34 encoder, whereas Hydra-MDP takes 243.9\,ms with its larger \mbox{V2-99} encoder while gaining roughly 7 PDMS points. Because our synchronous benchmark executes gradient computation sequentially on the main thread and does not log standalone timing for Cluster Entropy (Centaur), deployment overhead could be reduced either by offloading backpropagation to an asynchronous worker or by updating weights only when frame uncertainty exceeds a cutoff (bottom row of the ablation table, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}).

\paragraph{SVD gradient filtering.} Following prior test-time adaptation work~\cite{Gao2024fstta}, one can replace uniform gradient averaging across the history buffer with singular-value decomposition (SVD) projection to extract principal gradient directions. On TransFuser-SE, however, SVD projection ($\text{TTT}^{*}$ in the ablation table, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}) lowers PDMS relative to simple averaging.

\paragraph{Visual encoder comparison.} Our ablation table (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}) also contrasts the default \mbox{V2-99} image encoder of Hydra-MDP against a ViT-L encoder across deployment modes. \mbox{V2-99} achieves higher PDMS in every row except the KL-Divergence fallback setting, where ViT-L scores roughly 4 points higher, subject to the fallback threshold discrepancy analysed in Section~\ref{centaur:app:consistency}.

\paragraph{Buffer length $F$.} Sweeping the gradient buffer horizon $F \in \{1,2,3,4\}$ for Hydra-SE (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}) produces single-seed PDMS scores within a 0.5-point interval between $F=2$ (91.3) and $F=4$ (91.8); all four settings improve over unadapted Hydra-MDP (90.3), while the narrow spread across single runs precludes any strong claim about optimal buffer length.

\paragraph{Failure identification thresholds.} Varying the classification cutoff in the threshold table (\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class_supp}) illustrates the trade-off between true-positive recall (TPR) and overall accuracy for Evidential TransFuser and Semantic-Entropy Hydra-MDP: higher cutoffs reduce false alarms on normal driving frames and thus raise accuracy while lowering recall on failure frames. Because failures represent a minority of \texttt{navtest} scenes, every tested cutoff records lower raw accuracy than a trivial Select None classifier, confirming that raw accuracy is not an informative metric under class imbalance and motivating threshold-free summaries (AUROC, AUPRC) in future audits.

\subsection{Internal Consistency Checks}\label{centaur:app:consistency}

In this subsection, we present the exact algebraic checks underlying the three numerical discrepancies noted in the main text (\ref{ch:centaur}), derived strictly from the rounded entries in the main and supplementary tables (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation}, \ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}, \ref{centaur:tab:navsafe}, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}, \ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}, and~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class_supp}).

\paragraph{Failure base rate.} In the main failure-detection table (\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}), the Select All baseline lists a 15.6\% positive failure prevalence for Hydra-MDP on \texttt{navtest} under the nominal definition $\mathrm{PDMS}=0$. Because every frame score satisfies $\mathrm{PDMS} \in [0, 100]$, having 15.6\% of frames at zero bounds the dataset-wide mean PDMS by $100 - 15.6 = 84.4$, which is below the 90.3 mean PDMS reported in the main ablation table (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation}). In addition, the multiplicative PDMS formula (\ref{centaur:eq:pdms}) forces $\mathrm{PDMS}=0$ only when $\mathrm{NC}=0$, when $\mathrm{DAC}=0$, or when $\mathrm{TTC}=\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{EP}=0$ simultaneously (impossible when mean comfort is 100.0). From Hydra-MDP's 98.4 mean NC and 97.8 mean DAC, at most $1.6\% + 2.2\% = 3.8\%$ of frames can have $\mathrm{NC}=0$ or $\mathrm{DAC}=0$. The same arithmetic applies to TransFuser: a 24.1\% zero-PDMS prevalence (\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class_supp}) bounds mean PDMS at 75.9 versus the 83.4 reported in the NAVSIM v1.0 comparison (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}), while its 97.1 mean NC and 91.9 mean DAC allow at most $2.9\% + 8.1\% = 11.0\%$ zero-PDMS frames. Hence the failure labels in both detection tables (\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class} and~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class_supp}) either reflect a broader nonzero cutoff or were evaluated on a different frame subset.

\paragraph{Trigger rates of the fallback.} Let $p$ denote the positive failure prevalence, $\mathrm{TPR}$ the true-positive recall, and $a$ the overall binary accuracy on a common test set. Standard confusion-matrix algebra gives the total flagged fraction as $1 - p - a + 2p\,\mathrm{TPR}$ and the false-positive rate as $(1 - p - a + p\,\mathrm{TPR})/(1 - p)$. Substituting the values from the main detection table (\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}), which use the same cutoffs as the fallback in Sections~\ref{centaur:sec:setup} and~\ref{centaur:app:baselines}, produces the flagging fractions in Table~\ref{centaur:tab:implied_rates}, ranging from 29.0\% to 33.3\% of \texttt{navtest}. Because the fraction of frames exceeding a fixed uncertainty cutoff depends solely on the uncertainty scores and the cutoff value, these flagging fractions are invariant to how ground-truth failure labels are defined whenever the main ablation and detection tables (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation} and~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class}) evaluate the same frames. Even if every replaced trajectory achieved $\mathrm{EP}=0$, overriding 29.0\%--33.3\% of frames could reduce Hydra-MDP's 86.5 mean EP to no less than $86.5 - 33.3 = 53.2$, whereas the reported fallback EP values of 8.2--13.2 require overriding 73.3\%--78.3\% of frames. Moreover, the implied flagging fractions show that Full Entropy (33.3\%) flags more frames than Cluster Entropy (30.4\%) and that reported fallback PDMS moves upward rather than downward as the implied flagging fraction rises (63.1, 64.2, 65.3, and 65.9 at 29.0\%, 30.4\%, 31.7\%, and 33.3\%). For TransFuser with Semantic Entropy at cutoff 0.8 ($\mathrm{TPR}=35.3$\%, $a=63.1$\%, $p=24.1$\% in~\ref{centaur:tab:fail_class_supp}), the same identity yields a 29.8\% flagging fraction and an EP lower bound of $79.3 - 29.8 = 49.5$ versus the 14.6 reported in the supplementary ablation table (\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_ablation_supp}).
