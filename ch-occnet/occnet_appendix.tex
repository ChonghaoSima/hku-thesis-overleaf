\section{Evaluation Metrics}\label{occnet:app:metrics}

\paragraph{Completion metrics.} In 3D semantic scene completion, the network predicts a category index for every cell of the discretised volume. Overall semantic accuracy across categories is measured by the class-averaged intersection-over-union (mIoU), defined as
\begin{equation}
  \text{mIoU} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\frac{\text{TP}_c}{\text{TP}_c+\text{FP}_c+\text{FN}_c},
  \label{occnet:eq:miou}
\end{equation}
in which $C=16$ denotes the total count of semantic categories in OpenOcc, while $\text{TP}_c$, $\text{FP}_c$ and $\text{FN}_c$ denote the numbers of true-positive, false-positive and false-negative voxel predictions for category $c$. To evaluate binary 3D shape reconstruction independently of category assignment, we also compute the class-agnostic geometric accuracy $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$ by collapsing all 16 categories into a single non-empty label:
\begin{equation}
  \text{IoU}_{\text{geo}} = \frac{\text{TP}_{\text{occ}}}{\text{TP}_{\text{occ}}+\text{FP}_{\text{occ}}+\text{FN}_{\text{occ}}},
  \label{occnet:eq:iougeo}
\end{equation}
with $\text{TP}_{\text{occ}}$ counting cells both predicted and labelled as non-empty, $\text{FP}_{\text{occ}}$ counting empty cells predicted as non-empty, and $\text{FN}_{\text{occ}}$ counting non-empty cells predicted as empty. During evaluation on OpenOcc, both $\text{mIoU}$ and $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$ are accumulated over the camera-visible voxel subset determined by the ray-casting visibility mask, excluding occluded cells behind observed surfaces. Because standard voxel-level IoU penalises depth shifts along camera rays unevenly depending on surface thickness, subsequent benchmarks introduced the ray-based RayIoU metric~\cite{liu2024sparseocc}, which was developed after our study and is not computed in this chapter.

\paragraph{Detection metrics.} Bounding-box estimation is scored under the standard nuScenes benchmark protocol~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes}, which pairs mean average precision (mAP) calculated across centre-distance thresholds on the ground plane with five true-positive error measures: average translation (ATE), scale (ASE), orientation (AOE), velocity (AVE) and attribute (AAE) errors, combined into the composite nuScenes detection score (NDS).

\paragraph{Planning metrics.} Trajectory quality is assessed under the open-loop evaluation protocol of ST-P3~\cite{hu2022stp3}. Path fidelity is measured by the L2 Euclidean distance between predicted waypoints and the logged human-driven trajectory at 1\,s, 2\,s and 3\,s horizons, while safety is quantified by the percentage of evaluation frames in which the planned ego vehicle box intersects the ground-truth future bounding boxes of dynamic road users (vehicles and pedestrians). Because evaluation proceeds open-loop on pre-recorded nuScenes logs without closed-loop simulation, surrounding traffic participants follow their recorded paths without reacting to the ego vehicle, and single-step tracking errors do not compound over time.

\section{Implementation Details of OccNet}\label{occnet:app:implementation}

\paragraph{Image backbone and feature pyramid.} Consistent with established multi-camera 3D perception pipelines~\cite{li2022bevformer,wang2021fcos3d}, surround-view images are encoded by a ResNet101~\cite{he2016deep} backbone coupled with a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)~\cite{lin2017feature}. Multi-scale representations are tapped from residual stages $S_3$, $S_4$ and $S_5$, where stage $S_n$ downsamples the input spatial dimensions by a factor of $1/2^n$ and outputs $C_n=256\times2^{n-2}$ channels. The FPN neck fuses these three stages into pyramid levels at spatial scales of 1/16, 1/32 and 1/64 of the input image, projecting every level to a uniform width of 256 channels.

\paragraph{Ground-plane encoder.} Our bird's-eye-view encoder adopts the transformer architecture of BEVFormer~\cite{li2022bevformer} to aggregate multi-scale FPN features onto the horizontal plane. It stacks two encoder blocks, each alternating between ego-aligned temporal self-attention and camera-projected spatial cross-attention, progressively updating the learnable grid queries $Q_t$ into a unified planar feature map.

\paragraph{Cross-stage projection in the cascade decoder.} Within the cascade voxel decoder, transitioning from stage $i$ with feature volume $V'_{t,i}$ of shape $Z_i\times H\times W\times C_i$ to stage $i+1$ with feature volume $V'_{t,i+1}$ of shape $Z_{i+1}\times H\times W\times C_{i+1}$ is carried out by a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that maps the flattened vertical-channel product from $Z_i C_i$ to $Z_{i+1} C_{i+1}$ at every horizontal grid cell $(h,w)$. Prior to executing voxel-based spatial cross-attention at stage $i$, an accompanying linear layer projects the 256-channel FPN pyramid features to match the stage channel width $C_i$.

\paragraph{Optimisation schedule.} Following standard multi-view training recipes~\cite{li2022bevformer,wang2021fcos3d}, networks are trained for 24 epochs using the AdamW optimiser~\cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} with an initial learning rate of $2\times10^{-4}$, a weight decay of $1\times10^{-2}$, and a per-GPU batch size of one multi-camera sample (six surround views). In multi-task experiments, the primary training stage optimises semantic scene completion and 3D object detection jointly, after which the learned representation is frozen while downstream BEV segmentation and trajectory planning heads are fine-tuned separately.

\paragraph{Single-scale baselines.} Whereas OccNet expands vertical resolution across four cascaded stages, the VoxelNet and BEVNet baselines operate at a single intermediate feature resolution. VoxelNet defines a 3D query volume $Q_{\text{voxel}}$ of size $4\times H\times W$ and lifts multi-view image features directly into a coarse 3D tensor $F_{\text{voxel}}$ of shape $4\times H\times W\times C_1$ through 3D deformable attention (Eq.~\eqref{occnet:eq:3dda}), followed by a linear layer that expands the four vertical slices into the full 16-slice occupancy volume $V$ of shape $16\times H\times W\times C_2$. Conversely, BEVNet constructs a planar representation $F_{\text{bev}}$ of shape $H\times W\times C_1$ identical to BEVFormer and reshapes its channel axis directly into the 3D volume $V$ of shape $16\times H\times W\times C_2$, where $C_1$ and $C_2$ denote the intermediate and output channel widths. Both baselines incorporate temporal alignment across consecutive sweeps on the same footing as OccNet.

\paragraph{Registration of dynamic foreground instances.} Building dense 3D ground truth from sparse LiDAR requires decoupling moving traffic participants from the static environment before temporal aggregation. Because nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} provides 3D bounding boxes and point-wise panoptic labels only at 2\,Hz key frames, bounding-box poses for intermediate 20\,Hz LiDAR sweeps are synthesised by linearly interpolating 3D box centres and spherically interpolating yaw angles between adjacent key frames. Points falling inside each interpolated box are transformed into the instance-local coordinate system, accumulated across the scene, and transformed back into the target key frame.

\paragraph{Ground-truth refinement stages.} After registering accumulated static background sweeps and instance-centric foreground point clouds into the reference LiDAR frame, dense voxel annotations are produced through the four sequential processing stages illustrated in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:occ_gt_pipeline}, yielding the completed semantic volume shown in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:occ_gt_pipeline}(d).

\paragraph{Class and motion distributions.} Across the 700 training logs and 150 validation logs of nuScenes, OpenOcc annotates 34,149 key frames comprising over 1.4 billion occupied voxels partitioned into 16 semantic categories. Category counts across the full dataset are plotted in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:statistics_occ} and reveal a pronounced long-tailed distribution: the ten foreground object categories collectively occupy just 5.33\% of non-empty voxels, with small road users such as bicycles and motorcycles representing 0.02\% and 0.03\% of the total. In addition, 3D scene-flow vectors are assigned to occupied voxels belonging to the eight movable foreground categories by differentiating box trajectories, providing motion supervision for forecasting and planning tasks even though flow prediction itself is not benchmarked in this thesis. Applying a speed threshold of $v_{\text{th}}=0.2$\,m/s divides foreground instances into moving and stationary states; as plotted in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:statistics_flow}, more than half of foreground object voxels are in motion, reflecting the prevalence of dynamic actors in urban driving logs.

\section{Additional Experiments}\label{occnet:app:experiments}

\paragraph{Temporal context length.} Tables~\ref{occnet:tab:frame_number_ssc} and~\ref{occnet:tab:frame_number_lidarseg} evaluate how varying the number of historical frames fused by voxel-based temporal self-attention during training affects single-run perception accuracy. On semantic scene completion, aggregate mIoU increases from 19.21 without temporal context to 20.81 when conditioning on four preceding frames, and levels off at 20.68 when extending the window to nine frames; camera-only LiDAR segmentation exhibits a similar overall gain from zero to four frames. Progression across intermediate window lengths is not strictly monotonic: conditioning on a single past frame scores below the zero-history baseline on both benchmarks (18.35 versus 19.21 mIoU on scene completion, and 48.41 versus 53.82 mIoU on LiDAR segmentation), and individual categories display wide single-run swings, with bus IoU in LiDAR segmentation ranging between 57.71 and 75.49 across window lengths. Consequently, differences of roughly 1 mIoU between adjacent temporal settings lie within the expected band of single-seed training variation.

\paragraph{Separate ablation batches across tables.} In Table~\ref{occnet:tab:frame_number_lidarseg}, OccNet with a ResNet50 backbone and two history frames records 52.33 mIoU on nuScenes LiDAR segmentation, whereas Table~\ref{occnet:tab:lidarseg_resolution} lists 47.29 mIoU at the default 0.50\,m voxel resolution. These two series come from separate training batches executed at different stages of our study: the temporal sweep in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:frame_number_lidarseg} was evaluated from checkpoints trained under the full multi-task schedule, whereas the resolution sweep in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:lidarseg_resolution} was trained as a standalone single-task batch across the three voxel grid scales. Each table should therefore be read internally along its own ablation axis rather than compared across tables.

\paragraph{Voxel-based obstacle collision evaluation.} Standard open-loop planning benchmarks test collisions only against annotated 3D bounding boxes of vehicles and pedestrians, ignoring static roadside structures and irregular obstacles. To evaluate clearance against the full 3D environment, Table~\ref{occnet:tab:occupancy_metrics} measures collisions between the planned ego footprint and ground-truth occupied voxels belonging to all ten foreground categories plus four non-drivable background categories (other flat, terrain, manmade and vegetation). Under this voxel-based criterion, trajectories conditioned on predicted occupancy record slightly lower collision rates than box-conditioned trajectories at 1\,s and 3\,s (1.68\% versus 1.75\% and 4.32\% versus 4.37\%), but a slightly higher rate at 2\,s (2.94\% versus 2.85\%), with all differences confined to 0.05--0.09 percentage points and reversing sign across horizons. Because Table~\ref{occnet:tab:occupancy_metrics} was evaluated in a separate planner batch using the full-class occupancy rasterisation, its L2 displacement numbers shift by 0.01 to 0.05\,m relative to the corresponding entries in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning} (for example, 3.02\,m versus 3.07\,m for predicted occupancy and 3.03\,m versus 3.00\,m for ground-truth boxes at 3\,s), demonstrating that run-to-run variation in L2 is on the same scale as the gap between box and occupancy inputs. Designing a native 3D voxel cost function rather than projecting predictions to a binary BEV grid remains an open avenue for future work.

\paragraph{Transfer of pre-trained features to planning.} Complementing the 3D detection and BEV segmentation transfer experiments in Section~\ref{occnet:sec:main_results}, Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning_pretrain} examines whether initialising the perception backbone of ST-P3~\cite{hu2022stp3} with occupancy pre-trained weights improves downstream trajectory fine-tuning relative to 3D detection pre-training. Across all three evaluation horizons in this single-run test, occupancy pre-training results in higher L2 errors and higher collision rates than detection pre-training. Note that Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning_pretrain} follows the cumulative horizon-averaging convention of the end-to-end fine-tuned ST-P3 planner, which averages L2 displacement and collision rates over all timesteps from $t=0$ up to $T\in\{1\,\text{s},2\,\text{s},3\,\text{s}\}$. Accordingly, the two rows within Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning_pretrain} are directly comparable to each other but not to the discrete-step numbers in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning}, showing that under end-to-end feature fine-tuning, occupancy pre-training does not improve trajectory regression over detection pre-training.

\section{Visualisation Results}\label{occnet:app:visualization}

Qualitative examples from two validation scenes are displayed in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:planning2} and Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:occ_pred}. Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:planning2} contrasts trajectory selection when projecting predicted 3D bounding boxes versus predicted 3D occupancy onto the ground plane for cost evaluation, illustrating a scenario in which the occupancy-conditioned path maintains wider lateral clearance beside an articulated truck. Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:occ_pred} shows surround-view camera inputs alongside perspective and top-down renderings of the predicted semantic voxel grid, where OccNet captures both road geometry and small distant obstacles.
