\section{Introduction}\label{occnet:sec:intro}
\suppressfloats[t]

An embodied agent can only act on what its internal representation resolves. Before braking, changing lanes, or yielding, an automated vehicle must determine which 3D regions contain physical matter and what occupies them. Classical driving perception splits this task into two approximations: dynamic actors are enclosed in 3D cuboids from a fixed taxonomy, while static road layout is projected onto a planar bird's-eye-view (BEV) grid. This split assumes that every hazard belongs to a catalogued class, fits a rigid box, and lacks relevant vertical structure. Urban streets violate all three assumptions: truck-mounted cranes swing past their chassis, flatbeds carry overhanging pipes, gantries and bridges restrict vertical clearance, and irregular barriers border the road. Cuboids clip protruding geometry, whereas planar BEV maps discard elevation.

This chapter addresses the first research question (RQ1): whether an embodied driving system can represent arbitrary 3D obstacle geometry from surround-view cameras in a form that downstream planners can query. We use \emph{3D occupancy}: a regular ego-centric voxel grid in which occupied cells receive semantic labels and dynamic cells receive motion vectors. The grid imposes no cuboid shape prior and represents foreground objects and background structure in one coordinate frame, although its 16-class vocabulary remains closed. We evaluate occupancy as a shared scene descriptor on nuScenes under single-run perception and open-loop planning experiments. The study covers unusual geometries within known classes; it does not test open-set or cross-domain generalisation.

This chapter makes four contributions. First, OccNet reconstructs dense 3D occupancy from a spatiotemporal BEV representation through a cascade voxel decoder with temporal attention and 3D deformable attention\footnote{Distinct from the continuous single-object Occupancy Networks of Mescheder \etal~\cite{mescheder2019occupancy}.}. Second, OpenOcc provides dense foreground and background semantics together with foreground velocity vectors on nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes,fong2022panoptic}. Third, experiments cover semantic scene completion (SSC), point-queried LiDAR segmentation, 3D detection, BEV segmentation, and open-loop planning against planar BEV baselines and retrained TPVFormer~\cite{huang2023tpvformer}; the small open-loop planning differences (0.06--0.24 percentage points) are reported as single-run observations rather than established safety gains. Fourth, OpenScene extends occupancy and flow labels from 5.5 hours of nuScenes to more than 120 hours of multi-city nuPlan driving~\cite{sima2023openscene}, whose redistributed sensor streams underpin NAVSIM and the CVPR 2024 and CVPR 2025 Autonomous Grand Challenge tracks (which use the sensor streams rather than the voxel labels). Methodologically, OccNet shares BEVFormer~\cite{li2022bevformer} and 3D deformable-attention~\cite{Zhu2021DeformableDD} foundations with contemporaneous VoxFormer~\cite{li2023voxformer}, while contributing the coarse-to-fine height cascade, voxel temporal self-attention, OpenOcc pipeline, and cross-task evaluation.

The remainder of this chapter is organised as follows: Section~\ref{occnet:sec:related} reviews related work; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:method} presents OccNet; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:benchmark} details OpenOcc; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:experiments} reports main experiments; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:discussion} discusses limitations, NAVSIM comparisons (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}), and 2023--2026 work; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:openscene} describes OpenScene; Section~\ref{app:occnet} presents implementation details and additional ablations; and Section~\ref{occnet:sec:summary} concludes the chapter.

Human drivers judge 3D obstacle geometry before identifying fine-grained categories, whether passing a saloon car or trailing a flatbed lorry with an overhanging pipe. For automated vehicles, determining whether a 3D volume is physically occupied precedes category recognition, yet inferring 3D structure from cameras alone is challenging across cluttered urban streets.

Partitioning space into semantically labelled voxels (3D occupancy) directly captures arbitrary 3D geometry. Industrial stacks have adopted similar designs: Mobileye estimates pseudo-LiDAR via VIDAR~\cite{mobile2020ces}, and Tesla predicts 3D occupancy grids~\cite{tesla_ai_day} (Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:motivation}(a)--(b)).\footnote{Mobileye's VIDAR differs from ViDAR~\cite{yang2024vidar}, the point-cloud forecasting model in Section~\ref{occnet:sec:openscene}.} Whereas a rigid cuboid encloses only a truck's cab, voxels trace its extended crane boom (Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:motivation}(c)--(d)).

Table~\ref{occnet:tab:compare_to_other_form} contrasts 3D occupancy with standard representations: 3D boxes cover only enumerated foreground categories; LiDAR segmentation resolves $\sim$0.02\,m surfaces but requires range sensors at deployment; and BEV segmentation collapses height. Volumetric occupancy unifies foreground and background in 3D at 0.25--0.5\,m resolution from cameras alone. Unlike classical binary occupancy grids~\cite{moras2015grid} and early SSC models~\cite{song2017sscnet,huang2023tpvformer,li2023voxformer,cao2022monoscene,miao2023occdepth}, which mostly processed single frames (except VoxFormer-T~\cite{li2023voxformer}) and were disconnected from planning, OccNet lifts temporal BEV features~\cite{li2022bevformer,chen2022persformer,wang2022monocular} into a shared 3D occupancy descriptor for SSC, 3D detection, BEV segmentation, and open-loop planning; planning-oriented end-to-end design is represented separately by UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad}. Trained on OpenOcc (34,149 nuScenes frames~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes,fong2022panoptic}, $>$1.4B voxels across 16 classes plus unbenchmarked foreground flow), our experiments show three results (qualified in Sections~\ref{occnet:sec:experiments} and~\ref{occnet:sec:discussion}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Perception.} OccNet reaches 26.98 mIoU on OpenOcc SSC (+14\% relative over retrained TPVFormer~\cite{huang2023tpvformer} at 23.67 mIoU) and trails LiDAR RangeNet++~\cite{milioto2019rangenet++} by $\sim$5 mIoU on point-queried LiDAR segmentation. Occupancy pre-training lifts low-data 3D detection (1/256--1/64 splits) over FCOS3D~\cite{wang2021fcos3d} initialisation by up to $\sim$10 mAP at 1/128.
  \item \emph{Open-loop planning.} Rasterised occupancy lowers open-loop nuScenes collision rates by 0.06--0.24 percentage points relative to boxes from OccNet's detection head (single runs), without uniform L2 gains.
  \item \emph{Supervision density.} Dense OpenOcc pre-training beats sparse LiDAR-voxel pre-training at the 1/128 detection split while performing similarly at 1/256 and 1/64.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}\label{occnet:sec:related}

We situate 3D occupancy against standard driving perception outputs and contemporaneous SSC models; broader multi-camera 3D perception is surveyed in Chapter~\ref{ch:background} and~\cite{li2023bevdevils}, while 2023--2026 occupancy work appears in Section~\ref{occnet:sec:subsequent}.

\paragraph{3D detection, LiDAR segmentation, and BEV segmentation.} 3D detectors~\cite{Shi2020PVRCNNPF,wang2021fcos3d,li2022bevformer,liu2023bevfusion} fit oriented cuboids to foreground actors, omitting background hazards and distorting articulated geometry. Point-cloud segmenters~\cite{zhu2021cylindrical,milioto2019rangenet++} label 3D laser returns directly but require LiDAR hardware and suffer from distance-dependent sparsity and occlusion~\cite{song2017sscnet}. Planar BEV segmentation~\cite{zhou2022cross,li2022hdmapnet} jointly maps road layout and actors but discards vertical clearance (gantries, overhangs, bridges). Volumetric occupancy retains 3D height and arbitrary contours from cameras alone.

\paragraph{3D reconstruction and semantic scene completion.} Multi-view 3D reconstruction~\cite{Hartley2004,mildenhall2020nerf} and neural implicit fields~\cite{mueller2022instant,cao2022scenerf,Tancik2022blocknerf,mescheder2019occupancy} typically optimise per-scene geometry or rendering rather than real-time feed-forward semantic perception. Semantic scene completion was established for depth input by SSCNet~\cite{song2017sscnet}; camera-only SSC was pioneered by MonoScene~\cite{cao2022monoscene} and expanded concurrently by depth-seeded VoxFormer~\cite{li2023voxformer}, stereo-distilled OccDepth~\cite{miao2023occdepth}, tri-perspective TPVFormer~\cite{huang2023tpvformer}, and OpenOccupancy~\cite{wang2023openoccupancy}. Earlier studies targeted front-view SemanticKITTI~\cite{behley2019semantickitti} or indoor NYUv2~\cite{Silberman2012nyuv2}, and apart from VoxFormer-T~\cite{li2023voxformer}, BEVDet4D~\cite{huang2022bevdet4d}, and unpublished industry systems~\cite{tesla_ai_day}, contemporaneous SSC models were single-frame and disconnected from motion planning.

\section{OccNet}\label{occnet:sec:method}
\suppressfloats[t]

As shown in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:framework}, OccNet maps multi-camera images to a shared 3D \emph{occupancy descriptor} in two phases: \emph{occupancy reconstruction} and \emph{occupancy exploitation}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{ch-occnet/images/fig_pipeline_overview_v8_used.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The OccNet pipeline.} \textbf{I.~Occupancy reconstruction.} Surround-view images pass through a spatiotemporal BEV encoder, after which a cascade voxel decoder progressively expands vertical resolution, fusing historical voxel features $V'_{t-1,i}$ and image features via 3D deformable attention (skip connections omitted). \textbf{II.~Occupancy exploitation.} Task heads decode semantic scene completion and 3D boxes from the shared descriptor; vertical projection yields a BEV obstacle map for trajectory planning~\cite{hu2022stp3}.}
  \label{occnet:fig:framework}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Reconstruction and exploitation.} To lift 2D camera features~\cite{li2022bevformer,chen2022persformer,liang2022bevfusion} into 3D voxel space, we contrast two baseline extremes: \emph{BEVNet}, which feeds a 2D BEV grid directly into task heads at minimal cost but lacks height resolution, and \emph{VoxelNet}, which maintains full-resolution 3D voxel queries throughout at high memory and latency cost.\footnote{BEVNet and VoxelNet denote the ablation baselines in this chapter; our camera-based VoxelNet differs from the LiDAR detector of Zhou and Tuzel~\cite{zhou2018voxelnet}.} OccNet bridges both extremes, matching or exceeding VoxelNet's completion accuracy with fewer parameters and less memory while outperforming BEVNet (Section~\ref{occnet:sec:analysis}). A BEVFormer encoder~\cite{li2022bevformer} combines multi-camera features $F_t$, previous BEV features $B_{t-1}$, and queries $Q_t$ into $B_t$, which the cascade voxel decoder (Section~\ref{occnet:sec:cascade_voxel_decoder}) lifts alongside $B_{t-1}$ and $F_t$ into the 3D occupancy descriptor. Following UniAD~\cite{hu2023uniad}, modular heads decode 3D SSC~\cite{song2017sscnet} and 3D boxes, whose vertical projections form a binary BEV obstacle map for a command-conditioned sampling and GRU-refinement planner (Section~\ref{occnet:sec:exploitation_of_occupancy}).

\subsection{Cascade Voxel Decoder}\label{occnet:sec:cascade_voxel_decoder}

\paragraph{From BEV to cascaded voxels.} Decoding 3D voxels directly from 2D BEV loses vertical fidelity, whereas full-grid 3D transformers incur heavy memory and latency costs (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:efficiency}). We factor the mapping from $B_t\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C_{\text{BEV}}}$ to $V_t\in\mathbb{R}^{Z\times H\times W\times C_{\text{Voxel}}}$ across $N$ stages, where $H\times W$ is horizontal resolution, $Z$ is final voxel height, and $C_{\text{BEV}}, C_{\text{Voxel}}$ are channel widths. Stage $i$ produces $V'_{t,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{Z_i\times H\times W\times C_i}$, doubling height $Z_i$ towards $Z$ while halving channels $C_i$ from $C_{\text{BEV}}$ to $C_{\text{Voxel}}$ (Section~\ref{occnet:sec:exp_setup}). Linear projections lift $B_{t-1}$ and $B_t$ to $V'_{t-1,1}$ and $V'_{t,1}$, and inter-stage MLPs map $Z_i\times C_i$ to $Z_{i+1}\times C_{i+1}$ (Section~\ref{occnet:app:implementation}). Each stage applies voxel temporal self-attention between $V'_{t,i}$ and ego-motion-aligned $V'_{t-1,i}$, followed by voxel spatial cross-attention over image features $F_t$. On OpenOcc, adding two history frames improves single-run mIoU by 0.27 points over single-frame input, and four frames by 1.60 points (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:frame_number_ssc}).

\paragraph{3D deformable attention and spatial cross-attention.} Because dense 3D self-attention is quadratic in the voxel token count $ZHW$, we extend 2D deformable attention~\cite{Zhu2021DeformableDD} to 3D (\emph{3D-DA}). Given volume $V'_{t,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{Z_i\times H\times W\times C_i}$, query $\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{C_i}$, and normalised 3D reference point $\boldsymbol{p}\in[0,1]^3$, 3D-DA computes
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{3D-DA}(\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{p},V'_{t,i})
  = \sum_{m=1}^{M} W_m \sum_{k=1}^{K} A_{mk}(\boldsymbol{q})\, W'_m\, \phi\!\big(V'_{t,i};\,\boldsymbol{p}+\Delta\boldsymbol{p}_{mk}(\boldsymbol{q})\big),
  \label{occnet:eq:3dda}
\end{equation}
with $M=8$ heads, $K=4\ll Z_iHW$ sampling points per head, projections $W_m\in\mathbb{R}^{C_i\times(C_i/M)}$ and $W'_m\in\mathbb{R}^{(C_i/M)\times C_i}$, softmax weights $A_{mk}(\boldsymbol{q})\ge 0$ ($\sum_{k}A_{mk}(\boldsymbol{q})=1$), and signed learned 3D offsets $\Delta\boldsymbol{p}_{mk}(\boldsymbol{q})\in\mathbb{R}^3$ evaluated via zero-padded trilinear sampling $\phi(V'_{t,i};\,\boldsymbol{p}+\Delta\boldsymbol{p}_{mk}(\boldsymbol{q}))$. For spatial cross-attention, each voxel in $V'_{t,i}$ projects $N_{\text{ref},i}$ 3D reference heights onto the camera planes and aggregates multi-scale features $F_t$ via 2D deformable attention~\cite{Zhu2021DeformableDD}, preserving per-voxel height resolution rather than collapsing pillars as in BEVFormer~\cite{li2022bevformer}.

\subsection{Exploiting Occupancy for Driving Tasks}\label{occnet:sec:exploitation_of_occupancy}

From the shared descriptor $V_t$, lightweight heads decode four tasks. For \emph{semantic scene completion}, an MLP trained with focal loss~\cite{Lin2017FocalLF} classifies each voxel into a semantic class or empty space, while an $L_1$-supervised branch regresses horizontal velocity vectors for occupied foreground voxels. For \emph{3D object detection}, $V_t$ is pooled vertically into a BEV map for a BEVFormer-style Deformable DETR head~\cite{li2022bevformer,Zhu2021DeformableDD}. For \emph{BEV segmentation}, four ST-P3 branches~\cite{hu2022stp3} decode drivable area, lanes, vehicles, and pedestrians from pooled BEV features. For \emph{motion planning}, predicted 3D occupancy or 3D boxes are projected vertically into a binary BEV obstacle mask (1 occupied, 0 free; Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:framework}). Sampled velocity-acceleration-curvature trajectories are ranked by safety, comfort, and progress costs; because dense occupancy populates more background cells than sparse boxes, the safety cost weight is scaled between the two rasters (without a sensitivity sweep), and voxel flow is not used. Conditioned on the driving command (forward, left, or right), the lowest-cost trajectory is refined by a front-camera GRU following ST-P3~\cite{hu2022stp3}.

\section{The OpenOcc Benchmark}\label{occnet:sec:benchmark}

To train and evaluate dense camera-based 3D occupancy, we build OpenOcc on nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes,fong2022panoptic}. Developed concurrently with Occ3D~\cite{tian2023occ3d}, OpenOccupancy~\cite{wang2023openoccupancy}, and SurroundOcc~\cite{wei2023surroundocc}, OpenOcc pairs 360-degree six-camera views with surround 3D voxel labels (unlike forward-frustum SemanticKITTI~\cite{behley2019semantickitti}) and includes per-voxel foreground motion vectors (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:openscene}). Because nuScenes occupancy benchmarks use different bounds, voxel sizes, taxonomies, and visibility masks, scores are not directly comparable across suites (\eg the CVPR 2023 challenge used Occ3D-nuScenes; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:subsequent}).

\subsection{Benchmark Overview}\label{occnet:sec:benchmark_overview}

OpenOcc accumulates multi-sweep LiDAR returns and 3D box tracks across all 700 training and 150 validation scenes of nuScenes (34,149 frames, $>$1.4B occupied voxels). Occupied voxels take 16 classes: 10 foreground (barrier, bicycle, bus, car, construction vehicle, motorcycle, pedestrian, traffic cone, trailer, truck), of which the 8 movable vehicle, pedestrian, and cyclist classes carry non-zero 2D horizontal velocity vectors (barriers and traffic cones are static), and 6 background (driveable surface, other flat, sidewalk, terrain, manmade, vegetation). The ego LiDAR volume $\mathcal{V}=[-50\,\text{m},50\,\text{m}]\times[-50\,\text{m},50\,\text{m}]\times[-5\,\text{m},3\,\text{m}]$ is quantised at $\Delta s=0.5$\,m into a $200\times200\times16$ grid.

\subsection{Generating High-Quality Annotations}\label{occnet:sec:annotation}

To aggregate nuScenes' 2\,Hz annotated key frames with 20\,Hz unannotated intermediate sweeps~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes,fong2022panoptic} without motion streaking, we separate static background points (registered in the world frame) from points inside 3D boxes (registered in each object's canonical frame using linearly interpolated box centres and yaw angles across intermediate sweeps; Section~\ref{occnet:app:openocc}). Transformed back into the target key frame and quantised into 0.5\,m voxels, each cell takes the majority panoptic LiDAR label~\cite{fong2022panoptic} of its key-frame points, while foreground cells inherit finite-difference box velocities. Voxels containing only unlabelled intermediate points inherit the majority class within a $3\times3\times3$ neighbourhood; morphological hole-filling closes $z$-axis odometry gaps in the drivable surface; and ray casting from the camera centres masks out occluded voxels (Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:occ_gt_pipeline}, Section~\ref{occnet:app:openocc}). Heuristics were tuned via qualitative spot checks without exhaustive frame-by-frame manual verification.

\section{Experiments}\label{occnet:sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{occnet:sec:exp_setup}

\paragraph{Implementation.} Following BEVFormer~\cite{li2022bevformer}, we use ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50~\cite{he2016deep,deng2009imagenet} and FCOS3D-pretrained ResNet101-DCN~\cite{he2016deep,wang2021fcos3d} backbones. The BEV grid $B_t$ has $H=W=200$ and $C_{\text{BEV}}=256$. Across $N=4$ cascade stages, $V'_{t,i}\in\mathbb{R}^{Z_i\times H\times W\times C_i}$ uses heights $Z_i=2^i$, widths $C_1=C_2=128$ and $C_3=C_4=64$, and $N_{\text{ref},i}=4$ reference heights per voxel. Models train for 24 epochs at learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$ with two history frames (matching BEVFormer-small for memory efficiency, though four frames reach 20.81 vs.\ 19.48 mIoU in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:frame_number_ssc}). Details for BEVNet and VoxelNet appear in Section~\ref{occnet:app:implementation}.

\paragraph{Tasks and metrics.} OpenOcc SSC is evaluated by 16-class voxel mIoU and binary geometric $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$ (RayIoU~\cite{liu2024sparseocc} was introduced later; Section~\ref{occnet:sec:subsequent}). LiDAR segmentation and 3D detection use standard nuScenes validation metrics, BEV segmentation uses class IoU, and open-loop planning follows ST-P3~\cite{hu2022stp3} (L2 error and box collision rate on recorded logs, which ego kinematics alone can largely satisfy~\cite{zhai2023rethinking,li2024ego}; Chapter~\ref{ch:background}). Metric definitions appear in Section~\ref{occnet:app:metrics}; all tables report single runs.

\paragraph{Occupancy for planning.} Projecting OccNet's jointly trained 3D boxes (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:joint_train}) or semantic voxels into BEV rasters for the ST-P3 cost filter and GRU refiner~\cite{hu2022stp3}, Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning} compares two-class (vehicle/pedestrian) and 16-class (\emph{full}) occupancy against OccNet boxes, a re-run ST-P3 segmentation baseline, and ground-truth oracles under non-reactive open-loop nuScenes replay. Ground-truth occupancy gives the lowest oracle collisions. Among predicted inputs, two-class OccNet occupancy lowers collision rates at 1\,s, 2\,s, and 3\,s from 0.27\%, 0.68\%, and 1.59\% (predicted boxes) to 0.21\%, 0.55\%, and 1.35\%, a single-run reduction of 0.06--0.24 percentage points (15\%--22\% relative, or $\sim$4--14 of $\sim$6,000 validation frames) without paired significance tests or confidence intervals.

\paragraph{How strong is the planning evidence?} Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning} provides limited evidence for a planning benefit. Two-class occupancy lowers collision rate relative to boxes but has slightly higher 3\,s L2 error (3.07\,m \vs 3.03\,m), whereas full occupancy gives the lowest L2 values (1.29\,m, 2.13\,m, 2.99\,m) with somewhat higher 2\,s and 3\,s collisions (0.59\%, 1.37\%), and neither ego-progress speeds nor ego-status baselines~\cite{zhai2023rethinking,li2024ego} were tested. Separate-batch runs in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:occupancy_metrics} move 3\,s L2 on identical inputs by as much as 0.05\,m, so differences of that order should not be over-interpreted. The comparison also uses OccNet's 0.276-mAP auxiliary box head (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:joint_train}) rather than a dedicated detector, and the ground-truth collision gap is only 0.03--0.20 percentage points. Finally, the safety-cost weights differ between box and occupancy rasters (Section~\ref{occnet:sec:exploitation_of_occupancy}) and were not swept. The result therefore supports a same-model representational comparison rather than a general claim that occupancy improves planning.

\paragraph{Comparison with the published ST-P3.} Published ST-P3~\cite{hu2022stp3} reports 0.23\%, 0.62\%, and 1.27\% collisions and 1.33\,m, 2.11\,m, and 2.90\,m L2 at 1\,s, 2\,s, and 3\,s, beating our ST-P3 re-run (0.50\%, 0.88\%, 1.49\% collisions; 1.39\,m, 2.21\,m, 3.02\,m L2) and even beating our ground-truth oracles on 3\,s collisions (1.27\% \vs 1.30\%/1.50\%). Against published ST-P3, OccNet occupancy has lower 1\,s/2\,s collisions (0.21\%, 0.55\%) but higher 3\,s collisions (1.35\%) and 2\,s/3\,s L2 (2.18\,m, 3.07\,m). Thus, the controlled comparison between boxes and occupancy from the same OccNet model is the primary internal reference; qualitatively, full-class occupancy avoids unboxed background obstacles in Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:planning}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{occnet:sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Experiments Show}\label{occnet:sec:disc_show}

\paragraph{What occupancy provides.} Our experiments address RQ1 across three dimensions. First, voxel occupancy imposes no cuboid shape prior, capturing articulated vehicles better than voxelised detections (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:irregular}, subject to the surface-vs.-volume caveat in Section~\ref{occnet:sec:analysis}) while representing unboxed background obstacles in the binarised planning grid (Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:planning}); its 16-class taxonomy remains semantically closed. Second, camera-only OccNet infers 3D semantics from surround video and trails LiDAR RangeNet++ by $\sim$5 mIoU when queried at laser returns (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:lidarseg}). Third, in single-run open-loop nuScenes planning, predicted occupancy trims collision rates by 0.06--0.24 percentage points relative to OccNet boxes without a uniform L2 reduction (Section~\ref{occnet:sec:main_results}).

\paragraph{Relation to the occupancy results of Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}.} In non-reactive NAVSIM rollouts (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}, Table~\ref{centaur:tab:navtest_all_supp}), UniAD's post-hoc occupancy cost lowers PDMS from 83.4 to 75.2 (lowering NC, DAC, TTC, and EP), and adding an auxiliary occupancy head to PARA-Drive shifts PDMS from 84.0 to 83.6. Those single-seed runs use different backbones and coupling mechanisms (post-hoc optimisation or auxiliary regularisation rather than sampling-based raster filtering), showing that downstream gains depend on how the planner consumes the 3D grid.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}\label{occnet:sec:limitations}

Key boundaries include: (i) \emph{LiDAR and box supervision}: OpenOcc relies on calibrated nuScenes LiDAR sweeps and interpolated 3D boxes without manual voxel audits; (ii) \emph{single home benchmark}: all 3D perception runs use OpenOcc against baselines retrained in our codebase without cross-evaluating on Occ3D-nuScenes, SurroundOcc, or OpenOccupancy (Section~\ref{sec:method:validity}); (iii) \emph{voxel metrics and resolution}: evaluation uses voxel mIoU/$\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$ rather than RayIoU, queries LiDAR segmentation at known laser returns, and uses a 0.5\,m grid that is too coarse for sub-voxel box regression (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:joint_train}) compared with 0.25\,m (Tables~\ref{occnet:tab:irregular} and~\ref{occnet:tab:lidarseg_resolution}); (iv) \emph{task-dependent transfers and unvalidated flow}: occupancy pre-training does not beat detection pre-training for planning (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:planning_pretrain}), voxel-collision ranking flips at 2\,s (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:occupancy_metrics}), TPVFormer leads on large vehicles, Cylinder3D leads LiDAR segmentation (76.10 vs.\ 60.46 mIoU), and foreground flow vectors are not benchmarked; and (v) \emph{single-seed open-loop within nuScenes}: all tables report single runs without confidence intervals, reactive closed-loop rollouts, or cross-domain/open-set tests.

\subsection{Subsequent Developments (2023--2026)}\label{occnet:sec:subsequent}

\paragraph{Challenges and metrics.} The CVPR 2023 3D Occupancy Prediction challenge used Occ3D-nuScenes~\cite{tian2023occ3d} (0.4\,m, $\pm$40\,m, voxel mIoU; won by FB-OCC~\cite{li2023fbocc}), whereas the CVPR 2024 Autonomous Grand Challenge Occupancy and Flow track adopted OpenOcc v2 (0.4\,m, Occ3D grid with foreground flow) ranked by RayIoU~\cite{liu2024sparseocc} and mAVE.\footnote{\sloppy Challenge repositories: \url{https://github.com/CVPR2023-3D-Occupancy-Prediction/CVPR2023-3D-Occupancy-Prediction} and \url{https://github.com/OpenDriveLab/OccNet}.}

\paragraph{Efficiency, supervision, and world models.} Later architectures reduced OccNet's dense 3D cost (18G, 2.6 FPS in Table~\ref{occnet:tab:efficiency}) via channel-to-height reshaping in FlashOcc~\cite{yu2023flashocc}, coarse-to-fine panoptic queries in PanoOcc~\cite{wang2024panoocc}, sparse grids/tokens/points~\cite{tang2024sparseocc,liu2024sparseocc,wang2024opus}, and 3D Gaussians~\cite{huang2024gaussianformer,huang2025gaussianformer2}. Neural-rendering supervision~\cite{pan2024renderocc,huang2024selfocc} and open-vocabulary distillation~\cite{vobecky2023pop3d,zheng2024veon} relaxed LiDAR and closed-set constraints, while 4D forecasting, generative world models~\cite{ma2024cam4docc,zheng2024occworld,wang2026occsora,li2025uniscene}, and 3D pre-training~\cite{yang2024unipad,yan2025spot,yang2024vidar} extended volumetric representations to temporal prediction.

\section{Scaling Up: The OpenScene Benchmark}\label{occnet:sec:openscene}

To scale beyond nuScenes' 5.5 hours (Table~\ref{occnet:tab:openscene}), OpenScene~\cite{sima2023openscene} curates over 120 hours of 2\,Hz multi-sensor logs (eight cameras + merged LiDAR) and 3D occupancy/flow labels across 1,521 nuPlan~\cite{caesar2021nuplan} logs in Las Vegas (64\%), Singapore (15\%), Pittsburgh (12\%), and Boston (9%) (1,310 trainval, 147 test, 64 mini logs), shrinking raw storage from $>$20\,TB to $\sim$2\,TB while preserving nuPlan's directory layout and 75 scenario types.

\paragraph{Labels, tasks, and downstream use.} Within $[-50\,\text{m},50\,\text{m}]\times[-50\,\text{m},50\,\text{m}]\times[-4\,\text{m},4\,\text{m}]$ at 0.5\,m resolution, 20\,s LiDAR accumulation provides per-voxel semantics (seven foreground classes plus merged background) and 3D velocity vectors alongside ego states, traffic lights, route commands, and tracked 3D boxes. OpenScene defines a large-scale occupancy track (Eq.~\eqref{occnet:eq:miou}, ResNet50 OccNet baseline) and a nine-task OpenScene Metric track. Its redistributed 2\,Hz sensor logs and v1.1 splits underpin NAVSIM~\cite{dauner2024navsim} (Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}) and the End-to-End Driving and Predictive World Model (ViDAR~\cite{yang2024vidar}) tracks at the CVPR 2024 and CVPR 2025 Autonomous Grand Challenges (Fig.~\ref{occnet:fig:openscene_usage}). Public documentation omits a manual label-accuracy audit, leaves a few v1.1 frames without voxel files, and inherits non-commercial nuPlan/CC BY-NC-SA terms.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics and Architecture Details}\label{occnet:app:metrics}\label{occnet:app:implementation}

\paragraph{Completion, detection, and planning metrics.} For $C=16$ classes with voxel counts $\text{TP}_c, \text{FP}_c, \text{FN}_c$ accumulated globally across validation frames within the camera visibility (ray-casting) mask and reported as percentages ($\times 100$), semantic mIoU and binary geometric $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$ are
\begin{equation}
  \text{mIoU} = \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\frac{\text{TP}_c}{\text{TP}_c+\text{FP}_c+\text{FN}_c},
  \label{occnet:eq:miou}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \text{IoU}_{\text{geo}} = \frac{\text{TP}_{\text{occ}}}{\text{TP}_{\text{occ}}+\text{FP}_{\text{occ}}+\text{FN}_{\text{occ}}}.
  \label{occnet:eq:iougeo}
\end{equation}
Detection on nuScenes~\cite{caesar2020nuscenes} combines mAP and five TP errors into NDS; open-loop ST-P3~\cite{hu2022stp3} reports 1\,s, 2\,s, and 3\,s L2 error and box collision rate (\%).

\paragraph{Encoder, cascade, and baselines.} ResNet101~\cite{he2016deep} with FPN~\cite{lin2017feature} fuses $S_3, S_4, S_5$ into three 256-channel levels (1/16, 1/32, 1/64) fed to two BEVFormer~\cite{li2022bevformer} blocks on queries $Q_t$. Stage $i\to i+1$ uses an MLP at $(h,w)$ mapping $Z_i C_i\to Z_{i+1} C_{i+1}$ and a linear layer mapping FPN features to $C_i$, trained with AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} for 24 epochs (lr $2\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, batch size 1 per GPU). Single-scale VoxelNet lifts $4\times H\times W$ queries into $F_{\text{voxel}}$ ($4\times H\times W\times C_1$) via 3D-DA (Eq.~\eqref{occnet:eq:3dda}) before expanding to $16\times H\times W\times C_2$; BEVNet reshapes planar $F_{\text{bev}}$ ($H\times W\times C_1$) into $16\times H\times W\times C_2$.

\section{Chapter Summary}\label{occnet:sec:summary}

To address RQ1, this chapter formulated 3D semantic occupancy as a geometry-open spatial representation for autonomous driving. Rather than fitting cuboids to a fixed list of foreground categories and projecting static infrastructure onto a 2D plane, volumetric occupancy discretises the surrounding 3D environment into semantic voxels with motion vectors, capturing arbitrarily shaped obstacles and overhead structures within a unified grid. Around this representation we developed three contributions: OccNet, which lifts surround-view camera features into 3D volumes through a coarse-to-fine cascade voxel decoder with voxel temporal self-attention and 3D deformable cross-attention; OpenOcc, a dense nuScenes benchmark comprising 34,149 frames and over 1.4 billion labelled voxels across 16 semantic categories with foreground flow vectors; and OpenScene, which expands occupancy and flow annotations to over 120 hours of nuPlan logs and subsequently supplied the sensor corpus for NAVSIM as well as the end-to-end driving and predictive world-modelling tracks at the CVPR 2024 and CVPR 2025 (NAVSIM-v2) Autonomous Grand Challenges.

Our experimental evaluation provides graded evidence across the three requirements of RQ1. First, volumetric occupancy can be inferred from surround-view cameras alone: on OpenOcc, OccNet attains 26.98 mIoU and 41.08 $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$, outperforming our retrained TPVFormer baseline (23.67 mIoU and 37.47 $\text{IoU}_{\text{geo}}$), and when evaluated at ground-truth LiDAR returns it reaches 60.46 mIoU, trailing the LiDAR segmenter RangeNet++ (65.50) by roughly 5 points and the point-supervised camera model TPVFormer-Base (68.9). Second, volumetric grids capture non-cuboid vehicles more accurately than voxelised 3D detections, with the $\text{mIoU}_{\text{ir}}$ margin expanding from 2.21 to 3.51 points as grid resolution tightens from 0.5\,m to 0.25\,m, though part of this gap reflects comparing surface-shell labels against solid cuboids. Third, in single-run open-loop nuScenes planning, rasterised occupancy predictions lower collision percentages by 0.06--0.24 points relative to box predictions from the same network without a consistent improvement in L2 error. Architecturally, with a ResNet50 backbone, the cascade decoder improves upon both planar BEVNet and dense VoxelNet while trimming VoxelNet's parameter count from 72M to 40M. At the same time, clear boundaries apply: supervision still depends on curated LiDAR sweeps and interpolated 3D boxes; semantic predictions remain confined to 16 fixed classes; occupancy flow is annotated but not experimentally evaluated; occupancy pre-training does not aid the trajectory planner; all perception benchmarks rely on our own OpenOcc split; and every table reports single-seed runs without closed-loop simulation or domain-shift testing. Combined with the neutral or negative occupancy results observed on NAVSIM in Chapter~\ref{ch:centaur}, this thesis does not establish that 3D occupancy universally improves downstream planning.

Ultimately, a 3D occupancy grid specifies \emph{what occupies each location}, but not \emph{why a traffic configuration matters} or \emph{how the ego vehicle should respond}. Identical occupied voxels represent a kerbside pedestrian whether waiting or stepping into the roadway, and a stationary lead vehicle whether parked or held by a red signal, even though each scenario demands a distinct driving manoeuvre. Resolving such ambiguities requires world knowledge of traffic conventions together with explicit reasoning that links scene perception to agent intent prediction and ego trajectory planning. Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm} addresses this gap by grounding vision-language models in driving scenes through structured, verifiable graph reasoning spanning perception, prediction, and planning; note that Chapters~\ref{ch:occnet}, \ref{ch:drivelm}, and~\ref{ch:centaur} investigate perception, reasoning, and test-time adaptation on their respective standard task stacks rather than passing intermediate tensors inside one monolithic software binary, and Chapter~\ref{ch:drivelm} adopts a 2D front-camera VLM (BLIP-2) to evaluate language-model priors without confounding the representation with this chapter's 3D voxel decoder.
